In the late 1830s, botanist Matthias **Schleiden** and zoologist Theodor **Schwann** proposed the **unified cell theory**. It has three parts: all living things are made of one or more cells; the cell is the basic unit of life; and new cells come only from existing cells.

**Photosynthesis** is how plants and other autotrophs capture the Sun's energy. Using **sunlight, carbon dioxide and water**, they make energy-storing **carbohydrates** such as glucose and release **oxygen** as a waste product. In plants this happens inside **chloroplasts**, where the green pigment **chlorophyll**, embedded in the thylakoid membranes, absorbs light. Photosynthesis is the only biological process that turns sunlight into chemical energy, so nearly all life, including animals, depends on it.

A common trap: plants also respire. Photosynthesis makes sugar and oxygen; respiration uses them.
Memory aid: "Cells from cells; light + CO₂ + water → sugar + O₂ in the chloroplast."
