# HE-B2 — Food science and nutrition

Annex F gives this line **12 items** (Understand 2, Apply 6, Analyze 2, Evaluate 1, Create 1). It joins three courses [SRC-0002, Annex F, B.2]:
- **Principles of food preparation:** food selection, preparation and cooking techniques; kitchen tools and equipment; safety and sanitation.
- **Food and nutrition:** nutrients and their roles in the body; planning and preparing nutritious meals for an individual's or family's needs, such as age and budget.
- **Fundamentals of food technology:** food safety and preservation; the chemical makeup of food (carbohydrates, proteins, water, fats, vitamins and minerals) and how it affects quality and safety; microorganisms in food safety and preservation.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Nutrients.** The body needs six classes of nutrients: carbohydrates, lipids (fats), proteins, water, vitamins and minerals.
- The first three are **macronutrients**, needed in large amounts and used for energy.
- Vitamins and minerals are **micronutrients**: needed in small amounts but essential.

**Energy.** Carbohydrates and proteins each give about 4 kilocalories per gram; fats give about 9.

**Vitamins** are either fat-soluble (A, D, E, K) or water-soluble (vitamin C and the B vitamins).

**Deficiencies.** Too little of a vitamin or mineral causes a typical deficiency disease, such as scurvy, rickets, beriberi, pellagra, anemia or goiter. Severe lack of protein causes kwashiorkor; lack of both protein and calories causes marasmus.

**Food safety.** It rests on four habits: **clean, separate, cook, chill**. Keep hot food hot and cold food cold. Food can be contaminated by bacteria, viruses, parasites, and toxins from molds.

**Preservation** methods, old and new, keep food safe and protect its flavor, color, moisture and nutrients.

**In the Philippines:**
- The *Nutritional Guidelines for Filipinos* (NGF, revised 2012) give the official messages.
- *Pinggang Pinoy*, the healthy food plate for adults, **completes** the daily nutritional guide pyramid; it did not replace it.
- Laws require iodized salt (ASIN law), food fortification, and a food safety regulatory system.

## 2. Testable facts

**Nutrients and energy**
- **Six classes of nutrients:** carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, water, vitamins and minerals. [SRC-0108, ch. 1 Introduction]
- **Macronutrients** (needed in large amounts) are carbohydrates, lipids and proteins; they can be processed into cellular energy. [SRC-0108, ch. 1 Introduction]
- **Micronutrients** (needed in lesser amounts) are all the essential minerals and vitamins: **16 essential minerals** and **13 vitamins**. [SRC-0108, ch. 1 Introduction]
- Energy: carbohydrates **4 kcal/g**, proteins **4 kcal/g**, lipids **9 kcal/g**. [SRC-0108, ch. 1 Introduction]
- Lipids also form cell membranes, protect organs, and insulate the body for temperature regulation. [SRC-0108, ch. 1 Introduction]
- Providing energy is not protein's most important job. Proteins build the structure of bones, muscles and skin and run most of the body's chemical reactions. [SRC-0108, ch. 1 Introduction]

**Vitamins**
- The 13 vitamins are **water-soluble** (vitamin C and all the B vitamins: thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, pantothenic acid, pyridoxine, biotin, folate, cobalamin) or **fat-soluble** (**A, D, E, K**). [SRC-0108, ch. 1 Introduction]
- Vitamin C is not stored in significant amounts. The higher the intake, the more is excreted. [SRC-0108, ch. 10 Water-Soluble Vitamins]
- **Vitamin A deficiency:** **night blindness** is its most definitive sign. Severe deficiency causes **xerophthalmia** (dry eyes, Bitot's spots), which can progress to corneal ulceration and blindness. [SRC-0108, ch. 9 Fat-Soluble Vitamins]
- **Vitamin D deficiency:** **rickets** in children (soft, weak, deformed bones); **osteomalacia** in adults. [SRC-0108, ch. 9 Fat-Soluble Vitamins]
- **Vitamin C deficiency: scurvy.** Signs include bleeding gums, skin disorders, painful joints, weakness, depression and more infections. Vitamin C is essential for making **collagen**. [SRC-0108, ch. 10 Water-Soluble Vitamins]
- **Thiamin (B1) deficiency: beriberi.** Symptoms include fatigue, confusion, movement problems, leg pain, swelling and heart failure. It is common where the staple is **white rice**, because milling removes the bran. [SRC-0108, ch. 10 Water-Soluble Vitamins]
- **Niacin (B3) deficiency: pellagra.** It starts with fatigue, poor appetite and indigestion, then the **four D's: diarrhea, dermatitis, dementia, and sometimes death**. [SRC-0108, ch. 10 Water-Soluble Vitamins]

**Minerals**
- **Iron:** low iron reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of red blood cells, causing **anemia**. [SRC-0108, ch. 11 Iron]
- **Iodine** is essential for making **thyroid hormone**, which regulates basal metabolism, growth and development. Iodine prevents **goiter**, a gross enlargement of the thyroid gland in the neck. [SRC-0108, ch. 11 Iodine]

**Protein deficiency**
- **Kwashiorkor** is caused by severe dietary **protein** deficiency. Signs: **edema** (swelling of feet and abdomen, a pot-belly), poor skin, growth retardation, low muscle mass, liver malfunction, and light-colored thinning hair. The edema occurs because too little protein means too little albumin to hold water in the blood vessels. [SRC-0108, ch. 6 Diseases Involving Proteins]
- The name kwashiorkor comes from a language in Ghana, meaning "rejected one". It is common in children recently weaned and fed low-protein grain porridge. [SRC-0108, ch. 6 Diseases Involving Proteins]
- **Marasmus**: neither enough **protein nor enough calories**. Signs: extreme **emaciation**, poor skin, growth retardation. It mostly affects children under one in poor countries; the name is Greek for "starvation". [SRC-0108, ch. 6 Diseases Involving Proteins]

**Food safety and microorganisms**
- The **four steps** for safe food handling: **Clean** (wash hands, surfaces, utensils, produce); **Separate** (avoid cross-contamination, e.g. separate cutting boards for produce and for meat, poultry, seafood and eggs); **Cook** (heat to proper temperatures, checked with a food thermometer); **Chill** (refrigerate leftovers within **two hours**). [SRC-0108, ch. 17 Efforts on the Consumer Level]
- Hot food should be kept above **140°F**; cold food at **40°F (4°C) or lower**. The refrigerator should be kept at 40°F (4°C) or colder. [SRC-0108, ch. 17 Efforts on the Consumer Level]
- Store raw meat at the **bottom** of the refrigerator so juices do not drip onto other foods. Keep eggs on a shelf, not in the door, where it is warmest. [SRC-0108, ch. 17 Efforts on the Consumer Level]
- Foodborne illnesses are **infections** (caused by microbes such as bacteria) or **intoxications** (caused by toxins, such as those produced by molds). [SRC-0108, ch. 17 Major Types of Foodborne Illness]
- **Salmonella**, found in the intestines of birds, reptiles and mammals, spreads through meats, poultry, eggs, dairy and seafood. Salmonellosis brings fever, diarrhea and cramps within **12–72 hours**. [SRC-0108, ch. 17 Causes of Food Contamination]
- **Molds** are microscopic fungi that grow in warm, humid or damp conditions on many foods. [SRC-0108, ch. 17 Causes of Food Contamination]
- Proteins give food structure and texture; cooking **denatures** proteins and changes their functional qualities. [SRC-0108, ch. 6 Role of Proteins in Foods]

**Food preservation**
- Traditional methods: **curing, smoking, pickling, salting, fermenting, canning**, and preserving fruit as jam. Modern methods: **drying, vacuum packing, pasteurization, freezing and refrigeration**. Preservation guards against foodborne illness and protects flavor, color, moisture and nutritive value. [SRC-0108, ch. 17 Food Preservation]

**Philippine guidelines and laws**
- **Fortification** means adding nutrients to processed foods at levels **above their natural state**. A **micronutrient** is an essential nutrient needed in very small quantities (milligrams or micrograms). [SRC-0061, Sec. 3(c),(e)]
- The **ASIN law** (RA 8172) makes salt fortification a priority health program. All food outlets, restaurants and stores must make **only iodized salt** available to customers. [SRC-0060, Secs. 2, 5(c)]
- The **Food Safety Act** (RA 10611) aims to protect the public from food-borne and water-borne illness and from unsanitary, unwholesome, misbranded or adulterated food. [SRC-0058, Sec. 3(a)]

**Cooking methods and preparation terms (Stage 7 addition; Wikipedia, tertiary, because no open culinary text was reachable)**
- **Sauté:** cook in a **small amount of fat** in a shallow pan over **relatively high heat**; the name means "jumped" (tossing). [SRC-0239, lead]
- **Blanch:** scald briefly in **boiling water**, then plunge into **iced or cold water** ("shocking") to stop cooking. [SRC-0240, lead]
- **Braise:** a **combination** method: brown at high temperature first, then **simmer covered** in liquid. [SRC-0241, lead]
- **Poach:** heat food **submerged in liquid** at a **lower** temperature than simmering or boiling, about **70–80 °C**. [SRC-0242, lead]
- **Broil:** grilling with the heat source **above** the food (US term). [SRC-0243, lead]
- **Deep-fry:** food **submerged in hot fat**, unlike shallow (pan) frying. [SRC-0244, lead]
- **Steam:** cook with **steam**; the food sits in the steam, not in the water. [SRC-0248, lead]
- **Marinate:** soak food in a **seasoned, often acidic** liquid (the marinade) before cooking. [SRC-0249, lead]
- **Julienne:** long, thin **matchstick** strips. **Dice:** small **cubes**, uniform for even cooking. **Mince:** **small, uniform pieces**, finer than dice. [SRC-0245, lead; SRC-0247, lead; SRC-0246, lead]
- **Temperature danger zone:** the range in which food-borne bacteria grow readily; agencies define it differently, roughly **4 to 63 °C**. Keep hot foods hot and cold foods cold. [SRC-0253, lead]
- **Kneading** works bread dough to mix it and give strength by forming **gluten**. **Creaming** butter and sugar beats **air bubbles** into the fat, giving a light, fluffy mixture. A **sieve** (sifter) separates lumps and controls particle size. [SRC-0250, lead; SRC-0252, Baking; SRC-0251, lead]

- **Roasting** cooks with **dry heat**, hot air surrounding the food (at least about 150 °C). **Whisking or whipping** beats **air** into a mixture such as cream or egg whites. [SRC-0269, lead; SRC-0268, lead]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Pellagra has four D's** in our source (diarrhea, dermatitis, dementia, and death), not just three. If an option lists only three, it can still be the best answer, but know all four.
- **Kwashiorkor vs marasmus.** Swelling or a pot-belly despite eating = kwashiorkor (protein). Extreme thinness, "skin and bones" = marasmus (protein *and* calories).
- **Pinggang Pinoy did not replace the pyramid**. It completes it.
- **Iodine is a mineral, not a vitamin**. Iron (anemia) and iodine (goiter) are minerals.
- **Fat-soluble is "A, D, E, K"**. Vitamin C and the B vitamins are water-soluble.
- **Fat has more than double the energy per gram** (9) of carbohydrate or protein (4 each).
- **Infection vs intoxication.** Live microbes = infection; toxins (e.g. from molds) = intoxication.
- **Poach vs boil vs simmer.** Poaching is the *gentlest* (about 70–80 °C). Braising starts dry (browning) and ends wet.
- **Broil vs grill.** In broiling the heat comes from **above**.
- **Julienne = strips, dice = cubes, mince = finest.**

## 4. Memory aid

**"A Dog Eats Kibble"** = fat-soluble A, D, E, K.
**"4-4-9"** = carbohydrate, protein, fat kcal per gram.
**"Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill — and Chill within 2."**
**"Strips, Cubes, Crumbs"** = julienne, dice, mince. **"Brown then Bathe"** = braise.
