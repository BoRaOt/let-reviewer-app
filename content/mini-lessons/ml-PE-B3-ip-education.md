The **Indigenous Peoples' Rights Act (RA 8371, 1997)** sets out how the State must educate indigenous cultural communities and indigenous peoples (ICCs/IPs).

Section 28 says the State, through the **National Commission on Indigenous Peoples (NCIP)**, shall provide a complete, adequate and **integrated system of education relevant to the needs** of IP children and youth. Section 29 obliges the State to respect and protect IPs' right to **preserve their culture, traditions and institutions**, and to consider these in national plans.

Section 30 gives IPs **equal access** to educational and cultural opportunities **without losing their right** to set up and control their own education systems, **in their own language**, and in ways **appropriate to their cultural methods of teaching and learning**. IP children have the right to all levels and forms of State education.

For a teacher, this means using IP learners' language and community knowledge as resources, not obstacles.
