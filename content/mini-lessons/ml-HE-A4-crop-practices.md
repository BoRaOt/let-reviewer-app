Healthy crops start with good habits. **Crop rotation** means planting different crops in a planned sequence on the same land across seasons. It keeps the soil from being drained of one set of nutrients, interrupts the life cycles of pests and weeds that specialize on one crop, and lowers the chance of resistant pests. Growing the same crop year after year, called **monocropping**, does the opposite and makes the farm depend on more fertilizer and pesticide.

Rotations are planned for a goal: controlling weeds, **adding nitrogen** to the soil (often with legumes), reducing erosion, or improving soil structure.

Other preventive or **cultural** practices include choosing varieties suited to local conditions and resistant to pests, keeping plants healthy, and **intercropping**, which mixes crops in one field. These are the first line of defense in integrated pest management.
Memory aid: "Rotate to starve the pests and feed the soil."
