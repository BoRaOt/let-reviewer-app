**Reliability** is consistency: would learners get similar scores if they took the assessment again, or if a different teacher scored it? Teachers can raise reliability in three ways. Use **more items or tasks**, since one confusing item changes a 5-item score by 20 percent but a 50-item score by only 2 percent. Write **clear directions and tasks**. Use **clear scoring criteria**, such as rubrics.

**Bias** occurs when parts of an assessment distort performance because of learners' gender, ethnicity or social class. It comes in two forms. **Offensiveness** uses negative stereotypes, such as showing all doctors as men and all nurses as women. **Unfair penalization** depends on background experiences some groups lack, such as a math problem built around a sport only some learners know.

Low scores alone do not prove bias. And a test can be reliable without being valid: it can consistently measure the wrong thing.
Memory aid: "More items, clear tasks, clear criteria — no stereotypes, no unfair background."
