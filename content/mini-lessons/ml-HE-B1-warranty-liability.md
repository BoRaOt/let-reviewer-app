subtopic: Warranties and liability for defective products

When a seller or manufacturer gives an express warranty, the Consumer Act requires its terms to be written in clear, understandable language. The warranty must:
- name the warrantor and the person covered;
- state which products or parts it covers;
- state what the warrantor will do if there is a defect, and at whose expense;
- state what the consumer must do to claim it;
- state the period within which the warrantor will act.

Written warranties take effect from the moment of sale, not later. Separately, manufacturers, producers and importers are liable, regardless of fault, for damages caused by defects in design, manufacture, assembly, formulas, handling or packing, and for poor information about use and hazards. A product is defective when it does not give the safety a person can rightfully expect.
Memory aid: "Warranty starts at the sale; liability needs no fault."
