Two carving traditions are often tested.

**Okir**, also spelled okil, is the set of flowing plant-based and geometric motifs of the **Maranao** and other Moro and Lumad peoples of Mindanao. Its roots are pre-Islamic, in the carving of the Sama people. The Maranao used okir to decorate the **torogan**, the house of the ruling datu, especially its carved **panolong** beams, which became symbols of power and status. A favorite motif is the **naga**, an S-shaped sea serpent. Living things are stylized rather than drawn realistically.

**Paete, Laguna** is called the **Carving Capital of the Philippines**. Its craftsmen are known for **woodcarving**, or ukit, including religious images. Paete also gave the country **taka**, papier-mâché made by layering paper and paste over a carved wooden mold, then finishing the piece as an artwork.
Memory aid: "Okir = Maranao curves on the torogan; Paete = carving and taka."
