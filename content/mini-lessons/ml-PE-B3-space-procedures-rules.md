Most good classroom management is prevention. Seifert and Sutton describe four tools.

**Arrange space** so learners can focus and distractions are few. Use wall displays to reinforce lessons and show student work, but do not crowd the walls, which makes a room feel busy and small.

Set **procedures**, or routines, for common repeated tasks, such as passing papers or going to the restroom. They are like social conventions, so teachers may simply announce and explain them.

Set a few **rules**, standards of behavior that learners must take personal responsibility for, such as "respect other people's property". Keep them **few and general**. Because rules concern responsibility, there is a stronger case for involving learners in making rules than procedures.

Plan **transitions** between activities. They waste time and invite misbehavior unless materials are ready and directions are clear.
