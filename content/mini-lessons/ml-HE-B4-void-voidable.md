subtopic: Family Code — void and voidable marriages

A **void marriage** is treated as never valid. The Family Code lists several kinds:
- a party below eighteen, even with parental consent;
- solemnized by someone without authority, unless the couple believed in good faith that the officer had authority;
- no license, unless the case is exempt;
- bigamous or polygamous;
- made through mistake about the other party's identity.

Article 36 adds that a marriage is void if a party was psychologically incapacitated to fulfil the essential marital obligations, even if the incapacity shows only later.

A **voidable marriage** is valid until annulled. One classic cause is a party aged eighteen to below twenty-one who married without parental consent, unless that party freely lived with the spouse after turning twenty-one. Exam items often hinge on the age: under eighteen is void, eighteen to twenty without consent is voidable.
Memory aid: "Under 18 = never; 18 to 20 without consent = fixable or annullable."
Sources: SRC-0071 Arts. 35, 36, 45
