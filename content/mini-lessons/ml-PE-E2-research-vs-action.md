The TOS asks teachers to **recommend solutions based on research**. That means two skills: reading others' research critically and using your own findings.

When reading research, Seifert and Sutton advise asking what the study's **purpose** is and how useful it is for your own work and goals. Research by professors or administrators may frame questions differently from a classroom teacher, so translate findings to your setting.

When writing up your own action research, a common structure is **IMRaD**: Introduction (the problem and question), Methods (who, what data, how), Results (what you found) and Discussion (what it means, and what to do). Recommendations belong in the Discussion and should follow from the results, not from wishes. For example, if checklists raised handwashing compliance from 50 to 90 percent in three weeks, recommend checklists for all kitchen labs and a follow-up cycle to see if the gain lasts.
Memory aid: "Recommend only what your Results can carry."
