# HE-A1 — Industrial Arts I and II (exploratory)

Annex F gives this course **10 items** (Remember 1, Understand 1, Apply 6, Analyze 1, Evaluate 1). Outcomes: livelihood and vocational fields; trades (**carpentry, automotive, basic electronics, electrical, civil technology, metal works, refrigeration and air-conditioning (RAC), graphic arts, plumbing**); IA career paths; and teaching strategies for exploratory IA (**constructivist, problem-based, project-based**; classroom technology). [SRC-0002, Annex F, A.1]

With six Apply items, expect short computations (Ohm's law, power, cost of electricity, series/parallel) and "what happens next in the cycle" items.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Electricity basics.** Voltage pushes current through resistance: **V = IR** (Ohm's law). Power is **P = IV**. Energy used is **E = Pt**, billed in **kilowatt-hours**.

**Series vs parallel.** In **series**, the same current flows through each part and resistances add. In **parallel**, each branch gets the full voltage, and the total resistance is **less than the smallest branch**. House wiring and car lamps are parallel so each device works on its own.

**Electronics.** Resistor values are read from **color bands** (black 0 to white 9); gold = ±5%, silver = ±10% tolerance.

**Automotive.** A four-stroke engine runs **intake → compression → power → exhaust**.

**RAC.** A vapor-compression system has four parts: **compressor, condenser, expansion valve, evaporator**. It moves heat out of the cooled space.

**Plumbing.** A leak test closes all openings except the highest one before testing.

**Workshop order: 5S** — sort, set in order, shine, standardize, sustain.

**Careers.** TESDA's training regulations (e.g. Electrical Installation and Maintenance, Electronic Products Assembly and Servicing, Plumbing, Automotive Servicing) define the National Certificate (NC) qualifications that IA learners can pursue.

**Teaching IA.** Constructivism says learners build knowledge from experience. Inquiry, cooperative and **project-based learning** let students investigate a real question over time and produce a product.

## 2. Testable facts

**Electricity (OpenStax College Physics 2e)**
- **Ohm's law: V = IR** (voltage = current × resistance). [SRC-0094, 20.2]
- **Electric power: P = IV**; combining with Ohm's law, **P = V²/R = I²R**. [SRC-0094, 20.4]
- Energy used **E = Pt**; electric bills use the **kilowatt-hour (kWh)**; cost = kWh × price per kWh. [SRC-0094, 20.4]
- **Series:** total resistance **Rs = R1 + R2 + R3 + …**; the current passes through each resistor in sequence. [SRC-0094, 21.1]
- **Parallel:** each resistor is connected directly to the source and gets the **full voltage**; total resistance is **less than the smallest** individual resistance, and more current flows from the source. Car headlights and radio are wired in parallel. [SRC-0094, 21.1]

**Electronics**
- Resistor color code (IEC 60062): **black 0, brown 1, red 2, orange 3, yellow 4, green 5, blue 6, violet 7, gray 8, white 9**; the multiplier band gives the number of zeros; **gold ±5%, silver ±10%, no band ±20%**. Example: red-violet-green-gold = 2,700,000 Ω ±5%. [SRC-0167, lead and color table]
- TESDA EPAS NC II testing of components starts by obtaining and interpreting **data sheets / application notes** per manufacturer's specifications and defining testing criteria. [SRC-0083, "Test electronic components", element 1]

**Automotive and RAC**
- Four-stroke cycle: **intake** (air-fuel mixture drawn in), **compression**, **combustion/power** (ignition drives the piston), **exhaust** (spent gases expelled); the four strokes take two crankshaft revolutions (720°). [SRC-0164, lead]
- The cycle is named after **Nicolaus Otto** (Otto cycle). [SRC-0164, "History: Otto cycle"]
- Vapor-compression refrigeration is the most widely used method for air conditioning; its **four components: compressor, condenser, thermal expansion (throttle) valve, evaporator**; refrigeration removes heat from an enclosed space and rejects it at a higher temperature. [SRC-0165, lead and "Description"]

**Plumbing and electrical TRs**
- Pipe leak testing: all openings in the pipe system are **tightly closed except the highest opening**; testing is done after completing the installation. [SRC-0084, "Conduct pipe leak testing", elements 1–2]
- Plumbing knowledge includes mensuration, **blueprint reading**, materials specification, **5S**, and the **3Rs (reduce, reuse, recycle)**. [SRC-0084, "Conduct pipe leak testing", required knowledge]
- EIM NC III includes the units "Perform roughing-in and wiring activities" and "Maintain tools and equipment"; its technical-drawing unit covers **blueprint reading and orthographic drawing**. [SRC-0082, list of units; technical drawing unit]

**Workshop organization**
- **5S**: **sort (seiri)** remove unneeded items; **set in order (seiton)** put needed items in their best place; **shine (seiso)** clean and inspect regularly; **standardize (seiketsu)**; **sustain (shitsuke)** self-discipline. [SRC-0166, "Sort" to "Sustain"]

**Career paths**
- Under the TESDA Act (RA 7796), **national occupational skills standards** are set by TESDA-accredited **industry committees**, and TESDA runs a **certification and accreditation** program in which accredited industry groups conduct trade tests. [SRC-0043, Sec. 22]

**Teaching strategies for exploratory IA (Seifert & Sutton)**
- **Constructivism:** learning as students actively construct knowledge from experience; **psychological** (Piaget, Dewey: the independent investigator) vs **social** (Vygotsky: assisted performance). [SRC-0109, Ch. 2 "Constructivism"]
- **Inquiry learning** (also discovery learning, Bruner; progressive education, Dewey): the teacher poses questions and **avoids answering directly**, guiding students to investigate. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Inquiry learning"]
- **Cooperative learning:** students work in groups and are rewarded partly or wholly for group success; jigsaw and STAD are common formats. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Cooperative learning" and Table 26]
- **Project-based learning (Katz):** a teacher or students pose a question of interest; students work **together for extended periods** to investigate it, ending in a **presentation, report or other product**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 Table 26]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Series ≠ parallel.** In series the *current* is the same; in parallel the *voltage* is the same. Parallel total resistance is smaller than any branch, not the average.
- **Watts vs kilowatt-hours.** Watts are power; kWh is energy (what you pay for). 1,000 W for 1 hour = 1 kWh.
- **Color code order.** Brown is 1, red 2; gold is ±5% tolerance, not a digit.
- **Compression comes before power.** Exhaust is last.
- **Condenser vs evaporator.** The evaporator absorbs heat inside the cooled space; the condenser releases it outside.
- **Project-based vs problem-based.** Both start from a problem or question; project-based learning is defined by an extended investigation ending in a product.
