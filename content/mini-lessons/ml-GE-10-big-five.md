Trait theorists describe personality by stable characteristics.

**Gordon Allport** found about **4,500** English words that describe people. **Raymond Cattell** reduced personality to **16 factors**, such as warmth, dominance and perfectionism. **Hans and Sybil Eysenck** proposed just two dimensions, **extroversion versus introversion** and **neuroticism versus stability**, rooted in inborn temperament.

The **Five Factor Model**, or **Big Five**, sits in the middle and is the most widely used today:
- **Openness to experience:** curious and imaginative vs conventional.
- **Conscientiousness:** organized and dependable vs careless.
- **Extroversion:** outgoing and energetic vs reserved.
- **Agreeableness:** cooperative and kind vs critical.
- **Neuroticism:** emotionally unstable and anxious vs calm.

Each trait is a continuum, not a box. Culture matters too: people in more collectivist cultures tend to score lower in extroversion.
Memory aid: "OCEAN — Openness, Conscientiousness, Extroversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism."
