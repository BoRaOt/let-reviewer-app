# HE-C1 — Theories and principles of assessment in HE

Annex F gives this line **13 items** (Remember 1, Understand 2, Apply 7, Analyze 2, Evaluate 1). Outcomes: **competency-based assessment tools**; **authentic assessment in TLE**; **using assessment data to improve instruction**. [SRC-0002, Annex F, C.1] ProfEd D (assessment of learning) covers test construction and statistics; this lesson covers the TVET and performance side.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Competency-based assessment (CBA)** asks one question: can the learner perform the task to the industry standard? The answer is **competent** or **not yet competent**, judged from evidence against a unit of competency. TESDA's assessor follows six steps: organize, prepare the candidate, gather evidence, decide, record, give feedback.

**Assessment for learning vs of learning.** Formative assessment happens during instruction to adjust teaching; summative happens after, to certify and grade.

**Performance assessment** has learners do a complex task (cook a dish, sew a garment, repair a machine) while the teacher judges both the **process** and the **product**. **Authentic** assessment is a task close to real-world work. **Alternative** assessment simply means not pencil-and-paper.

**Rubrics** make judgments consistent: **holistic** (one overall score) or **analytic** (separate scores per criterion).

**Portfolios** are purposeful collections that tell the story of achievement or growth.

**Data use.** Results show which competencies are weak, so the teacher re-teaches, gives more practice, and offers reassessment.

## 2. Testable facts

**Conducting competency assessment (TESDA Trainers Methodology Level I, "Conduct competency assessment")**
- Elements: (1) **organize** assessment activities; (2) **prepare the candidate**; (3) **gather evidence**; (4) **make the assessment decision**; (5) **record** results; (6) **provide feedback**. [SRC-0081, conduct competency assessment unit]
- Preparing the candidate: explain the **context and purpose**, determine needs for **allowable adjustments**, explain legal and ethical responsibilities, the **competency standards** and evidence to be collected, and the procedure. [SRC-0081, conduct competency assessment unit, element 2]
- Evidence is gathered using the methods in the **Evidence Guide**, with **reasonable adjustments** where appropriate, and documented with the relevant tools. [SRC-0081, conduct competency assessment unit, element 3]
- The decision evaluates evidence against the **rules of evidence** and the **dimensions of competency**, and the requirements of the unit(s) of competency. [SRC-0081, conduct competency assessment unit, element 4]
- Records and **confidentiality** of outcomes are kept per policy; issuing of certificates is organized. [SRC-0081, conduct competency assessment unit, element 5]
- Feedback is clear and constructive; **gaps are explored** with the candidate; the candidate is advised of **reassessment**; disputed decisions are recorded and reported. [SRC-0081, conduct competency assessment unit, element 6]
- Purposes of assessment listed: **certify competency**, recruit and select, monitor performance, determine **training needs**, skills audit, **recognize prior learning (RPL)**, classify against industry standards, and **give feedback on progress** during training. [SRC-0081, conduct competency assessment unit, range of variables 1]
- Evidence may be **direct** or **indirect**. [SRC-0081, conduct competency assessment unit, range of variables 2]
- When planning a session, trainers select assessment methods that fit the module's learning outcome and check instruments for **validity, fairness, safety and cost-effectiveness**. [SRC-0081, plan training session unit, elements 4.3–4.5]
- Methods of assessment used across TM units include **demonstration/observation with oral questioning**, written tests, interviews, **third-party reports** and **portfolios**. [SRC-0081, evidence guides, "methods of assessment"]

**Basic concepts (Seifert & Sutton Ch. 11)**
- **Assessment** gathers information about learning and makes value judgments; **measurement** answers "how much?" (assigning numbers); **evaluation** makes judgments about the information. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Basic concepts"]
- **Assessment for learning** is usually **formative** (during instruction, to revise teaching and improve learning); **assessment of learning** is usually **summative** (after instruction, to certify and grade). [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Basic concepts"]
- Formative assessment can be **informal** (spontaneous observation during Q&A or seatwork) or **formal** (planned, systematic data gathering). [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Basic concepts"]

**Performance and authentic assessment**
- In **performance assessment**, students complete a complex task while the teacher observes the **process or procedure** as well as the **product**; examples include repairing a machine, conducting an experiment, artistic creation. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Performance assessments"]
- **Alternative assessment** = tasks that are not pencil-and-paper. **Authentic assessment** = tasks similar to those in the **real world**; authenticity is a matter of degree. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Performance assessments"]
- Advantages: assess **complex outcomes**, assess **process and product**, communicate goals clearly, and usually have **good content validity**. Disadvantages: **time-consuming** (so fewer tasks) and **hard to score reliably**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Advantages and disadvantages"]

**Rubrics**
- **Holistic rubric:** general descriptions and **one overall score**; quicker to develop. **Analytic rubric:** levels described for **each characteristic** separately (e.g. six writing traits), giving more specific feedback. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Scoring rubrics"]

**Portfolios**
- A portfolio is "a meaningful collection of student work that tells the story of student achievement or growth" — **purposeful**, not a folder of everything. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Portfolios"]
- Four dimensions of purpose: assessment **for** vs **of** learning; **current accomplishments** vs **progress**; **best-work showcase** vs **documentation**; **finished** vs **working**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Portfolios"]
- Showcase portfolios hold **best work**, usually chosen by the student; documentation portfolios include **all** work samples; working portfolios hold day-to-day work and are **not** meant for assessment of learning. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Portfolios"]

**Qualifications and certification**
- The **Philippine Qualifications Framework** (RA 10968) describes levels of qualifications and sets standards for outcomes; its level descriptors are defined by **knowledge, skills and values; application; degree of independence**. [SRC-0042, Secs. 4, 8]
- The PQF harmonizes qualification levels across **basic, technical-vocational and higher education** and supports **recognition of prior learning**. [SRC-0042, Secs. 5, 8]
- TESDA-accredited **industry committees** set national occupational skills standards, and accredited bodies conduct trade tests for certification. [SRC-0043, Sec. 22]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Competency results are "competent / not yet competent"**, not ranked scores.
- **Authentic ≠ alternative.** An essay is alternative to MCQ in some sense but not necessarily authentic; cooking for real customers is highly authentic.
- **Performance assessment judges process too.** Watching the knife technique matters, not only tasting the dish.
- **Holistic = one score; analytic = several.** Use analytic when you need diagnostic feedback.
- **Working portfolio is not for grading.**
- **RPL** is a purpose of assessment: certifying skills already gained outside formal training.
- **Formative is not "ungraded quiz" only;** its defining feature is that results are used to adjust teaching during instruction.

## 4. Memory aid

**TESDA assessor "O-P-G-D-R-F"**: Organize, Prepare, Gather, Decide, Record, Feedback — "Only Prepared Grads Do Real Fieldwork."
**Portfolio pairs "For/Of, Now/Growth, Best/All, Done/Working."**
