In the 1880s, Filipino students and exiles in Spain formed the **Propaganda Movement**. They did not seek independence at first; they asked for **political reforms** for the colony under Spain.

Their newspaper was **La Solidaridad**, published in Barcelona and later Madrid. Rizal contributed essays, allegories, poems and editorials, often under **pen names**: **"Dimasalang"**, **"Laong Laan"** and **"May Pagasa"**. He later had a rivalry with Marcelo H. del Pilar over the paper's leadership.

In London, Rizal studied **Antonio de Morga's** early account of the Philippines at the British Museum, to show that Filipinos had a civilization before Spain.

By the early 1890s, amid the Calamba land dispute, Rizal grew more separatist, leading to his break with some colleagues and his return home.
