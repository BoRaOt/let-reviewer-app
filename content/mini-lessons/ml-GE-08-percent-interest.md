**Percent** comes from the Latin *per centum*, "by the hundred". So 15% = 15/100 = 0.15. To find a percent of a number, multiply by the decimal: 15% of ₱800 is 0.15 × 800 = ₱120.

**Discount:** sale price = original × (1 − rate). A ₱1,200 bag at 25% off costs 1,200 × 0.75 = ₱900. **Markup** and **sales tax** work the other way: price × (1 + rate).

Successive percents multiply, so +20% then −20% gives 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96, a 4% net loss.

**Simple interest:** I = P × r × t. ₱10,000 at 5% a year for 3 years earns 10,000 × 0.05 × 3 = ₱1,500.

**Compound interest:** A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt), where n is the number of compounding periods per year. Compounding earns interest on interest, so it grows faster than simple interest.
Memory aid: "Percent = per hundred; I PRay for Time; compound grows on itself."
