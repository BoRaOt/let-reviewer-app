**Validity** asks whether the interpretations and uses of assessment results are justified. Strictly, validity belongs to the meaning drawn from scores, and it is a matter of degree: high, moderate or low.

There are three main sources of validity evidence.
- **Content validity:** Does the assessment cover the content and tasks it should? A midterm on chapters 1 to 7 should sample all seven chapters, not only the last four, and only content from that course.
- **Construct validity:** Does it measure the intended ability and not something else? A math-reasoning test written in difficult English may measure English proficiency for recent immigrants, not math.
- **Criterion-related or predictive validity:** Do scores predict later performance? Admission tests are judged by how well they predict first-year grades.

A practical rule: align the assessment with the goal. If the goal is to cook a dish, have learners cook it.
Memory aid: "Content covers, Construct targets, Criterion predicts."
