A **prime number** has exactly two factors, 1 and itself, such as 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11. A **composite number** has more factors. Online encryption depends on large primes.

**Divisibility shortcuts** save time. A number is divisible by 3 when the **sum of its digits** is divisible by 3: 298 gives 2 + 9 + 8 = 19, so 298 is not divisible by 3.

The **greatest common divisor (GCF)** is the largest number that divides two numbers evenly. Use prime factorization: 24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3², so the GCF takes the smaller powers, 2² × 3 = 12. Use GCF to split things into the largest equal groups, such as packing 24 cupcakes and 36 cookies into identical bags.

The **least common multiple (LCM)** is the smallest number both divide into; take the larger powers, 2³ × 3² = 72. Use LCM for "when will they meet again" problems.
Memory aid: "GCF = smaller powers, split into groups; LCM = larger powers, meet again."
