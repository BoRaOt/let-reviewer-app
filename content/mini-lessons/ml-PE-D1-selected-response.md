**Selected response** items ask learners to recognize an answer the teacher provides. They are called **objective** because scoring does not depend on the scorer's judgment, which raises reliability. But using only objective tests can lower validity, because they do not fit every learning goal.

**True-false** items suit facts such as vocabulary, formulas, dates, names and technical terms. They are quick to answer and easy to write, but learners have a **50 percent chance** of guessing correctly, so scores are hard to interpret.

**Matching** items use two parallel columns, such as terms and definitions. Put **more choices in the answer column** than in the premise column, so one mistake does not force another. Matching works for lower-level knowledge, but the lists must be **homogeneous**, all about one kind of thing, which is harder than it looks.

Common flaws in both: unclear wording, copied textbook sentences, negatives, and trivial content.
Memory aid: "True-false: fast but 50–50; Matching: extra answers, same kind."
