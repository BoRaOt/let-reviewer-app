For conflicts that need more than a look, Seifert and Sutton describe four steps based on Thomas Gordon's **Teacher Effectiveness Training**.
1. **Decide problem ownership.** The owner is the person primarily troubled by the behavior: the learner, the teacher or a classmate. The owner takes main responsibility for solving it.
2. **Listen actively.** Attend to everything the learner says and keep checking your understanding with questions.
3. **Be assertive and use I-messages.** Speak firmly, neither apologetic nor hostile, as in Canter's assertive discipline. An **I-message** describes how the behavior affects you: "Your talking makes it hard for me to remember what I'm saying." A you-message blames: "You're so rude."
4. **Negotiate** when a problem persists: define it together, brainstorm solutions with the learner, pick one, try it and review.

Dictating a solution to a long-running conflict often leaves everyone feeling defeated.
Memory aid: "O-L-I-N: Ownership, Listen, I-message, Negotiate."
