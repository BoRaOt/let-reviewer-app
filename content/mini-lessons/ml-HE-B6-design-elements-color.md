Clothing design uses the same building blocks as art. The **elements of design** are line, shape, form, color, space, texture and value. In a garment, lines come from seams, pleats and stripes; shape is the silhouette; texture is how the fabric looks and feels; value is how light or dark it is.

**Color** has three properties: **hue** (the color name), **intensity** or chroma (how bright or dull), and **value** (how light or dark).

Common color schemes help in choosing outfits. **Monochromatic** uses tints and shades of one hue, calm and unified. **Analogous** uses neighbors on the color wheel, such as yellow, yellow-green and green. **Complementary** pairs opposites, such as blue and orange, for strong contrast. **Split-complementary** uses a color plus the two beside its opposite. **Triadic** uses three evenly spaced hues, such as red, yellow and blue.
Memory aid: "One hue = mono; neighbors = analogous; opposites = complementary; three apart = triad."
