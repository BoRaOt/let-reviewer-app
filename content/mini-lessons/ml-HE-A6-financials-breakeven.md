A small business reads three main financial statements: the balance sheet, the income statement and the statement of cash flows. The statement of cash flows links the other two. Its first section, operating activities, shows day-to-day cash, such as buying supplies, paying rent and collecting from customers. To plan prices, find the contribution margin: the selling price of one item minus its variable cost. Then the break-even point in units is the total fixed costs divided by the contribution margin. Suppose a pizza sells for twelve pesos and its ingredients cost four pesos. The margin is eight pesos. If rent, a fixed cost, is two thousand pesos a month, the owner must sell two thousand divided by eight, or two hundred fifty pizzas, just to break even. Every sale beyond that point earns profit.
Memory aid: "Break-even = Fixed divided by (Price minus Variable)."
