Field study and internship rest on **experiential learning**: learning from direct experience that is then reflected on and used. **David Kolb**, influenced by Dewey, Piaget and Lewin, described it as a four-stage cycle.
1. **Concrete experience:** you do something, such as observing a Grade 8 cookery class.
2. **Reflective observation:** you look back on it. What happened? What did learners do?
3. **Abstract conceptualization:** you make sense of it by linking it to ideas or theory, for example noticing that the teacher's demonstration fits Bandura's modeling.
4. **Active experimentation:** you try out what you learned, such as planning your own demonstration, which becomes a new concrete experience.

Kolb's framework stresses that experience alone is not enough. The learner must be willing to take part, able to reflect, able to conceptualize, and able to make decisions and solve problems. It also needs an intention to learn.
Memory aid: "Every Rider Can Experiment" = Experience, Reflect, Conceptualize, Experiment.
