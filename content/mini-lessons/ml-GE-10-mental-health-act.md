Understanding the self includes caring for it. The **Mental Health Act (RA 11036, 2018)** defines **mental health** as a state of well-being in which a person realizes their own abilities and potential, **copes with the normal stresses of life**, shows **resilience** in the face of extreme events, **works productively**, and is able to contribute to the community.

The definition is positive. Mental health is not only the absence of illness; it includes coping, resilience and contribution.

This links to the humanistic view in psychology. Rogers saw well-being in **congruence** between the real and ideal self, and Maslow placed **self-actualization**, reaching one's potential, at the top of human needs.

For teachers, self-understanding means noticing one's own stress and limits, seeking help when needed, and building classrooms where learners can grow and cope, since learners' well-being affects their learning.
Memory aid: "Mental health = Realize, Cope, Bounce back, Work, Contribute."
