Pregnancy has three developmental periods.
- **Germinal:** the first and shortest, lasting about two weeks after conception. The zygote begins dividing within a day, becomes a blastocyst about five to seven days later, and then implants in the uterus.
- **Embryonic:** from the third to the eighth week. The support structures form (amnion, chorion, placenta and umbilical cord), and so do the organs. This organ formation is called organogenesis.
- **Fetal:** the last seven months, when the organs grow and are refined and the fetus gains size quickly.

Teratogens are substances or conditions that can harm development before birth. How much harm they do depends on which teratogen it is, how long and how much the exposure is, genetic susceptibility, and the prenatal environment. Different body parts have different sensitive periods.
Memory aid: "2-8-7" — germinal about 2 weeks, embryonic to week 8, fetal about 7 months.
