There are two ways to write objectives. The **cognitive approach** (Gronlund) starts **general**: state a broad goal, such as "understand photosynthesis", then list a few representative **indicators** of success. The list cannot be complete, only representative.

The **behavioral approach** (Mager) starts **specific**. A good behavioral objective has three parts.
1. An **observable behavior**: what the learner will do, such as "list", "measure" or "sew", not "know" or "appreciate".
2. The **conditions**: the circumstances or materials given, such as "given a recipe for four servings".
3. The **criterion**: the minimum acceptable level, such as "with 100 percent accuracy within 15 minutes".

Example: "Given a recipe for four, the learner will scale every ingredient for twelve servings with no errors." Objectives always describe what **students** do, not what the teacher does.
Memory aid: "Behavior, Condition, Degree — can I see it, under what, how well?"
