Art serves many purposes, and they often overlap. Scholars sort them into two groups.

**Non-motivated functions** are part of simply being human and serve no specific outside goal. They include our instinct for **harmony, balance and rhythm**, which we feel as beauty; the **experience of the mysterious**; the **expression of imagination** in ways words cannot carry; and **ritual and symbolic** uses, such as decoration in ceremonies and dances.

**Motivated functions** are intentional. Art can **communicate**, as in scientific illustration; **entertain**, as in films and games; push for **political change**, as the Dadaists and Surrealists did; **question society**; raise awareness for **social causes**; help **heal**, as in art therapy; and **persuade or sell**, as in propaganda and advertising.

When interpreting a work, ask what it was made to do. A Santo Niño statue in a procession serves ritual; a protest mural serves political change.
Memory aid: "Non-motivated = because we're human; Motivated = because we want something."
