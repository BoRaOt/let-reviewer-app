**Computer security** protects systems and information from unauthorized use. **Computer ethics** are the guidelines for using computers in morally acceptable ways. **Cybercrime** is any crime that involves a computer and a network.

The most common threat for ordinary users is **phishing**: a fake email or website that tricks you into typing your password or personal details. It usually works through **spoofing**, a message disguised to look as if it came from a trusted sender such as a bank or school.

Protect yourself with security software, a **firewall**, **encryption**, and by not logging in to accounts on open, password-free Wi-Fi. Use strong passwords: at least eight characters, mixed upper and lower case, numbers and symbols, no real names or plain words, and never reuse them. **Biometrics** such as fingerprints are harder to steal.

Remember that **cookies** quietly record your browsing for advertisers.
Memory aid: "Spoof is the mask, phish is the hook."
