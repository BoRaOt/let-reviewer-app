Action research can create a **conflict of interest**. A teacher's first priority is the **welfare of students**: their learning, motivation and acceptance. A researcher's priority is the topic. Usually these fit together, but not always.

Ethical problems arise when results are made **public**, in a journal, a conference or even informal talk outside the class. Three issues matter.
- **Privacy:** use a **pseudonym** and change identifying details, in any report or hallway conversation, without hiding facts essential to the study.
- **Informed consent:** if data will be shared outside the school community, get permission from parents or guardians and the students, using a form that describes the study and data, states participation is voluntary, and promises confidentiality or anonymity.
- **Freedom to participate:** learners may decline to be in the research, even though class work itself is required.

The Data Privacy Act also treats education and health data as sensitive.
