Seifert and Sutton describe the action research cycle from Mertler in four stages.

**1. Planning.** First identify and define the problem. It often starts as vague unease and becomes a researchable question, such as "Why do half my learners skip the handwashing step?" Next, **review related literature**, alone, in a study group or in a course. Then write a **research plan**: what data to collect, how and when.

**2. Acting.** Collect and analyze the data. Keep it **manageable**; complex statistics are not required.

**3. Developing an action plan.** Decide on changes, carry them out, and **document** what you did so others can understand it.

**4. Communicating and reflecting.** Share results with colleagues, at a conference or in a teachers' journal, and include students and parents if they took part. Ask: What did I learn? What should I do next? That begins a new cycle.
Memory aid: "Plan, Act, Action plan, Share and Reflect — then spiral again."
