Food preservation protects food from spoilage and foodborne illness. It also protects flavor, color, moisture and nutritive value.
- **Traditional methods, practiced for generations:** curing, smoking, pickling, salting, fermenting, canning, and turning fruit into jam.
- **Modern methods:** drying with equipment, vacuum packing, pasteurization, freezing and refrigeration.

Food irradiation is another modern technique, and our source stresses that it does not make food radioactive. On the exam, expect two kinds of items. Some ask you to sort a method into traditional or modern. Others give a scenario, such as keeping a family's harvest safe for months or protecting a dish's color and nutrients, and ask which preservation goal or method fits best. Remember the purpose behind every method: preservation guards against foodborne illness while protecting the food's quality.
Memory aid: "Old ways cure, smoke, pickle, salt, ferment, can; new ways dry, vacuum, pasteurize, freeze."
