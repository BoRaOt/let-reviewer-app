Ivan Pavlov discovered classical conditioning while studying digestion in dogs. Meat powder is an unconditioned stimulus because it naturally makes dogs salivate, and that salivation is an unconditioned response. A tone at first means nothing to the dog, so it is a neutral stimulus. Pavlov sounded the tone just before giving the meat powder, again and again. Eventually the tone alone made the dogs salivate. The tone had become a conditioned stimulus, and the salivation to it a conditioned response. Classroom versions are common. A learner who was embarrassed during recitation may feel anxious whenever their name is called. Classical conditioning pairs two stimuli and works on reflexes and feelings. Operant conditioning, by contrast, works through the consequences of a chosen behavior.
Memory aid: "Pavlov Pairs" — pairing a signal with something that already triggers a reflex.
