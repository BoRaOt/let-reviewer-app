Reading plans is a common competency because every trade builds from drawings. TESDA's unit on preparing and interpreting technical drawings has three stages.
1. **Identify:** choose the correct technical drawing for the job, and sort drawings by type and kind.
2. **Interpret:** recognize the components or objects shown, identify the dimensions of key features, read the symbols correctly, and check the drawing against the job requirements and standard procedures.
3. **Prepare:** draw simple schematics following drawing conventions and dimensioning.

The knowledge behind this includes trade mathematics, such as linear measurement, dimensions and unit conversion. It also includes blueprint reading and plan specification for architectural, electrical, electronic and mechanical plans, with their symbols and abbreviations, and orthographic drawing. In Home Economics the same skill means reading a garment pattern or a kitchen layout plan.
Memory aid: "Pick it, read it, check it, then draw it."
