The **National Cultural Heritage Act (RA 10066)** protects what makes Filipinos who they are. **Cultural property** means all products of human creativity by which a people and a nation reveal their identity, whether **movable or immovable, tangible or intangible**, public or private.

**Intangible cultural heritage** covers practices, knowledge and skills that communities recognize as their heritage: oral traditions and languages, performing arts, rituals and festivals, knowledge about nature, and **traditional craftsmanship**. So the skill of weaving t'nalak is heritage, not only the cloth.

The law grades important items. A **National Cultural Treasure** is unique, of outstanding value, and **officially declared** by the proper agency. An **Important Cultural Property** has exceptional significance, as determined by the National Museum or the National Historical Institute.

The **NCCA** leads, working with the National Museum, National Library, NHI, National Archives, Cultural Center of the Philippines and Komisyon sa Wikang Filipino.
Memory aid: "Tangible or intangible — if it shows who we are, it is cultural property."
Sources: SRC-0049 Sec. 3
