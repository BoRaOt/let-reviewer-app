Philippine weaving changes with place and fiber.

**T'nalak** belongs to the **T'boli** of Lake Sebu, **South Cotabato**. It is woven from **abacá** fibers, resist-dyed with the **ikat** technique, on a **backstrap loom**. The women weavers say they receive the designs **in dreams** from Fu Dalu, the goddess of abacá, so they are called dreamweavers and the patterns cannot be mass-produced.

**Inabel** or abel is the **Ilocano** cotton cloth of the **Ilocos Region and Abra**, woven on hardwood pedal looms. It is soft, durable and suited to hot weather. The binakul or whirlwind pattern was believed to confuse evil spirits.

**Piña** is sheer cloth from **pineapple leaves**, especially in **Aklan**, whose handloom weaving is on the UNESCO intangible heritage list. **Abacá**, or Manila hemp, comes from a banana relative found only in the Philippines and also makes rope and fabric.
Memory aid: "South Cotabato dreams in abacá; Ilocos weaves cotton; Aklan weaves pineapple."
