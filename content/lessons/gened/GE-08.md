# GE-08 — Mathematics

GenEd unit, **15 items** (10%). Outcomes: apply **mathematical concepts and procedures**; solve **real-life problems**.

## 1. Plain explanation

Most LET math items are short word problems. The skills that recur:

- **Order of operations:** grouping symbols first, then exponents, then multiplication and division **left to right**, then addition and subtraction **left to right**.
- **Number theory:** primes, divisibility, GCF and LCM.
- **Exponents and sequences:** add exponents when multiplying same bases; arithmetic sequences add a common difference, geometric ones multiply by a common ratio.
- **Ratio, proportion, equations:** cross-multiply; quadratics by formula.
- **Percent and interest:** percent means "per hundred"; simple interest I = Prt; compound interest A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt).
- **Counting and probability:** permutations (order matters) vs combinations (order does not); probability = favorable ÷ total.
- **Geometry:** triangle angles sum to 180°; Pythagorean theorem; area and volume formulas.
- **Sets and logic:** union, intersection, complement; a conditional is false only when the "if" part is true and the "then" part false.
- **Statistics:** mean, median, mode, range, SD (see PE-D1).

## 2. Testable facts

**Numbers and operations (OpenStax Contemporary Mathematics)**
- Order of operations: after grouping symbols, **exponents** are computed first, then **multiplication and division one at a time from left to right** (they hold the **same position** in the order), then **addition and subtraction left to right**. [SRC-0090, 3.3]
- A **prime** is a natural number with **only two unique divisors, 1 and itself** (e.g. 7, 19); a number with other divisors is **composite** (2,117 = 29 × 73). [SRC-0090, 3.1]
- A number is **divisible by 3** if the **sum of its digits** is divisible by 3 (298 → 19 → not divisible). [SRC-0090, 3.1]
- The **greatest common divisor** can be found from the **prime factorization** of the numbers; GCD and LCM solve grouping and scheduling problems. [SRC-0090, 3.1]
- Exponent rules: **product rule aⁿ·aᵐ = aⁿ⁺ᵐ**; **quotient rule** aⁿ/aᵐ = aⁿ⁻ᵐ; **any non-zero number to the zeroth power equals 1**; negative exponents mean reciprocals. [SRC-0090, 3.8]
- **Arithmetic sequence:** the i-th term is **aᵢ = a₁ + d(i − 1)**, where d is the common difference. **Geometric sequence:** each term is the previous term times the **common ratio** (3, 6, 12, 24 … has ratio 2). [SRC-0090, 3.10, 3.11]

**Algebra**
- In a **proportion** a/b = c/d, **cross-multiply**: a × d = b × c. [SRC-0090, 5.4]
- A quadratic in **standard form ax² + bx + c = 0** can be solved with the **quadratic formula x = [−b ± √(b² − 4ac)] / 2a**. [SRC-0090, 5.6]

**Financial mathematics**
- **Percent** comes from Latin *per centum*, "by the hundred". [SRC-0090, 6.1]
- **Simple interest: I = P × r × t** (principal × annual rate × time in years). [SRC-0090, 6.3]
- **Compound interest: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)** (n = compounding periods per year). [SRC-0090, 6.4]
- Budgeting guideline **50-30-20**: needs, wants, savings and debt (see HE-B1). [SRC-0090, 6.5]

**Counting and probability**
- **Permutations** (order matters): **nPr = n! / (n − r)!**; **combinations** (order does not matter): **nCr = n! / [r!(n − r)!]**; **0! = 1**. [SRC-0090, 7.2, 7.3]
- The **sample space** is the set of all possible outcomes; with equally likely outcomes, **P(E) = n(E) / n(S)**; every probability is **between 0 and 1** inclusive. [SRC-0090, 7.5]

**Geometry and measurement**
- The **interior angles of a triangle sum to 180°**; an obtuse triangle has one angle between 90° and 180°; in a right triangle the side opposite the right angle is the **hypotenuse** (longest side). [SRC-0090, 10.3]
- **Pythagorean theorem: a² + b² = c²** for right triangles (c = hypotenuse). [SRC-0090, 10.8]
- Areas: **rectangle A = lw**; **triangle A = ½bh**; **circle A = πr²**. [SRC-0090, 10.6]
- **Volume of a prism V = B·h** (area of base × height). [SRC-0090, 10.7]

**Sets and logic**
- A **set** is a well-defined collection of objects (its **elements**); the **cardinal number** is how many elements it has; the **empty set** has none. [SRC-0090, 1.1]
- **Union** (A ∪ B) = elements in A or B; **intersection** (A ∩ B) = elements in both; **complement** = elements of the universal set not in A; **n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B)**. [SRC-0090, 1.4]
- A **conditional** "if p then q" is **false only when p is true and q is false**; a **biconditional** is true when p and q have the same truth value. [SRC-0090, 2.4]

**Statistics**
- **Mean** = sum ÷ count; **median** = middle value; **mode** = most frequent; the mean is pulled by **outliers**, the median is not. [SRC-0109, Ch. 12 "Central tendency and variability"]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Multiplication is not always before division** — go left to right: 12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 8, not 2.
- **aⁿ·aᵐ adds exponents; (aⁿ)ᵐ multiplies them.** a⁰ = 1 only for a ≠ 0.
- **Permutation vs combination:** choosing a president and VP (order matters) vs choosing a 2-person committee (order does not).
- **Simple vs compound interest:** simple interest is on the principal only.
- **Percent increase then decrease does not cancel:** +20% then −20% leaves 96% of the original.
- **1 is neither prime nor composite.**
- **Conditional truth table:** "If it rains, the ground is wet" is not false when it doesn't rain.

## 4. Memory aid

**"GEMDAS, left to right twice."**
**"Order matters → Permutation (P for Position); Committee → Combination."**
**"I = PRT" — "I PRay for Time."**
