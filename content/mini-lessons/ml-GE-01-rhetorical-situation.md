Every purposeful message responds to a situation. **Lloyd Bitzer**, in a 1968 article, described the **rhetorical situation** as an event made of three parts.
1. An **issue or exigence**: a problem or need that calls for communication, such as rising absences in a class.
2. An **audience**: the people who can be moved to act on the issue, such as parents.
3. **Constraints**: the beliefs, facts, time limits, traditions and relationships that shape what can be said and how.

Richard **Vatz** later replied in "The Myth of the Rhetorical Situation" that speakers help create situations by choosing what to talk about.

For a teacher writing a letter to parents about absences, analyzing the situation means asking: What is the problem? Who must act? What do they already believe, and what language and channel will reach them? The OpenStax Writing Guide calls this the conditions of communication around the message.
