On **January 20, 1872**, Filipino military personnel at **Fort San Felipe**, the Spanish arsenal in Cavite, rose up. The trigger was economic: laborers found that taxes and the *falla*, the fee paid to be exempt from forced labor, had been deducted from their pay.

The colonial government and Spanish friars used the mutiny to accuse three secular Filipino priests, **Mariano Gómez, José Burgos and Jacinto Zamora**, known together as **Gomburza**. They were executed. Their deaths deeply affected a generation of Filipinos, and the movement for reform, and later revolution, grew from that anger.

The event is a classic case of **conflicting accounts**: some describe a plot to remove the Spanish government, others a protest over lost privileges and pay. Readers must compare who wrote each account and why.
Memory aid: "1872: pay cut → mutiny → Gomburza martyred → nationalism grows."
