In housekeeping, the surface decides the method. TESDA's unit on cleaning public areas starts with **selecting and setting up** equipment and materials.

Choose equipment for the type of cleaning, and check it is clean and safe before you use it. Choose suitable **dry or wet cleaning agents** according to the manufacturer's directions and safety rules. Wear **protective clothing** when needed, such as aprons, overalls, goggles and masks, waterproof clothing and footwear.

Then match chemicals to the **material**: glass, leather, fabrics, brass or steel, wood, carpet and hard floors each need different products. A glass cleaner is wrong for leather, and a harsh chemical can damage wood.

Stay alert to hazards: spills, wet or slippery floors, fumes, broken glass, sharp objects, hot surfaces, and dangerous waste like blood or used needles, which need special handling.
Memory aid: "Surface first, chemical second, gear on, eyes open."
