# HE-A7 — Technology for Teaching and Learning

Annex F gives this line **3 items** (Understand 1, Apply 1, Analyze 1). Outcomes: integrate educational technology into teaching; **evaluate online resources**, search effectively and manage digital content; use **multimedia, gamification and adaptive platforms** for diverse learners. [SRC-0002, Annex F, A.7] ProfEd B3 (educational technology) overlaps heavily.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Technology changes the teacher's role.** With more computers and internet access, a teacher moves from "full-frontal teaching" to **"guide on the side"**, helping students build their own knowledge. But technology costs money, does not suit every activity, and students need help telling trustworthy sites from "fluff".

**TPACK** says good tech integration blends three kinds of teacher knowledge: **technology (TK), pedagogy (PK) and content (CK)**. The sweet spot where all three overlap is TPACK.

**Evaluate online sources** with the **CRAAP test**: Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose. A faster alternative is **SIFT**: Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims to the original.

**Gamification** adds game elements (points, badges, leaderboards, levels, immediate feedback) to ordinary lessons. It is different from **game-based learning**, where students learn by playing or making a game.

**Adaptive learning** uses computer algorithms (and AI) to adjust material and activities to each learner's needs.

**Law still applies.** Copying for classroom teaching can be *fair use* under the IP Code, judged by several factors. Learners' personal data are protected by the Data Privacy Act.

## 2. Testable facts

**Technology and the teacher's role (Seifert & Sutton Ch. 1)**
- Technology has not always been integrated thoroughly, partly because many classrooms have only one or two computers and limited internet; computers then tend to be used in traditional ways (a "fancy typewriter" or encyclopedia). [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 "New trend #2: using technology to support learning"]
- Even one classroom computer can present assignments, enrich students with special interests, and give extra review to those who need it. [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 "New trend #2"]
- More technology makes the shift from **"full-frontal teaching" to "guide on the side"** easier: teachers facilitate students' own construction of knowledge. [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 "New trend #2"]
- Challenges: cost (money taken from staff or books), students needing help to sort **trustworthy information from "fluff"**, and activities (sports, choral practice) that do not suit computers. [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 "New trend #2"]

**TPACK**
- TPACK describes the intersections of **technology, pedagogy and content** for effective technology integration; it builds on **Shulman's pedagogical content knowledge (PCK)**. [SRC-0144, lead and "History"]
- Three base domains: **CK, PK, TK**; pairwise intersections: **PCK, TCK, TPK**; all three together: **TPACK**. Context (XK) surrounds them. [SRC-0144, lead]
- Developed by **Mishra and Koehler** (named TPCK, later TPACK). [SRC-0144, "History"]

**Evaluating online resources**
- **CRAAP = Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose**; created by librarian **Sarah Blakeslee (2004)** to help students judge whether a source is credible. [SRC-0145, lead and "History"]
- Critics prefer step-by-step heuristics such as Mike Caulfield's **SIFT: Stop; Investigate the source; Find better coverage; Trace claims, quotes and media to the original context**. [SRC-0145, "Alternatives and criticisms"]
- Studies found students and even historians are fooled by official-looking **logos and domain names**, while professional fact-checkers reached better-supported conclusions in a fraction of the time. [SRC-0145, "Alternatives and criticisms"]

**Gamification and adaptive platforms**
- **Gamification of learning** uses video-game design and game elements in learning environments to motivate students and keep them participating. [SRC-0146, lead]
- Game elements that can support learning: **points, badges and leaderboards (PBLs)**, narrative and characters, player control, **immediate feedback**, collaborative problem solving, **scaffolded increasing challenge**, mastery and **leveling up**, social connection. [SRC-0146, "Game elements that can facilitate learning"]
- Gamification is **not** game-based learning: in game-based learning students play or create games and the learning is inside the game; **serious games** are built with learning goals in the gameplay. [SRC-0146, lead]
- Surface rewards alone (e.g. badges on a quiz) may raise participation without improving achievement; gamification must be tied meaningfully to learning goals. [SRC-0146, lead]
- **Adaptive learning** uses computer algorithms and artificial intelligence to deliver **customized resources and activities** to each learner's needs; it grew out of **intelligent tutoring** research in the **1970s**. [SRC-0147, lead and "History"]

**Media choice**
- **Edgar Dale** introduced the **Cone of Experience** in **1946** (revised 1954 and 1969) as an intuitive model of how **concrete or abstract** different media are. The popular "we remember 10% of what we read…" percentages are **not Dale's**; he included no numbers. [SRC-0148, "Cone of Experience"]

**Law in the digital classroom**
- **Fair use** for criticism, comment, news reporting, **teaching including multiple copies for classroom use**, scholarship and research is not infringement. Factors include the **purpose and character** of the use (commercial vs non-profit educational) and the **nature of the work**, among others. [SRC-0044, Sec. 185.1]
- Learners' **education and health** records are **sensitive personal information** under the Data Privacy Act. [SRC-0045, Sec. 3(l)]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **TPACK is not "using more gadgets".** It is the fit between content, pedagogy and technology.
- **CRAAP's A's.** Authority = who wrote it; Accuracy = is it supported and correct. Purpose = why it was made (inform, sell, persuade).
- **Gamification ≠ playing games.** Adding badges to a worksheet is gamification; learning fractions through a math adventure game is game-based learning.
- **Dale's Cone has no retention percentages.** An item claiming "Dale found people remember 90% of what they do" is a myth.
- **Fair use is not automatic** for all school copying; it is weighed by factors.

## 4. Memory aid

**"CRAAP before you copy."** Currency, Relevance, Authority, Accuracy, Purpose.
**TPACK "Tech + Teach + Topic."**
