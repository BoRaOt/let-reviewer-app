How learners explain their results shapes their motivation. Weiner's attribution ideas describe three dimensions.
- **Locus:** internal ("I am good at this") or external ("the test was easy").
- **Stability:** ability is fairly stable; effort is unstable and must be renewed each time.
- **Controllability:** effort is controllable; luck and task difficulty are not.

Teachers should steer learners toward **effort and strategy**, which are internal, unstable and controllable: "You improved because you practiced the steps."

**Self-efficacy**, from Bandura, is the belief that you can carry out a specific task or reach a specific goal. It is like confidence, but tied to a particular task. When self-efficacy is very low, a learner may develop **learned helplessness**, feeling no control at all over success, and stop trying. Small, achievable successes and specific feedback rebuild it.
Memory aid: "Where? How lasting? Can I change it? — praise effort, build efficacy."
