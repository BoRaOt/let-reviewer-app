# HE-B6 — Clothing construction and design

Annex F gives this line **12 items** (Understand 2, Apply 6, Analyze 2, Evaluate 1, Create 1). Outcomes: **clothing selection, purchase and care**; **principles of clothing construction**; sewing **materials, tools and equipment**; **body measurement**; **pattern drafting**. [SRC-0002, Annex F, B.6] Expect scenario items: which measurement, which tool, which grain, what a care symbol means, what went wrong with a seam.

HE-A2 covers fibers, Philippine fabrics and the four machine faults; HE-B8 covers machine types and maintenance. This lesson goes deeper on measuring, drafting, cutting, sewing and care.

## 1. Plain explanation

**The TESDA dressmaking sequence** is: plan the design with the client → take body measurements in order → draft a basic (block) pattern → manipulate it into the style → cut the final pattern → prepare and cut fabric → sew → apply finishing touches → press.

**A block pattern (sloper)** is a basic, fitted pattern (bodice, skirt, trousers) from which many styles are made. Patterns are drafted flat from measurements, or made by **draping** fabric on a form.

**Grain matters.** Straight grain runs along the selvedge (warp threads) and stretches least, so most garments are cut with it running top to bottom. **Bias** (45°) stretches most and drapes softly.

**Seam allowance** is the strip between the cut edge and the stitching line. Patterns are cut with or without it.

**Selection and care.** Choose design and fabric for the client and the occasion. Follow care labels: washing tub, bleach triangle, dryer square, iron dots, professional-cleaning circle.

**Design.** Clothes use the same elements as art: line, shape, color, texture, value, form, space. Color schemes (monochromatic, analogous, complementary, triadic) guide combinations.

## 2. Testable facts

**Body measurement and pattern drafting (TESDA Dressmaking NC II, "Draft and cut pattern for casual apparel")**
- Elements: (1) plan garment design; (2) take client's body measurements; (3) draft basic/block pattern; (4) manipulate pattern; (5) cut final pattern. [SRC-0080, draft and cut pattern unit]
- Design and **fabric are discussed and selected** according to the client's specifications, and the client's **special needs** (pocket, stitch, collar, sleeve, skirt, cut) are incorporated. [SRC-0080, draft and cut pattern unit, element 1 and range of variables 3]
- Measurements are taken **in sequence**, following standard body measurement, and **recorded**. Assessors check the **correct position and sequence** of measuring. [SRC-0080, draft and cut pattern unit, element 2 and evidence guide 1.4]
- Standard body measurements listed: shoulder; neck circumference; chest width and bust circumference; bust height and width; figure (front and back); **waistline; hips**; lengths of sleeve, blouse, skirt and dress; **armhole circumference**. [SRC-0080, draft and cut pattern unit, range of variables 5]
- Measuring tools: **tape measure, hip curve rule, L-square, meter stick**. [SRC-0080, draft and cut pattern unit, range of variables 4]
- Drafting tools: **tailor's chalk, pattern paper, ruler with grid, triangle (45°–90°), hip curve, French curve, pencil**. Cutting tool: **scissors**. [SRC-0080, draft and cut pattern unit, range of variables 6, 8]
- Basic/block patterns listed: blouse, skirt, trouser, shorts, culottes, shirt. Final pattern is **labeled**, filed and secured; it is cut **with or without seam allowance**. [SRC-0080, draft and cut pattern unit, elements 4–5 and range of variables 7, 9]
- A **sloper (block) pattern** is a custom-fitted basic pattern from which many styles are developed; changing the size of a finished pattern is **grading**. [SRC-0151, lead]
- Pattern symbols: **notches** mark seam allowances, centerlines, zipper placement and matching points; **circular holes** mark dart apex, pocket and buttonhole placement; a **long arrow is the grainline**, placed parallel to the straight grain; **double lines** show where to lengthen or shorten. [SRC-0151, "Standard pattern symbols"]
- **Flat-pattern method:** pattern drafted on a flat surface from measurements with rulers and curves. **Draping:** pinning fabric (e.g. calico) on a form to make a mock-up pattern. [SRC-0151, "Pattern making"]

**Preparing and cutting fabric**
- Fabric is checked for **quality, faults, width and selvedges**, soaked or drip-dried and pressed; patterns are pinned **along the grain line** and seam allowances marked before cutting. [SRC-0080, "Prepare and cut materials", elements 1–2]
- Underpinning knowledge: **fabric properties such as weight, shrinkage, pile, grain and pattern shapes**. [SRC-0080, "Prepare and cut materials", evidence guide 2.2]
- **Straight grain** runs parallel to the warp threads and **selvedge** and has the **least stretch**; most garments are cut with it running top to bottom. [SRC-0150, "Straight grain"]
- **Cross grain** runs perpendicular to the selvedge, along the weft, with more stretch. [SRC-0150, "Cross grain"]
- **Bias** is any direction between them; **true bias is 45°**. Woven fabric is most elastic and fluid on the bias, so bias-cut garments drape and cling to body curves. [SRC-0150, "Bias"]
- **Seam allowance** is the area between the fabric edge and the stitching line. Home-sewing patterns use **1/4 to 5/8 inch** (about 6–16 mm); 5/8 in (1.5 cm) is common and helps with fabrics that ravel. Curved areas (neckline, armhole) get narrower allowances. [SRC-0149, lead]

**Sewing and finishing (Dressmaking NC II)**
- Accessories and accents: **zipper, buttons, bias tape, hook and eye**. [SRC-0080, "Prepare and cut materials", range of variables 4]
- Sleeve types listed: **puff, tailored, cup**. [SRC-0080, "Sew casual apparel", range of variables 3]
- In finishing, bodice **hem allowances** and sleeves are folded and pinned, and **hemline allowances are sewn** per the given stitch/seam specifications. [SRC-0080, "Apply finishing touches", elements 1.6–1.7]
- Faults to correct before release: **grease/oil spots, puckering, loose threads, missing buttons, uneven stitches**. [SRC-0080, "Apply finishing touches", range of variables 3]

**Care labels**
- A **care label** carries pictograms for washing, bleaching, drying, ironing and professional cleaning. [SRC-0152, lead]
- **Wash tub**: the number is the maximum water temperature in °C (US labels use dots). **Triangle**: bleach allowed; two oblique lines = no chlorine bleach; crossed = **no bleach**. **Square with circle**: tumble dryer. **Iron** with 1, 2 or 3 dots = about **110, 150, 200 °C**. **Circle**: professional (dry) cleaning. [SRC-0152, "Washing", "Bleaching", "Drying", "Ironing", "Professional cleaning"]

**Design and color**
- Elements of design: **line, shape, form, color, space, texture, value**. Color has three properties: **hue, intensity (chroma), value**. [SRC-0154, sections "Line" to "Value"]
- Color schemes: **monochromatic** (tints and shades of one hue), **analogous** (neighbors on the color wheel), **complementary** (opposites; maximum contrast), **split-complementary** (a base plus the two colors beside its complement), **triadic** (three evenly spaced hues, 120° apart). [SRC-0153, "Monochromatic", "Complementary", "Analogous", "Evenly spaced hues"]

**Philippine context**
- Government uniforms must use **Philippine tropical fabrics** (natural fibers produced, spun, woven or knitted and finished in the Philippines). [SRC-0050, Secs. 2–3]

**Shaping, fullness and finishing terms (Stage 7 addition; Wikipedia, tertiary)**
- **Darts** are folds that taper to a point, sewn to take in ease and **give shape** to a garment, especially at the bust. [SRC-0258, lead]
- **Gathering** turns an edge into many **small, irregular folds** held by a thread near the edge; the fabric becomes shorter where stitched and flares beyond. [SRC-0259, lead]
- A **pleat** is a fold made by **doubling fabric back on itself and securing it**; pleats are structured, unlike soft gathers. [SRC-0260, lead]
- A **hem** is an edge **folded and sewn** to **prevent unravelling** and set the length, as at the bottom of a garment or sleeve end. [SRC-0263, lead]
- A **seam ripper** is a small tool for **cutting and removing stitches**; its forked head has a cutting edge at the base of the fork. [SRC-0261, lead]
- A **tracing wheel** has teeth on a wheel and **transfers pattern markings onto fabric**, with or without tracing (carbon) paper. [SRC-0262, lead]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Draft before cut, block before style.** The block is drafted from measurements first, then manipulated into the final pattern.
- **Straight grain = along the selvedge**, not across it. Bias is diagonal (45°), not "the edge".
- **Hip curve vs French curve.** Both are curves; the hip curve is for long gentle curves (hip, side seam), the French curve for tighter ones (neck, armhole). (General tailoring knowledge; TESDA lists both tools.)
- **Crossed triangle = no bleach**, not "no washing". The circle alone is dry or professional cleaning, not tumble drying (that is a circle *inside a square*).
- **More iron dots = hotter.** Silk (Level 1) gets the coolest iron; cotton and linen higher.
- **Complementary ≠ analogous.** Opposite on the wheel vs side by side.
- **Dart vs pleat vs gather.** Dart = pointed fold for *fit*; pleat = pressed, structured fold; gather = soft, irregular bunching from pulled thread.

## 4. Memory aid

**Dressmaking flow "Plan–Measure–Draft–Style–Cut–Sew–Finish–Press."**
**Care symbols "Tub, Triangle, Square, Iron, Circle" = Wash, Bleach, Dry, Press, Pro-clean.**
**"Dart = Do-fit; Pleat = Pressed; Gather = Gentle bunch."**
