**Globalization** is the process of integrating governments, cultures and financial markets through international trade into a single world market. It often starts with one motive, such as a company seeking new markets, and spreads to culture, politics and health.

Two forces drive it: the global economy and **multinational corporations**, which control assets, sales, production and jobs in many countries at once. Fast transport and communication make this possible.

Globalization has winners and losers. Some developing countries gain factories, call centers and jobs. But **outsourcing** of manufacturing and service work has raised unemployment in some developed countries, and not every country benefits; the gap in income per person between the richest and poorest countries remains enormous.

To relate this to local life, think of Filipino BPO workers serving foreign clients, imported goods in the sari-sari store, and global prices affecting rice and fuel at home.
Memory aid: "One world market — built by trade, run by multinationals, felt locally."
