The humanistic psychologists focused on growth and potential.

**Carl Rogers** centered his theory on the **self-concept**: our thoughts and feelings about ourselves. He compared the **real self**, who we actually are, with the **ideal self**, who we think we should be. When the two are very similar, we experience **congruence**, and our self-concept is accurate. When they are far apart, we experience incongruence and distress. Parents, and teachers, help children toward congruence by giving **unconditional positive regard**: accepting and valuing them without conditions.

**Abraham Maslow** studied healthy, creative, productive people. His hierarchy of needs rises from basic needs to the highest one, **self-actualization**: achieving one's fullest potential.

For a future teacher, these ideas suggest accepting learners as persons even while correcting their behavior, and examining your own real and ideal teacher selves.
Memory aid: "Rogers: Real ≈ Ideal = Congruence; Maslow: top = self-actualization."
