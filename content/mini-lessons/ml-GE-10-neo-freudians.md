The **neo-Freudians** accepted parts of Freud's ideas but changed others, giving more weight to social and cultural forces. Four are commonly tested.

**Alfred Adler** founded **individual psychology**, which focuses on our drive to **compensate for feelings of inferiority**. A learner who feels weak in one area may strive hard to excel in another.

**Carl Jung** explored the **collective unconscious**, a deep layer of the mind shared by all humans, containing universal images and themes.

**Karen Horney**, one of the first women trained as a Freudian analyst, rejected "penis envy" and said growth can be blocked by **basic anxiety** from unmet needs such as loneliness.

**Erik Erikson** described **eight psychosocial stages** across the whole lifespan, each with its own crisis, such as identity versus role confusion in adolescence.

On the exam, "inferiority" points to Adler and "collective unconscious" points to Jung.
Memory aid: "Adler = inferiority, Jung = collective unconscious, Horney = basic anxiety, Erikson = eight stages."
