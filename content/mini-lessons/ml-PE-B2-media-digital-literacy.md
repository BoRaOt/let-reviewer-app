**Media literacy** is the ability to **access, analyze, evaluate and create** media in many forms, and to reflect critically and act ethically. A media-literate learner asks who made a message, why, how it tries to persuade, and what is left out, and can also make responsible media of their own. It can start in early childhood with simple critical-thinking questions about ads.

**Digital literacy** is the ability to **find, evaluate and communicate** information using digital devices and platforms, and to create and share in digital environments. It combines technical skills with thinking skills. The term was first defined by **Paul Gilster in 1997**.

Access alone is not enough. **Henry Jenkins** described a **participation gap**: even where nearly everyone is online, some learners lack the skills and confidence to take part fully. Teachers close it by teaching evaluation and creation, not just clicking.
Memory aid: "Access, Analyze, Evaluate, Create, Act."
