Most research articles follow **IMRaD**: Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion.
- **Introduction:** Why was the study done? What is the research question or hypothesis?
- **Methods:** Who took part, when, where and how was the study done, and with what materials or instruments?
- **Results:** What did the study find? Was the hypothesis supported?
- **Discussion:** What do the findings mean, how do they fit other research, and what should be studied next?

The flow is often drawn as a **wine glass** or hourglass: wide and general at the start, narrow and specific in the methods and results, then wide again in the discussion.

A quick test: a sentence describing the sample and instrument belongs in Methods; a percentage or mean belongs in Results; "this suggests" belongs in Discussion. Papers may also carry funding and ethics statements.
Memory aid: "I Must Report Discoveries" = Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion.
