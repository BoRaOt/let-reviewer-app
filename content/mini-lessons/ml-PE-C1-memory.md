The information processing view compares the mind to a computer. Richard Atkinson and Richard Shiffrin proposed in 1968 that information passes through three stages before it is stored for good.
1. **Sensory memory:** a very brief hold on everything we see and hear. Only what we attend to moves on.
2. **Short-term memory:** a small, temporary store that works on incoming information and links it to what we already know. It is a component of working memory, and the two terms are not exactly the same.
3. **Long-term memory:** continuous storage with a capacity believed to be unlimited.

For teaching, this explains why attention comes first, why new information should connect to prior knowledge, and why practice and retrieval help move learning into long-term memory.
Memory aid: "See it, hold it, keep it" — sensory, short-term, long-term.
