**Constructed response** items ask learners to recall and produce an answer, so guessing drops.

**Completion** and **short-answer** items are nearly the same. Completion gives a statement with a blank; short answer asks a direct question. Both are answered with a word, phrase, number or symbol, and they are common in mathematics, such as "If x = 6, what is x(x − 1)?". Write them so only one answer fits.

**Extended response**, or essay, items ask for a paragraph or more. Their main strength is measuring **complex outcomes**, especially integration and application, and they also show writing skill. They seem easy to write, but good prompts that match the outcome are hard. A clear prompt states the task and may give planning notes, such as the points to cover.

Essays are hard to score reliably. Use a **scoring rubric** or point scheme.
Memory aid: "Short = one right word; Essay = complex thinking, needs a rubric."
