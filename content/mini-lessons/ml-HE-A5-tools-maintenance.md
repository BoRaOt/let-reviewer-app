TESDA's unit on maintaining tools and equipment begins with checking their condition. A worker identifies materials, tools and equipment by their classification and the job they suit. Non-functional or defective tools are separated from good ones and labeled, so nobody uses them by mistake. Tool safety follows the manufacturer's instructions, and the condition of personal protective equipment is checked too. Maintenance then continues with preventive care and proper storage. The knowledge behind this includes safe handling of tools, the use of protective equipment, good housekeeping, and the types and uses of lubricants and cleaning materials. In a Home Economics kitchen or sewing room, the same habits apply: inspect knives and machines before use, set aside and tag anything broken, clean and lubricate as the manual directs, and store everything in its place.
Memory aid: "Check, tag, clean, oil, store."
