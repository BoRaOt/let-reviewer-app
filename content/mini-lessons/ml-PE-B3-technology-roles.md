Many Philippine classrooms have one computer or none, and patchy internet. Seifert and Sutton note that when computers are scarce they are often used only as a "fancy typewriter" or an encyclopedia. Yet even **one classroom computer** can show upcoming assignments, enrich a learner with a special interest, and give extra review to learners who need it.

As technology grows, the teacher's role shifts from **"full-frontal teaching"** to **"guide on the side"**, helping individuals plan and carry out their own learning. But technology has costs. It takes money that might buy books or hire staff, learners need help to tell reliable sites from "fluff", and some activities, such as choral practice or sports, do not suit it.

Two newer uses are **gamification**, adding points, badges, levels and quick feedback to lessons, and **adaptive learning**, in which software adjusts tasks to each learner's answers.
Memory aid: "One computer can still Present, Enrich, Review."
