Historians build accounts of the past from evidence. A **primary source** comes from the time or from people who took part: a diary, a letter, a photograph, an eyewitness account, a law, a constitution. Antonio Pigafetta's journal of Magellan's voyage and the 1898 Act of the Declaration of Independence are primary sources for Philippine history.

A **secondary source** interprets, analyzes or comments on primary sources, such as a textbook chapter or a historian's article about the Revolution.

A primary source is **not automatically more reliable**. Eyewitnesses have viewpoints, interests and gaps. Pigafetta sailed with Magellan; a Spanish official reporting on the 1872 mutiny wrote for colonial authorities. So historians compare several sources, ask who wrote each one, when, why and for whom, and look for agreement and contradiction. This is why some events, like the Cry of 1896, still have disputed dates and places.
Memory aid: "Primary = from the time; Secondary = about the time; always ask who, when, why."
