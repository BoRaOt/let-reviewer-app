The term **action research** was coined by **Kurt Lewin** in 1944. He described it as a **spiral of steps**, each a circle of **planning, action and fact-finding** about the result of the action. Each turn of the spiral leads to new planning.

Seifert and Sutton list four ideas that most action research studies share.
1. **Teaching is itself a form of research.** Every lesson tests ideas about how learners learn.
2. **Action research requires substantial reflection**, just as good teaching does.
3. **Collaboration** among teachers is crucial, both to make the research meaningful and to improve teaching.
4. **Teachers' knowledge should be shared publicly**, especially when it was gained systematically.

So a teacher who quietly tries a new seating plan is reflecting; a teacher who plans it, collects data, studies the results, and presents them at a school learning session is doing action research.
Memory aid: "Lewin's spiral: Plan, Act, Find out — and teachers Research, Reflect, Collaborate, Share."
