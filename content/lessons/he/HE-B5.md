# HE-B5 — Arts and crafts

Annex F gives this line **12 items** (Understand 2, Apply 6, Analyze 2, Evaluate 1, Create 1). Outcomes: art forms in **daily life and cultural identity**; applying art and creativity to everyday life (living spaces, **DIY crafts**); the role of **handicraft industries in culture and economy**; the **history of Philippine handicraft**; **famous handicrafts of selected regions**. [SRC-0002, Annex F, B.5] GE-06 (Art Appreciation) shares the art-elements material.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Crafts are culture you can use.** Mats, baskets, woven cloth, carved wood and metalwork are both **useful** and **expressive**. They carry each community's identity: T'boli t'nalak, Ilocano inabel, Maranao okir, Paete woodcarving.

**The State protects them.** The **Manlilikha ng Bayan Act (RA 7355, 1992)** honors master folk artists who keep a tradition alive and teach it. The **National Cultural Heritage Act (RA 10066)** protects cultural property, including **intangible heritage** such as traditional craftsmanship. The **Tropical Fabrics Law (RA 9242)** creates demand for local fibers.

**Crafts are also livelihood.** RA 7355 itself calls for vocational courses based on an awardee's methods and for studying how to turn the art into a **cottage industry**. Piña, abacá, banig and capiz products are traded nationally and abroad.

**Design still matters.** DIY crafts and home decoration use the elements of design (line, shape, color, texture, value, form, space), color schemes, and compositional techniques such as the rule of thirds.

## 2. Testable facts

**GAMABA — Manlilikha ng Bayan Act (RA 7355, 1992)**
- **Manlilikha ng Bayan:** a citizen or group engaged in a traditional art uniquely Filipino whose skills have reached a high level of technical and artistic excellence and have been **passed on to and widely practiced** by the present generation in the community. [SRC-0048, Sec. 3]
- **Traditional folk art:** expressions of distinctiveness and artistic quality serving **decorative, utilitarian or sociocultural** functions. [SRC-0048, Sec. 3]
- Criteria: (a) **technical and creative skill** (mastery of tools and materials); (b) **artistic quality**; (c) **community tradition** (has transferred or will transfer the skill); (d) **folk art tradition in existence and documented for at least 50 years**; (e) **character and integrity**. [SRC-0048, Sec. 4]
- Incentives: plaque or medal (duplicate displayed in the provincial museum); **₱100,000 initial grant and ₱10,000 a month for life**; documentation of works; possible **vocational course** based on the awardee's methods; DTI feasibility study to turn the art into a **cottage industry**. [SRC-0048, Sec. 5]
- The award is implemented by the **National Commission for Culture and the Arts (NCCA)**, following UNESCO's Living Human Treasures concept; it replaced the 1988–1992 National Folk Artists Award. [SRC-0170, "History"]
- Categories include **weaving, carving, pottery, textile or fiber arts, ornament, folk architecture, maritime transport, performing arts, literature, graphic and plastic arts**; **ethnomedicine** was added in 2020. [SRC-0170, "Categories"]

**GAMABA awardees in crafts (selected)**
- **Lang Dulay** (T'boli, Lake Sebu, South Cotabato, 1998): **t'nalak** abaca-ikat weaving. [SRC-0170, "Recipients"]
- **Salinta Monon** (Tagabawa-Bagobo, Davao del Sur, 1998): abaca-ikat (inabal) weaving. [SRC-0170, "Recipients"]
- **Darhata Sawabi** (Tausug, Sulu, 2004): **pis syabit** weaving. **Haja Amina Appi** (Sama, Tawi-Tawi, 2004): master **mat** weaver. **Eduardo Mutuc** (Kapampangan, Apalit, Pampanga, 2004): **pinukpuk** bronze and silver metalwork. [SRC-0170, "Recipients"]
- **Magdalena Gamayo** (Ilocano, Pinili, Ilocos Norte, 2012): **inabel/abel** weaving. **Teofilo Garcia** (San Quintin, Abra, 2012): **tabungaw** native headgear. [SRC-0170, "Recipients"]
- **Ambalang Ausalin** (Yakan, Lamitan, Basilan, 2016): Yakan **tennun** tapestry weaving. [SRC-0170, "Recipients"]
- **Ginaw Bilog** (Hanunuo Mangyan, Oriental Mindoro, 1993) preserved the **Mangyan script and poetry (ambahan)** written on bamboo. [SRC-0170, "Recipients"]

**Cultural heritage law (RA 10066)**
- **Cultural property:** all products of human creativity by which a people and nation reveal their identity, **movable or immovable, tangible or intangible**. [SRC-0049, Sec. 3(o)]
- **Intangible cultural heritage** includes oral traditions, performing arts, rituals and festive events, knowledge about nature, and **traditional craftsmanship**. [SRC-0049, Sec. 3(x)]
- **National Cultural Treasure:** a unique cultural property of outstanding value, **officially declared** by the pertinent cultural agency; **Important Cultural Property** has exceptional significance as determined by the National Museum and/or NHI. [SRC-0049, Sec. 3(w), (bb)]
- The **NCCA** is "the Commission" under the Act; cultural agencies include the **National Museum, National Library, NHI, National Archives, CCP, and KWF**. [SRC-0049, Sec. 3(h)–(j)]

**Famous regional crafts**
- **T'nalak:** T'boli (South Cotabato) cloth of **abacá**, resist-dyed by **ikat**, woven on a **backstrap loom** by women who receive designs **in dreams** ("dreamweavers"); designs are a gift from **Fu Dalu**, goddess of abacá. [SRC-0171, lead]
- **Inabel (abel):** Ilocano handwoven **cotton** textile from the **Ilocos Region and Abra**, woven on **hardwood pedal looms**; the **binakul** (whirlwind) pattern is said to ward off evil spirits. [SRC-0172, lead and "Description"]
- **Piña:** pineapple-leaf fiber woven into sheer **nipis**; **Aklan** piña handloom weaving is on UNESCO's intangible heritage list (2023). **Abacá** (Manila hemp) is endemic to the Philippines; the country produces most of the world's supply. [SRC-0142, lead; SRC-0143, lead]
- **Banig:** handwoven mats, typically of **pandan or sedge** (also buri, reed, rattan); the Waray of **Basey, Samar** weave banig from **tikog**. [SRC-0173, lead and "Regional/ethnic styles"]
- **Okir (okil):** curvilinear and rectilinear plant-based motifs of the **Maranao** and other Moro groups; decorates the **torogan** (royal house) with carved **panolong** beams; motifs include the **naga** (S-shaped sea serpent). [SRC-0174, lead and "History"]
- **Paete, Laguna:** "**Carving Capital of the Philippines**", known for woodcarving (ukit) and **taka** (papier-mâché formed over carved wooden molds). [SRC-0175, lead and "Taka"]
- **Capiz shell:** shell of the windowpane oyster (*Placuna placenta*), long used as a **glass substitute** in windows and for decorative items. [SRC-0176, lead]

**Design for everyday crafts**
- Elements of design: **line, shape, color, texture, value, form, space**. [SRC-0155, "Elements of design"]
- Compositional techniques include the **rule of thirds**, rule of odds, rule of space, simplification, geometry and symmetry, and creating movement. [SRC-0155, "Compositional techniques"]
- Color schemes: monochromatic, analogous, complementary, split-complementary, triadic. [SRC-0153, scheme sections]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **GAMABA ≠ National Artist.** GAMABA (RA 7355) honors *traditional folk* artists of a community; the Order of National Artists honors other artists.
- **50 years** is the minimum age of the *tradition*, not of the artist.
- **T'nalak is abacá, inabel is cotton.** T'nalak = T'boli, South Cotabato; inabel = Ilocano, Ilocos/Abra.
- **Okir is a motif tradition (Maranao)**, not a fabric.
- **Paete = woodcarving and taka**, not pottery.
- **Intangible heritage includes craftsmanship skills**, not only songs and dances.
