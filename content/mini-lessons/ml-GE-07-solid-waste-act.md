The **Ecological Solid Waste Management Act of 2000 (RA 9003)** shifted the country from dumping to managing waste.

Its core practice is **segregation at source**: separating different kinds of waste where they are produced, at home, in school or at work, so more can be reused and recycled and less has to be collected and disposed of.

**LGUs** implement the law. The **barangay** handles segregation and collection of biodegradable, compostable and reusable waste, and every barangay or cluster of barangays must have a **Materials Recovery Facility (MRF)**, which can include a sorting station, composting facility and recycling facility. Each LGU had to **divert at least 25 percent** of its waste through reuse, recycling and composting within five years.

The law also **banned open dumps**. They were to become controlled dumps within three years, and controlled dumps were not allowed after five years.
Memory aid: "Segregate at source, MRF per barangay, 25% in 5 years, no open dumps."
