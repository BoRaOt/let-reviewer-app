subtopic: Fat-soluble vs water-soluble vitamins

The thirteen vitamins fall into two groups.
- **Fat-soluble:** vitamins A, D, E and K. Because they dissolve in fat, they can be stored in the body. The liver, for example, is the richest store of vitamin A, which is why liver is such a rich food source.
- **Water-soluble:** vitamin C and the B vitamins, which are thiamine, riboflavin, niacin, pantothenic acid, pyridoxine, biotin, folate and cobalamin. Vitamin C is not stored in significant amounts, and the more you take in, the more the kidneys let out.

This difference explains two exam favorites. Very large doses of fat-soluble vitamins can build up, while water-soluble vitamins need regular intake from food. If a question asks which group vitamin C belongs to, the answer is water-soluble. A vitamin that is not A, D, E or K is water-soluble.
Memory aid: "A Dog Eats Kibble" = fat-soluble A, D, E, K.
