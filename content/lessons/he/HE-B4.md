# HE-B4 — Family life and child development

Annex F gives this line **15 items**, the most of any Home Economics line. Most are *Apply* items (7), with *Understand* (3), *Analyze* (3) and *Evaluate* (2). The outcomes are:
- differentiate developmental milestones from infancy to adolescence, and apply child development theories
- use healthy communication, conflict resolution and emotional intelligence in marriage and family
- apply parenting styles
- analyze family structures, roles and dynamics
- assess cultural, social and economic factors in Filipino families

[SRC-0002, Annex F, B.4]. The major development theories (Piaget, Erikson, Vygotsky, Kohlberg, Bronfenbrenner) are in the PE-C1 lesson. Learn them there and apply them here.

## 1. Plain explanation

**The legal family.** In the Philippines, marriage and family relations are governed by the **Family Code** (Executive Order No. 209, 1987).
- It calls marriage a special contract of permanent union and the foundation of the family.
- A valid marriage needs essential requisites (legal capacity and freely given consent) and formal requisites (an authorized solemnizing officer, a marriage license, and a ceremony with witnesses).
- If any requisite is missing, the marriage is void from the beginning. Some defects make a marriage only voidable.
- Both parents share parental authority over their children.

**Family forms.** Sociology describes families by structure and by how a person enters them.
- **Nuclear family:** parents and children.
- **Extended family:** a household that also includes other relatives, such as grandparents, aunts and cousins.
- **Single-parent and blended families** are common variations.
- **Family of orientation:** the family you are born into.
- **Family of procreation:** the family formed through marriage.

**The family over time.** The *family life cycle* describes predictable stages, from a childless couple, through children of different ages, to launching and the "empty nest".

**Parenting styles.** Diana Baumrind's styles vary by **warmth** and **structure**. A fourth style, uninvolved, was added later by Maccoby and Martin.

**Before birth.** Prenatal development has three periods: germinal, embryonic and fetal. Organ formation happens in the embryonic period, and harmful substances (teratogens) can disrupt development.

**Protecting family members.** RA 7610 protects children, RA 9262 protects women and their children from violence in intimate and family relationships, and RA 10354 (the RH Law) defines adolescents as ages 10–19.

## 2. Testable facts

**Marriage under the Family Code (EO 209)**
- Marriage is "a special contract of permanent union between a man and a woman", entered into according to law to establish conjugal and family life; it is the foundation of the family. [SRC-0071, Art. 1]
- **Essential requisites:** (1) legal capacity of the contracting parties, who must be a male and a female; (2) **consent freely given** in the presence of the solemnizing officer. [SRC-0071, Art. 2]
- **Formal requisites:** (1) authority of the solemnizing officer; (2) a valid **marriage license**, except in cases the Code exempts; (3) a marriage ceremony with the parties appearing before the officer and declaring they take each other as spouses, before **at least two witnesses of legal age**. [SRC-0071, Art. 3]
- Absence of any essential or formal requisite makes the marriage **void ab initio** (void from the beginning), except as stated in Art. 35(2). [SRC-0071, Art. 4]
- Minimum age to marry: **18**. [SRC-0071, Art. 5]
- **Void** marriages include those where a party is **below 18**, even with parental consent; those solemnized by an unauthorized person (with a good-faith exception); those without a license (unless exempt); bigamous or polygamous marriages; and those contracted through mistake about the other party's identity. [SRC-0071, Art. 35]
- A marriage is also void if a party was **psychologically incapacitated** to fulfil the essential marital obligations, even if this shows only after the wedding. [SRC-0071, Art. 36]
- A marriage is **voidable** (may be annulled) for causes existing at the time of marriage. An example: a party aged **18 to below 21** married without parental consent, unless they freely cohabited after turning 21. [SRC-0071, Art. 45]
- **Parental authority:** the father and the mother **jointly** exercise it over their common children; in disagreement the father's decision prevails unless a court orders otherwise. [SRC-0071, Art. 211]
- Parents are **civilly liable** for injuries and damages caused by their unemancipated children living with them, subject to the defenses allowed by law. [SRC-0071, Art. 221]

**Family forms and the family life cycle**
- **Extended family:** a household with at least one parent and child plus other relatives such as grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins. [SRC-0088, 14.1]
- **Family of orientation:** the family a person is born into. **Family of procreation:** the family formed through marriage. [SRC-0088, 14.1]
- **Monogamy:** married to one person at a time. **Polygamy:** married to more than one at a time. [SRC-0088, 14.1]
- The **functionalist** view: families perform vital roles inside the family and for society. They provide for members' physical, emotional and social well-being; parents care for and socialize children; adult children often later care for elderly parents. [SRC-0088, 14.1]
- Single-parent families, blended families, foster families and cohabitation are variations sociology studies alongside the two-parent family. [SRC-0088, 14.2]
- The **family life cycle** idea began with **Paul Glick (1955)**: people grow up, form families, rear and launch children, experience an empty nest, and the cycle repeats. [SRC-0088, 14.1]
- **Evelyn Duvall's** classic stages: (1) marriage family, childless; (2) procreation family, children 0–2.5; (3) preschooler family, 2.5–6; (4) school-age family, 6–13; (5) teenage family, 13–20; (6) launching family, children begin to leave; (7) empty nest family. [SRC-0088, 14.1 Table 14.1]
- Each life-cycle stage has its own structure and challenges, and family success can be judged by how well the family adapts to each transition. [SRC-0088, 14.1]

**Parenting styles**
- **Diana Baumrind** identified three parenting styles that differ in **warmth** and **structure** (strictness). [SRC-0087, 6.3]
- **Authoritarian:** high structure (rules, boundaries, discipline), **low warmth**; rules-focused; directs and demands rather than negotiates. [SRC-0087, 6.3]
- **Authoritative:** moderate to high structure **and high warmth** and emotional support; relationship-focused; talks things through; more democratic and less rigid than authoritarian. [SRC-0087, 6.3]
- **Permissive:** **high warmth, low structure**; child-focused; avoids discipline and gives in to requests. [SRC-0087, 6.3]
- **Uninvolved:** **low structure, low warmth**; little attention, discipline or support; parent-focused. This fourth style was added by **Maccoby and Martin (1983)**, not Baumrind. [SRC-0087, 6.3]

**Prenatal development**
- Three periods of pregnancy: **germinal, embryonic, fetal**. [SRC-0087, 2.3]
- **Germinal:** the first and shortest, from conception to about **two weeks**. The zygote divides within 24 hours, becomes a **blastocyst** about 5–7 days later, and implants in the uterus. [SRC-0087, 2.3]
- **Embryonic:** from the **third to the eighth week**. The amnion, chorion, placenta and umbilical cord form, and **organogenesis** (organ formation) takes place. [SRC-0087, 2.3]
- **Fetal:** the last **seven months**, a period of organ growth and refinement in which the fetus grows quickly. [SRC-0087, 2.3]
- A teratogen's impact depends on the specific agent, the length and amount of exposure, genetic susceptibility, and the prenatal environment. Different body features have different sensitive periods. [SRC-0087, 2.3]

**Laws that protect family members**
- **Adolescent:** young people aged **10 to 19**, in transition from childhood to adulthood (RH Law). [SRC-0072, Sec. 4(b)]
- **Children** under RA 7610: persons **below 18**, or older but unable to protect themselves because of a disability or condition. [SRC-0029, Sec. 3(a)]
- **VAWC** includes physical, sexual and psychological harm and **economic abuse** by a husband, former husband, or partner in a dating or sexual relationship or with a common child. It covers the woman and her child. [SRC-0028, Sec. 3(a)]
- The Early Years Act treats ages **0 to 8** as the first crucial stage of educational development. [SRC-0074, title]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Essential vs formal requisites.** Essential = *who* and *consent* (legal capacity; consent freely given). Formal = *procedure* (officer's authority, license, ceremony with two witnesses). A missing license is a *formal* defect.
- **Void vs voidable.** Under 18 = void, even with parental consent. Ages 18–20 without parental consent = voidable (annullable). Psychological incapacity = void (Art. 36), not voidable.
- **Authoritarian vs authoritative.** Both have structure. Only *authoritative* adds high warmth. "Because I said so" with little warmth = authoritarian.
- **Uninvolved was not Baumrind's.** Baumrind had three styles; Maccoby and Martin added the fourth.
- **Prenatal weeks.** Germinal ≈ 0–2 weeks; embryonic = weeks 3–8; fetal = the rest (about seven months). Do not put organ formation in the fetal period; the fetal period *grows and refines* organs.
- **Adolescent age range.** The RH Law says 10–19. The Family Code minimum marriage age is 18. RA 7610's "child" is below 18. They answer different questions.
- **Family life cycle names differ by author.** Duvall's stages are one classic version. Exam items usually test the *order* (couple → young children → school age → teens → launching → empty nest).

## 4. Memory aid

**"License, Officer, Witnesses = Form; Capacity + Consent = Essence"**: the 3 formal and 2 essential requisites of marriage.
**Parenting grid "Warmth × Rules"**: high-high = Authoritative; low warmth + high rules = Authoritarian; high warmth + low rules = Permissive; low-low = Uninvolved.
**Prenatal "2-8-7"**: germinal ≈ 2 weeks, embryonic to week 8, fetal ≈ 7 months.
