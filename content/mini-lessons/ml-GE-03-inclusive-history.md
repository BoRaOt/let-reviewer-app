For a long time, Philippine history was told mainly as the story of lowland Christian communities under Spain and the United States. The **Integrated History Act of 2016 (RA 10908)** aims for an **inclusive history that accounts for all Filipinos**. It requires that the **history, culture and identity of Filipino-Muslims and Indigenous Peoples** be integrated into the grand narrative of Philippine history. Agencies writing curricula and textbooks must consult recognized experts on Filipino-Muslim and IP history.

This connects to how history shapes society. Language policy is an example. **Act No. 74 (1901)** made English the basis of public schooling, and **EO 134 (1937)** proclaimed a national language based on Tagalog. Each policy changed who could learn, read and take part in public life.

For a history teacher, inclusive history means using sources from many communities, such as Moro sultanates and Cordillera societies, not only colonial records.
Memory aid: "RA 10908: everyone's story, including Muslims and IPs."
