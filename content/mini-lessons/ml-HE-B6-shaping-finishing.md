Several sewing terms describe how flat fabric is shaped to the body. A dart is a fold that tapers to a point, stitched to remove extra fabric and give shape, for example at the bust or waist of a blouse. Gathering pulls a loose line of stitching so the edge bunches into many small, soft, irregular folds, as on a gathered skirt. A pleat is a deliberate fold made by doubling fabric back on itself and fixing it, so it looks neat and structured, unlike gathers. A hem finishes an edge: the edge is folded and sewn so it will not unravel, and it sets the garment's length. Two small tools help: a seam ripper cuts and removes unwanted stitches, and a tracing wheel transfers pattern markings onto fabric.
Memory aid: "Dart = Do-fit, Pleat = Pressed, Gather = Gentle bunch."
