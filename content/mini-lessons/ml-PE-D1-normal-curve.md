Many large sets of scores form a **normal distribution**: a symmetric bell with most scores near the middle and fewer at the extremes. In it, the mean, median and mode are the same.

The normal curve links standard deviations to percentages. About **34 percent** of scores lie between the mean and one SD above it, and another 34 percent between the mean and one SD below. So about **68 percent** fall within one SD of the mean. About **14 percent** lie between one and two SDs above the mean, and only about **2 percent** lie beyond two SDs above.

Percentiles follow. A learner at the mean is at the **50th percentile**. A learner one SD above the mean is at about the **84th percentile**, because 50 plus 34 percent of scores are below. On an IQ test (mean 100, SD 15), 115 is about the 84th percentile.
Memory aid: "34–14–2 each side; mean = 50th, +1 SD = 84th."
