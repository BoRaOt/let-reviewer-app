Everyday crafts are also local industries. The **banig** is a handwoven mat for sleeping or sitting, usually made of **pandan or sedge leaves**, and in some places of buri palm, reed or rattan. The leaves are dried, often dyed, cut into strips and woven into colorful patterns. The Waray of **Basey, Samar** are known for banig woven from **tikog**. Long ago, pandan mats even served as boat sails.

**Capiz shell** comes from the windowpane oyster. Its thin, translucent shell has long been used as a **substitute for glass** in windows and is now made into lamps, lanterns and decor.

Crafts like these support families and towns. The Manlilikha ng Bayan law itself provides for vocational courses based on master artists' methods and for studies on turning their art into **cottage industries**. In class, DIY projects can use these local materials.
