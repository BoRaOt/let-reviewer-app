# PE-B2 — Methods and strategies of teaching

Section B is 45 items; our working split gives this line about **15 items**. Outcomes: **new literacies** (multicultural, social, media, financial, cyber/digital, eco, arts and creativity) and **developmentally sequenced lesson plans** on the K to 12 spiral curriculum. [SRC-0001, Annex A, ProfEd B] With 25 Apply items in Section B, expect "which strategy best fits this class" scenarios.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Two families of strategies.** **Teacher-directed** (lecture with advance organizers, mastery learning, direct instruction, Madeline Hunter's effective teaching model) organize content for the learner. **Student-centered** (independent study, inquiry/discovery, cooperative learning, project-based learning) let learners direct more of the work. Good teachers mix them.

**Choosing.** Choose by the kind of thinking you want, how much the content must be organized for learners, and how much responsibility learners can take.

**New literacies** extend "reading and writing" to media, digital tools, money, the environment, other cultures and social settings. Each is a set of skills: to **access, analyze, evaluate and create**.

**Lesson sequencing** follows K to 12's spiral progression (RA 10533): start from what learners know, move to the unknown, and revisit at greater depth.

## 2. Testable facts

**Teacher-directed strategies (Seifert & Sutton Ch. 9)**
- **Lecture/exposition:** telling or explaining previously organized information. Students may look passive but must make an effort to listen to learn. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Lectures and readings"]
- **Advance organizers (Ausubel, 1978):** brief verbal or graphic overviews of material before it is presented, which partially organize the material for students. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Advance organizers"]
- **Concept maps:** diagrams of relationships among concepts, usually made by the student. Outlining and note-taking also help learners organize expository material. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 Table 24 and "Concept maps"]
- **Mastery learning:** **all students learn to the same high level**, though some take longer. Typical cycle: brief lesson → **ungraded test** → enrichment for those who mastered it, **tutoring or extra materials** for others → retest. Challenges: fairness of enrichment vs remediation, and the need for a detailed, organized curriculum of small units. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Mastery learning"]
- **Direct instruction** (often): a **highly scripted** version of mastery learning (Engelmann) mixing behaviorism (immediate explicit praise) and cognitive theory (stated objectives, frequent review); small logical steps with ample practice. Very effective for **basic skills** such as early reading and arithmetic, especially in **K–3**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Direct instruction"]
- **Madeline Hunter's effective teaching model (mastery teaching):** **prepare students to learn** (use the start of the lesson, advance organizers, explicit objectives); **present information clearly** (a set structure, familiar examples, concise); **check for understanding and give guided practice** (questions everyone answers, choral responses, sampling individuals); **provide for independent practice** (do the first few together; keep practice brief with feedback). [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 Table 25]

**Student-centered strategies**
- **Inquiry learning** (also called discovery learning, Bruner; progressive education, Dewey): the teacher poses thoughtful questions and **does not answer them directly**, guiding students to investigate. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Inquiry learning"]
- **Cooperative learning:** groups rewarded partly or wholly for **group success**. Students need time and a place to work together. If a group grade goes to the whole group only, **freeloading** is possible; if only to individual contributions, **overspecialization** can occur. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Cooperative learning"]
- **Jigsaw (Aronson & Patnoe; Slavin):** each member becomes an **expert** on one part in an expert group, then teaches it back to the home group. **STAD (Student Teams–Achievement Divisions, Slavin):** teams of 4–5 study together; students are tested individually and **scored partly on improvement**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 Table 26; Ch. 6 jigsaw passage]
- **Project-based learning (Katz):** a question or problem of interest is investigated together over **extended periods**, ending in a presentation, report or product. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 Table 26; Ch. 6 jigsaw passage]
- Choosing a strategy depends on the forms of thinking to encourage, how much ideas must be organized by the teacher, and how much **responsibility** students should take for directing their own learning. [SRC-0109, Ch. 9 "Instructional strategies: an abundance of choices"]

**New literacies**
- **Media literacy:** the ability to **access, analyze, evaluate and create** media in various forms, and to reflect critically and **act ethically**. [SRC-0182, lead and goals paragraph]
- **Digital literacy:** the ability to **find, evaluate and communicate** information using digital devices and platforms; first defined by **Paul Gilster (1997)**. **Henry Jenkins** distinguished the **participation gap** from the digital divide. [SRC-0184, lead, "History", "Participation gap"]
- **Financial literacy:** the skills, knowledge, behavior and attitude that let a person make **informed decisions about money**. [SRC-0183, lead]
- **Ecological literacy (ecoliteracy):** understanding the natural systems that make life possible and using ecological principles to build **sustainable communities**; developed by **David Orr and Fritjof Capra** in the 1990s. [SRC-0185, lead]
- **Social literacy** (sociocultural view): literacy learned through **social interaction** with peers and adults, using books, games, conversations, field trips and stories. [SRC-0187, lead]
- **Multicultural education:** strategies that teach the histories, cultures and contributions of diverse groups. **James Banks's** dimensions: **content integration, knowledge construction, prejudice reduction, equity pedagogy, empowering school culture**. [SRC-0186, lead and "Implementation"]

**Sequencing lessons in K to 12**
- The curriculum uses the **spiral progression** approach and pedagogies that are **constructivist, inquiry-based, reflective, collaborative and integrative**; it proceeds **from the known to the unknown**. [SRC-0021, Sec. 5(e)–(g)]
- Objectives should be sequenced through the cognitive levels (revised Bloom: **Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyze → Evaluate → Create**). [SRC-0179, "Cognitive"]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Mastery learning ≠ Hunter's mastery teaching.** Mastery learning = everyone reaches the same high level with variable time; Hunter's model = a lesson-delivery sequence.
- **Direct instruction is not "lecture".** It is scripted, small-step, practice-heavy and praise-rich.
- **Inquiry: the teacher withholds the answer** and asks further questions.
- **Jigsaw = expert groups; STAD = improvement points.**
- **Advance organizer comes before** the new material, not after.
- **Media literacy includes creating** media, not only analyzing it.
- **Prejudice reduction and equity pedagogy are different Banks dimensions**: attitudes vs teaching methods that help diverse groups achieve.

## 4. Memory aid

**Hunter "Prepare, Present, Check, Practice."**
**Banks "CK-PEE"**: Content integration, Knowledge construction, Prejudice reduction, Equity pedagogy, Empowering school culture.
**Media literacy "AAECA"**: Access, Analyze, Evaluate, Create, Act.
