**Madeline Hunter** combined several direct-instruction ideas into one lesson model, called **mastery teaching** or the effective teaching model. It has four parts.
1. **Prepare students to learn.** Use the first minutes, when attention is highest. Point ahead with an advance organizer and state the objective clearly.
2. **Present information clearly and explicitly.** Keep one structure, use familiar words and examples, and be concise.
3. **Check for understanding and give guided practice.** Ask questions everyone answers, for example by raising hands, invite choral responses, and sample individuals.
4. **Provide for independent practice.** Do the first few items together, keep solo practice short, and mix in discussion and feedback.

The model needs content broken into small, clear parts. Do not confuse Hunter's **mastery teaching** with **mastery learning**, which is about everyone reaching the same standard with extra time.
Memory aid: "Prepare, Present, Check, Practice."
