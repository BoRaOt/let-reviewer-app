**Metaethics** asks what moral claims are and whether they can be true. Two broad answers dominate.

**Moral realism** holds that there are objective moral facts. "Torturing innocents for fun is wrong" is true no matter what anyone believes, much as a fact in science is true.

**Moral relativism** is an **anti-realist** position. It denies that there is any objective or universal justification for moral beliefs; moral claims are true only relative to a culture, group or person. Relativism appears often in debates about politics and religion.

Ongoing moral disagreement is a challenge for realism, because it makes morality look subjective. But disagreement alone does not prove relativism: people also disagree about facts that have objective answers.

On the exam, "What is right depends on the culture" signals relativism; "Some things are right or wrong for everyone" signals realism or universalism.
Memory aid: "Realism: right is real; Relativism: right is relative."
