The Anti-Sexual Harassment Act (RA 7877) covers work, education and training settings. In a school, the offender is a teacher, instructor, professor, coach or trainer who has authority, influence or moral ascendancy over the student. That person commits sexual harassment by demanding, requesting or otherwise requiring a sexual favor, whether or not the student gives in. The act needs no physical contact, and no acceptance by the student. The Safe Spaces Act (RA 11313) widened protection against gender-based sexual harassment. Every school, public or private, must designate an officer to receive complaints and must adopt and publish grievance procedures. School heads must post a copy of the law, run prevention campaigns, and set up an independent body to investigate complaints. School heads may themselves be liable if they fail to act.
Memory aid: "Power plus a demand equals harassment — the answer does not matter."
Sources: SRC-0026 Sec. 3; SRC-0027 Secs. 21–23
