# GE-01 — Purposive Communication in English

GenEd unit, **15 items** (10%). Outcomes: use English **with ease in effective communication**; use English **confidently for special purposes**. [SRC-0001, Annex A, GE] Expect sentence-correction items, register and audience scenarios, and identification of rhetorical appeals and figures of speech.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Communication models.** **Linear** models (Lasswell, Shannon–Weaver, Berlo) treat communication as one-way, sender → receiver. **Interaction** models add **feedback**. **Transactional** models (Barnlund) see both parties sending and receiving at once.

**Purpose and audience.** Every message sits in a **rhetorical situation**: an issue (exigence), an audience, and constraints. Choose a **register** to fit: **frozen, formal, consultative, casual, intimate** (Joos).

**Persuasion.** **Ethos** (credibility), **pathos** (emotion), **logos** (evidence and reasoning), **kairos** (timing).

**Grammar and usage.** Subjects and verbs agree; words between them don't change agreement; with *or/nor* the verb agrees with the nearer subject; *each, everyone, someone* take singular verbs. Commas set off **nonessential** information.

**Figures of speech.** Simile (*like/as*), metaphor (implicit comparison), personification, hyperbole, metonymy/synecdoche, alliteration, onomatopoeia, oxymoron, irony.

## 2. Testable facts

**Communication models**
- **Linear transmission models** see communication as a **one-way** process from sender to receiver; all early models were linear: **Lasswell's, Shannon–Weaver, Gerbner's, Berlo's**. [SRC-0218, "Classifications — Linear transmission"]
- **Interaction models** include a **feedback loop**: the receiver decodes the message and responds. [SRC-0218, "Classifications — Interaction"]
- From the 1970s, **transactional models** such as **Barnlund's** were proposed to overcome the limits of interaction models; non-linear models are **multi-directional**. [SRC-0218, "Classifications — Transaction"]

**Register**
- A **register** is a variety of language used for a particular purpose or communicative situation; registers form a **spectrum**. [SRC-0216, lead]
- **Martin Joos's five styles:** **frozen** (unchanging printed language, e.g. pledges, scripture); **formal** (one-way, no interruption, technical vocabulary, e.g. presentations); **consultative** (two-way, background given, back-channel "uh huh", e.g. teacher–student, doctor–patient); **casual** (in-group, ellipsis and slang); **intimate** (private, family and close friends, intonation over wording). [SRC-0216, "History and use"]

**Rhetoric (OpenStax Writing Guide 9.3; Bitzer)**
- **Ethos:** a believable, authoritative voice that earns **credibility** and trust. **Pathos:** appeals to **feelings and emotions** shared by an audience. **Logos:** credible **facts, reasons or examples** presented as evidence toward a sensible conclusion. **Kairos:** the sense of **appropriate timing**. [SRC-0104, 9.3]
- A rhetorical analysis addresses the **rhetorical situation**, the conditions of communication surrounding the rhetoric. [SRC-0104, 9.3]
- **Lloyd Bitzer (1968)**: a rhetorical situation consists of an **issue (exigence), an audience, and constraints**; Richard Vatz answered with "The Myth of the Rhetorical Situation". [SRC-0215, lead]

**Grammar and usage (OpenStax Writing Guide)**
- The **subject and verb must agree**; the verb agrees even when **words and phrases come between** them ("The **price** of these shoes at all stores **is** unbelievable"). [SRC-0104, 6.6]
- With compound subjects joined by **or/nor**, the verb agrees with the **subject closest** to it. [SRC-0104, 6.6]
- Indefinite pronouns taking **singular** verbs: **anybody, anyone, anything, each, either, everybody, everyone, everything, neither, nobody, no one, nothing, one, somebody, someone, something**. [SRC-0104, 6.6]
- **Nonessential information** is not necessary to understand the sentence and is set off with **commas** ("The current math curriculum, which was adopted ten years ago, no longer meets…"); **essential information** is necessary and is not set off. [SRC-0104, 8.6]
- Short choppy sentences can be combined into a **complex sentence** with a **dependent clause**. [SRC-0104, 3.6]

**Figures of speech**
- **Simile:** compares unlike things using **like** or **as**. **Metaphor:** compares without marking the comparison. **Personification:** human traits given to non-human things ("The moon smiled down"). **Hyperbole:** deliberate exaggeration. [SRC-0217, "Narrative devices", "Poetic and sound-based devices", "Rhetorical and argumentative devices"]
- **Metonymy and synecdoche:** referring to something by a related object or a part. **Alliteration:** repetition of an initial consonant sound. **Onomatopoeia:** words imitating sounds. **Oxymoron:** a two-word paradox of antonyms. **Irony:** language presenting something opposite to expectations. [SRC-0217, "Narrative devices", "Poetic and sound-based devices", "Rhetorical and argumentative devices"]

**Media literacy**
- Media literacy includes the ability to **access, analyze, evaluate and create** media and to act ethically. [SRC-0182, lead]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **"Along with / as well as" phrases don't change the verb:** "The teacher, along with her students, **is** coming."
- **Each/everyone = singular:** "Each of the students **has** a book."
- **Either/or:** "Neither the teacher nor the **students are** ready" (nearer subject *students*).
- **Which vs that:** nonessential clauses take commas (usually *which*); essential clauses take no commas.
- **Simile vs metaphor:** "like/as" = simile.
- **Consultative vs formal:** a class discussion with back-and-forth is consultative; a speech is formal.
- **Ethos ≠ ethics:** ethos is the speaker's credibility.

## 4. Memory aid

**Joos "Fancy Folks Consult Casually, Intimately"** = Frozen, Formal, Consultative, Casual, Intimate.
**Appeals "Credibility, Feelings, Facts, Timing" = Ethos, Pathos, Logos, Kairos.**
