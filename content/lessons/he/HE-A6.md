# HE-A6 — Entrepreneurship

Annex F gives entrepreneurship only **3 items**: Understand, Apply and Analyze, one each [SRC-0002, Annex F, A.6]. Do not over-study it. The outcomes are:
- evaluate business opportunities based on market needs and trends;
- analyze financial statements, manage budgets and assess startup funding;
- apply ethical business practices, **corporate social responsibility (CSR)** and sustainability.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Opportunity.** Not every idea is a business. An idea becomes an **entrepreneurial opportunity** when there is identifiable customer demand *and* it is feasible to meet that demand.

**Money.** A startup reads three core financial statements: the **balance sheet**, the **income statement** and the **statement of cash flows**. The **break-even point** tells how many units must be sold to cover fixed costs.

**Responsibility.** **CSR** means a business sees itself as a member of society with social and environmental obligations, not only a money-maker.

**Philippine forms and laws.**
- Small Home Economics businesses are often sole proprietorships, partnerships, cooperatives or small corporations.
- Philippine law sets the sizes of micro, small and medium enterprises (RA 9501) and gives incentives to barangay micro business enterprises (BMBEs, RA 9178).
- The Cooperative Code defines cooperatives.
- The Revised Corporation Code allows up to 15 incorporators and creates the One Person Corporation.

## 2. Testable facts

- **Entrepreneurial opportunity:** the point where **identifiable consumer demand** meets the **feasibility** of satisfying it. Specific criteria must be met to move from an idea to an opportunity. [SRC-0098, 5.1]
- The three primary financial statements are the **balance sheet, income statement and statement of cash flows**. [SRC-0098, 9.4]
- The statement of cash flows **bridges** the income statement and the balance sheet. Its first section, **operating activities**, covers day-to-day activities such as buying supplies, paying rent and receiving cash from customers. [SRC-0098, 9.4]
- **Contribution margin** = selling price − variable cost per item. [SRC-0098, 9.4]
- **Break-even point (units)** = total fixed costs ÷ contribution margin. Example: price P12, variable cost P4, so the margin is P8; with fixed rent of P2,000, the break-even is 2,000 ÷ 8 = **250 units**. The source uses dollars; the arithmetic is the same. [SRC-0098, 9.4]
- A **business plan** is a formal document for long-range planning, with background, financial information and a summary of the business; investors nearly always ask for one. [SRC-0098, 11.4]
- **CSR**: a business views itself as a member of society with implicit **social obligations and environmental considerations**. Legal compliance and ethical responsibility differ, because the law does not settle every ethical dilemma. [SRC-0098, 3.2]
- **BMBE:** a business entity whose total assets (excluding land) are **not more than P3,000,000**. [SRC-0064, Sec. 3(a)]
- **MSME** asset categories: **micro** not more than P3,000,000; **small** P3,000,001–P15,000,000; **medium** from P15,000,001. MSMEs may be single proprietorships, cooperatives, partnerships or corporations. [SRC-0065, Sec. 3]
- **Cooperative:** an autonomous, duly registered association of persons with a **common bond of interest** who voluntarily join to meet social, economic and cultural needs. Members make equitable capital contributions, patronize its services, and share risks and benefits fairly. [SRC-0066, Art. 3]
- Cooperative principles include **voluntary and open membership** and **democratic member control**, with **one member, one vote** in primary cooperatives. [SRC-0066, Art. 4]
- A corporation may be organized by **not more than 15** incorporators. A **One Person Corporation** is allowed under the Revised Corporation Code. [SRC-0067, Sec. 10]
- A corporation has **perpetual existence** unless its articles of incorporation provide otherwise. [SRC-0067, Sec. 11]
- **Variable costs** rise and fall with how much is made or sold (e.g. ingredients for each pizza). **Fixed costs** such as rent stay the same every month whatever the sales. [SRC-0098, 9.4]
- The **executive summary** gives an overview of the whole business plan; it is best **written last**, even though it comes first in the document. [SRC-0098, 11.4]
- The basic business structures are **sole proprietorships, partnerships and corporations**, plus hybrids; many owners who want **limited liability** form a corporation (or a similar hybrid). [SRC-0098, 13.1]
- The **marketing mix (4 Ps)**: **Product** = the good or service offered; **Price** = what the consumer pays; **Place** = where the product is purchased; **Promotion** = advertising, sales and other communication used to attract customers. [SRC-0099, 1.2]
- An entrepreneurial mindset includes being curious and open, taking **calculated risks**, and being **resourceful and resilient** when facing obstacles; entrepreneurs must **persevere** through challenges. [SRC-0098, 2.1]
- Entrepreneurship takes energy, decision-making skills, **tenacity, resourcefulness and flexibility**. [SRC-0098, 2.3]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Idea ≠ opportunity.** An opportunity needs both demand *and* feasibility.
- **Contribution margin uses variable costs only.** Fixed costs are covered by dividing them by the margin.
- **Cooperative voting** is one member, one vote, not by size of capital.
- **BMBE vs MSME.** BMBE is the P3M-asset category with special incentives under RA 9178. Micro also tops out at P3M under RA 9501. Know which law the question names.
- **CSR is more than legal compliance.**
- **Place is not just a shop.** In the 4 Ps, place means where (and through what channel) the customer buys; advertising belongs to promotion.
- **Fixed costs stay fixed in total**; variable costs follow output.

## 4. Memory aid

**"Break-even = Fixed ÷ (Price − Variable)."**
**"Demand + Doable = Opportunity."**
**4 Ps "What, How much, Where, How they hear": Product, Price, Place, Promotion.**
