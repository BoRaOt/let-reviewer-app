# GE-04 — The Life and Works of Rizal

GenEd unit, **15 items** (10%). Outcome: show how **Rizal's thoughts, works and valor** influenced the nation and other heroes.

## 1. Plain explanation

**The Rizal Law (RA 1425, 1956)** requires courses on Rizal's life, works and writings — especially the **Noli** and the **Fili** — in all schools, colleges and universities, using **unexpurgated** editions in college.

**Life.** Born **June 19, 1861**, in **Calamba, Laguna**, the **seventh child** of Francisco Rizal Mercado and Teodora Alonso. Studied at the **Ateneo Municipal**, then UST, then the **Universidad Central de Madrid**; became an **ophthalmologist**.

**Propaganda Movement.** In Europe he wrote for **La Solidaridad** under pen names (**Dimasalang, Laong Laan**). He annotated **Morga's** history of the Philippines.

**Novels.** **Noli Me Tángere** (Berlin, **1887**; printed with help from **Máximo Viola**; hero **Crisóstomo Ibarra**). **El Filibusterismo** (Ghent, **1891**; Ibarra returns as **Simoun**).

**La Liga Filipina** (Tondo, **July 3, 1892**) → arrested **July 6, 1892** → exile in **Dapitan** (1892–1896), where he met **Josephine Bracken**.

**Death.** Tried by **court-martial** for **rebellion, sedition and conspiracy**; shot at **Bagumbayan (Luneta)** on **December 30, 1896**. His farewell poem, later called **Mi Último Adiós**, was smuggled out in an alcohol stove/lamp.

**Influence.** The Katipunan was founded the night after his arrest (July 7, 1892).

## 2. Testable facts

**The Rizal Law (RA 1425)**
- Courses on the **life, works and writings of José Rizal**, particularly the **Noli Me Tangere** and **El Filibusterismo**, shall be included in the curricula of **all schools, colleges and universities, public or private**; collegiate courses use the **original or unexpurgated editions or their English translation** as basic texts. [SRC-0013, Sec. 1]
- All schools, colleges and universities must keep in their libraries an adequate number of copies of the **unexpurgated** Noli and Fili and Rizal's other works and biography. [SRC-0013, Sec. 2]

**Life and education**
- **José Protasio Rizal Mercado y Alonso Realonda**, born **June 19, 1861**, died **December 30, 1896**; a nationalist, writer and polymath. [SRC-0219, lead]
- Born in **Calamba, Laguna**, the **seventh child** of **Francisco Rizal Mercado** and **Teodora Alonso Realonda**. [SRC-0219, infobox and "Early life and education"]
- Studied at the **Ateneo Municipal de Manila** (AB), the **University of Santo Tomas**, and the **Universidad Central de Madrid** (enrolled **November 1882**; medicine). [SRC-0219, infobox and "Travel to Europe"]
- An **ophthalmologist** by profession, he became a key member of the **Propaganda Movement** of the 1880s, which sought political reforms under Spain. [SRC-0219, lead]

**Propaganda and writings**
- He wrote essays, poems and editorials for **La Solidaridad** (Barcelona) under the pen names **"Dimasalang", "Laong Laan" and "May Pagasa"**. [SRC-0219, "In Brussels and Spain (1890–1892)"]
- He studied **Morga's** writings on the Philippines while in London (British Museum). [SRC-0219, "Personal life, relationships, and ventures"]
- His poem **"A la Juventud Filipina"** ("To the Filipino Youth") is among his noted works. [SRC-0219, "Education — Tertiary"]

**Noli Me Tángere**
- "**Touch Me Not**" (Latin); finished in **March 1887**; printed in **Berlin** with financial help from **Máximo Viola**. [SRC-0220, lead and "Publication history"]
- Protagonist **Crisóstomo Ibarra** returns to **San Diego, Laguna** after **seven years** in Europe. [SRC-0220, "Plot"]

**El Filibusterismo**
- First published in **1891** in **Ghent**. [SRC-0221, lead]
- Ibarra returns for vengeance under the name **Simoun**. [SRC-0221, lead]
- The Cavite Mutiny of 1872 led to the execution of **Gomburza**, whose deaths shaped the reform movement. [SRC-0200, "Execution of Gomburza"]

**La Liga, arrest and exile**
- **La Liga Filipina** was founded by Rizal on **July 3, 1892**, in the house of **Doroteo Ongjunco, Ilaya Street, Tondo**; it grew out of La Solidaridad and the Propaganda Movement. [SRC-0223, lead]
- On **July 6, 1892**, Rizal was arrested for alleged possession of a seditious document and **banished to Dapitan**, Mindanao. [SRC-0219, lead]
- The **Katipunan** was founded on the night of **July 7, 1892**, following Rizal's arrest. [SRC-0208, "Formation"]
- In Dapitan he met **Josephine Bracken**, his common-law wife. After four years he left for Cuba in **August 1896** with Governor-General **Ramón Blanco's** permission, to serve as a doctor. [SRC-0219, lead]

**Trial and death**
- Tried before a **court-martial** for **rebellion, sedition and conspiracy**, he was convicted and sentenced to death. [SRC-0219, "Arrest and trial"]
- Executed on **December 30, 1896**, by a firing squad of Filipino soldiers of the Spanish Army, with Spanish troops standing by; his remains later went to **Bagumbayan (Luneta)**, site of the Rizal Monument. [SRC-0219, "Execution", "Exhumation and re-burial"]
- His farewell poem (written **1896**, later titled **Mi Último Adiós**) was hidden in an **alcohol stove/lamp** given to his family on **December 29, 1896**. [SRC-0222, lead and "Background"]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Noli = Berlin 1887 (Viola); Fili = Ghent 1891.** Don't swap the cities.
- **Simoun is Ibarra**, not a new character.
- **La Liga ≠ Katipunan.** La Liga (Rizal, July 3, 1892) was reformist; Katipunan (Bonifacio and others, July 7, 1892) was revolutionary.
- **Rizal Law covers all schools, colleges and universities**, not only college.
- **Execution date December 30, 1896**; the poem was handed over December 29.
- **Pen names:** Dimasalang and Laong Laan are Rizal's (Plaridel is del Pilar's — not sourced here; do not assume).

## 4. Memory aid

**Rizal timeline "61-82-87-91-92-96":** born 1861, Madrid 1882, Noli 1887, Fili 1891, Liga and Dapitan 1892, death 1896.
**"Berlin begins, Ghent ends."**
