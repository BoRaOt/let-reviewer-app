The Family Code calls marriage a **special contract of permanent union** and the **foundation of the family**, an institution the State protects. Families take many forms. A nuclear family is parents and children; an **extended family** adds relatives such as grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins, which is common in Filipino homes.

Conflict is a normal part of family life, especially when children reach adolescence. Popular media suggest teens and parents fight constantly, but research shows many families stay warm and close, particularly in **authoritative** homes that combine warmth with clear rules. Most parent–teen conflict comes from the teen's growing **autonomy**, sharper reasoning and new roles. It is part of how the relationship is renegotiated, not a sign the family is failing.

Healthy conflict resolution means listening, explaining reasons, and giving older children more say step by step.
Memory aid: "Warm + firm = calmer teens; conflict is renegotiation, not rejection."
