Most car and motorcycle gasoline engines are **four-stroke** engines, also called the **Otto cycle** after Nicolaus Otto. The piston moves up or down four times to complete one cycle, which takes **two turns of the crankshaft**.
1. **Intake:** the piston moves down and the intake valve lets in the air-fuel mixture.
2. **Compression:** both valves close and the piston moves up, squeezing the mixture.
3. **Power (combustion):** the spark ignites the mixture, and the expanding gas pushes the piston down. This is the only stroke that produces work.
4. **Exhaust:** the piston moves up again and the exhaust valve releases the burned gases.

Then the cycle repeats. Problems in a step show up as symptoms: poor compression means weak power, and a blocked exhaust makes the engine struggle. Automotive servicing starts from knowing which stroke is affected.
Memory aid: "Suck, Squeeze, Bang, Blow."
