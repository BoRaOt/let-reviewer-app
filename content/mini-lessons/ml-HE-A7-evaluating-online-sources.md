Before using a website in class, test it. The **CRAAP test**, made by librarian Sarah Blakeslee in 2004, has five checks.
- **Currency:** Is it recent enough for the topic?
- **Relevance:** Does it fit your question and your learners?
- **Authority:** Who wrote or published it, and are they qualified?
- **Accuracy:** Is it supported by evidence, and can it be checked?
- **Purpose:** Why does it exist: to inform, teach, sell or persuade?

Some librarians find checklists too slow and prefer **SIFT**: Stop; Investigate the source; Find better coverage; Trace claims, quotes and media back to the original. Research shows that students, and even historians, are often fooled by official-looking logos and web addresses, while professional fact-checkers reach sounder conclusions much faster by checking other sources.

For an HE teacher, a recipe blog selling supplements fails "Purpose".
Memory aid: "CRAAP before you copy; SIFT when you are in a hurry."
