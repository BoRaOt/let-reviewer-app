Fabrics are built in three main ways. Weaving interlaces two sets of yarns at right angles on a loom: the lengthwise warp and the crosswise weft. Knitting uses one continuous yarn, looped row after row, so knitted fabric stretches well, as in T-shirts. Felt is neither woven nor knitted. Fibers such as wool are matted, condensed and pressed together. In wet felting, hot water plus rubbing and pressing makes wool fibers lock. That is also why a wool sweater shrinks and felts if it is machine-washed hot, so wool is washed gently in cool water. Finishes can change a fabric. Mercerisation, devised by John Mercer in 1844, treats cotton so it takes dye better, becomes stronger and gains a silk-like luster.
Memory aid: "Weave = Cross, Knit = Loop, Felt = Press."
