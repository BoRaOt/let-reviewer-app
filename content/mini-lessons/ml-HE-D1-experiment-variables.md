An experiment tests whether changing one thing causes a change in another. The variable the researcher changes is the **explanatory variable**, also called the independent variable. The variable measured is the **response variable**, or dependent variable. For example, if a teacher compares two ways of teaching knife skills, the teaching method is explanatory and the skills score is the response.

Other factors can cloud the result; these are **lurking variables**, such as prior cooking experience at home. Good design isolates the explanatory variable, usually by **random assignment** to groups.

A **control group** gets no treatment or the usual treatment, so the effect of simply being studied is balanced out. **Blinding** keeps participants from knowing which treatment they got, which counters the power of suggestion. In a **double-blind** study, neither the participants nor the researchers working with them know.
Memory aid: "Explain → Response; Control the rest; Blind the bias."
