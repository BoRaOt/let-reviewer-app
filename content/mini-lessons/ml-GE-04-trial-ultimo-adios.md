Brought back to Manila, Rizal was tried before a **court-martial** for **rebellion, sedition and conspiracy**. Though he had not led the Revolution, he was convicted of all three charges and sentenced to death.

On the afternoon of **December 29, 1896**, his mother and sisters visited him in his cell. As they left, he told his sister Trinidad that there was something in the small **alcohol stove**, or lamp. Inside was his farewell poem, written in 1896 and later titled **Mi Último Adiós**. It was found after his death.

On the morning of **December 30, 1896**, Rizal was shot at **Bagumbayan**, now Luneta or Rizal Park, by a firing squad of Filipino soldiers in the Spanish Army, with Spanish troops ready to shoot them if they refused. His remains later rested at the site of the Rizal Monument.

His death strengthened the Revolution rather than ending it.
