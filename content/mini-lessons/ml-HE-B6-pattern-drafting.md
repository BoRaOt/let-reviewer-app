Pattern work moves from basic to specific. First the dressmaker drafts a **basic or block pattern**, also called a sloper, such as a bodice, skirt or trouser block fitted to the client. Then the block is **manipulated** into the chosen style. Finally the pattern is labeled, filed and cut, **with or without seam allowance**. Changing a finished pattern to other sizes is called grading.

Drafting tools listed by TESDA are tailor's chalk, pattern paper, a ruler with grid, a 45°–90° triangle, a hip curve, a French curve and a pencil. Scissors are the cutting tool.

Patterns may be drafted flat from measurements, or made by **draping** cloth on a dress form.

Learn the symbols: a long **arrow** is the grainline and must lie parallel to the straight grain; **notches** mark matching points and seam allowances; **holes** mark darts, pockets and buttonholes; **double lines** show where to lengthen or shorten.
Memory aid: "Block first, style second, arrow on the grain."
