B. F. Skinner built on Thorndike's law of effect: consequences change how often a behavior happens. Reinforcement makes a behavior more likely, and punishment makes it less likely. "Positive" means something is added, and "negative" means something is removed.
- Praising a student for finished homework is positive reinforcement.
- Excusing a student from a quiz because they joined every activity removes something unpleasant to increase participation. That is negative reinforcement, not punishment.
- Extra chores for misbehaving is positive punishment.
- Taking away recess is negative punishment.

Reinforcement can follow different schedules. A variable-ratio schedule rewards after an unpredictable number of responses, as in gambling. It produces a high, steady rate of behavior and the slowest extinction once rewards stop.
Memory aid: "Reinforce rises, punish drops; plus adds, minus takes."
