# GE-05 — The Contemporary World

GenEd unit, **15 items** (10%). Outcomes: **relate global issues to local realities**; identify ways the **Philippines can help mitigate global problems**. [SRC-0001, Annex A, GE]

## 1. Plain explanation

**Globalization** integrates governments, cultures and financial markets through international trade into a single world market. It is driven by the global economy and **multinational corporations**; it brings growth and jobs to some and **outsourcing** job losses to others.

**Explaining global inequality.** **Wallerstein's world-systems approach** sorts countries into **core**, **semi-periphery** and **periphery**. **Modernization theory** says poor countries develop by industrializing and changing values; **dependency theory** says core nations keep others poor by exploiting them.

**Population and environment.** **Malthus** predicted war, famine and disease would check population; **demographic transition** theory describes societies moving from high birth and death rates to low ones.

**Global and regional institutions.** The **UN** (1945) and its six principal organs; the **SDGs** (17 goals, adopted 2015, for 2030). **ASEAN** (1967, Bangkok Declaration; the Philippines is a founding member).

**The Philippines in the world.** An **independent foreign policy** (Constitution Art. II Sec. 7); **OFWs** and remittances; the **West Philippine Sea** and the 2016 arbitral award; climate action through RA 9729.

## 2. Testable facts

**Globalization (OpenStax Sociology 3e)**
- **Globalization** is the process of integrating governments, cultures and financial markets through international trade into a **single world market**; it often begins with a single motive such as market expansion. [SRC-0088, 18.2]
- Forces driving globalization include the global economy and **multinational corporations** that control assets, sales, production and employment. [SRC-0088, 18.2]
- **Outsourcing** of manufacturing and service jobs to developing countries has raised unemployment in some developed countries. [SRC-0088, 18.2]

**Global stratification**
- **Cold War terminology** (1945–1980) classified countries as **first, second and third world** by economic development and living standards. [SRC-0088, 10.1]
- The **World Bank** classifies countries by **gross national income per capita** (e.g. high-income threshold of US$12,536 in 2019). High-income countries face **capital flight** and **deindustrialization**. [SRC-0088, 10.1]
- **Wallerstein's world-systems approach (1979):** **core nations** are dominant, industrialized capitalist countries; **semi-peripheral** nations are in-between, supplying raw materials and markets to the core while exploiting the periphery (e.g. Mexico); **peripheral** nations have little industrialization. [SRC-0088, 10.1]
- **Modernization theory:** low-income countries improve through **adjusting cultural values** about work and **industrialization**; critics call it **ethnocentric**. **Dependency theory:** global inequality is caused mainly by **core nations exploiting** semi-peripheral and peripheral nations. [SRC-0088, 10.3]

**Population (Sociology 20.1)**
- **Thomas Malthus** (1766–1834): population exceeding the earth's **carrying capacity** is controlled by **war, famine and disease** (1798). [SRC-0088, 20.1]
- Other population theories: **zero population growth**, **cornucopian**, **demographic transition**; in Stage 1 of the demographic transition, birth, death and infant mortality rates are all **high** and life expectancy short. [SRC-0088, 20.1]

**United Nations and SDGs**
- The **UN** was established by the **UN Charter signed 26 June 1945**; it came into existence on **24 October 1945**; headquarters in **New York**; **193 member states**. [SRC-0194, lead and infobox]
- Six principal organs: **General Assembly, Security Council, Economic and Social Council, International Court of Justice, Secretariat, Trusteeship Council**. [SRC-0194, lead]
- The **17 Sustainable Development Goals** were adopted in **2015** by all UN members for the **2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development**. [SRC-0195, lead]

**ASEAN**
- **ASEAN** was founded on **8 August 1967** when the foreign ministers of **Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand** signed the **ASEAN Declaration** in **Bangkok**; its predecessor was the Association of Southeast Asia (ASA, 1961). [SRC-0193, "History"]
- Expansion: **Brunei 1984**, **Vietnam 1995**, **Laos and Myanmar 1997**, **Cambodia 1999**; the binding **ASEAN Charter** was signed in **2008**. [SRC-0193, "History"]
- **Timor-Leste** joined on **26 October 2025** as the **11th member** (recent; re-check). [SRC-0193, "History"]

**The Philippines in the world**
- "The State shall pursue an **independent foreign policy**. In its relations with other states, the paramount consideration shall be **national sovereignty, territorial integrity, national interest, and the right to self-determination**." The Philippines also adopts a policy of **freedom from nuclear weapons** in its territory. [SRC-0007, Art. II Secs. 7–8]
- **OFW** remittances reached about **₱1.9 trillion in 2022**, some **8.9% of GDP**; the term came into use with **RA 8042, the Migrant Workers and Overseas Filipinos Act of 1995**. [SRC-0196, lead and "Etymology"]
- **West Philippine Sea**: the Philippine name for the parts of the South China Sea in the country's **exclusive economic zone**, mandated by **Administrative Order No. 29 (September 2012)**. On **12 July 2016**, the **Permanent Court of Arbitration** ruled in favor of the Philippines. [SRC-0197, lead and "2016 PCA ruling"]
- Climate action: the **Climate Change Commission** (chaired by the President) and **LGU climate action plans** under RA 9729. [SRC-0051, Secs. 4–5, 14]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Core/semi-periphery/periphery is Wallerstein**, not modernization theory.
- **Dependency theory blames exploitation by core nations**; modernization theory locates the problem in the poor country's own lack of industrialization and values.
- **ASEAN founders = five** (Indonesia, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand); Brunei joined later.
- **UN Charter signed 26 June 1945; UN Day is 24 October.**
- **Malthus's checks: war, famine, disease.**
- **The 2016 award came from the Permanent Court of Arbitration**, not the International Court of Justice.
