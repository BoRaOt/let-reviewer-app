**Action research**, also called teacher research, is systematic, intentional inquiry by teachers to **improve their own practice**. It is different from research **about** teaching, which professional researchers design and interpret, often around questions set by academic fields or administrators.

Seifert and Sutton name three defining features. First, action research **starts from real problems** of classroom practice, such as chronic lateness or poor lab safety habits. Second, its findings are about **particular teachers and classrooms**, not teachers in general. Supporters say this makes it more credible as practical guidance, because it fits real classrooms. Third, its main audience is **other teachers**, so it offers an insider's view.

Compared with simply good teaching, action research is planned more carefully, recorded more systematically and shared more openly. That is why it is seen as a mark of teacher professionalism.
Memory aid: "My problem, my class, my colleagues."
