subtopic: Commas with essential and nonessential information
source_ids: [SRC-0104]
---
Commas often signal whether information is needed.

**Nonessential information** is not necessary to understand the sentence's meaning. It adds extra detail, so it is **set off with commas**. "The current math curriculum, **which was adopted by the school district 10 years ago,** no longer meets the needs of the students." Remove the middle part and the main meaning stays.

**Essential information** is necessary to identify what you mean, so it is **not set off with commas**. "The students **who failed the first quarter** will join the remedial class." Without the clause, we would not know which students.

A useful habit: try deleting the phrase. If the sentence still says what you mean, use commas; if not, leave them out.

Writers also improve clarity by combining short, choppy sentences into one **complex sentence** with a **dependent clause**, such as "Because the bell rang, the learners left."
Memory aid: "Can you cut it? Then comma it."
