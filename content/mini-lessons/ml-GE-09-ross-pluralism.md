Kant's view seems to leave no room when duties conflict, such as telling the truth versus protecting a friend. **W. D. Ross** (1877–1971) offered a **pluralist** deontology.

Ross held that we have several duties, and that **consequences are morally relevant when considered in light of those duties**. In a real dilemma, we weigh the duties involved and the likely results, then decide which duty is stronger in that situation. A theory that ignores either duty or consequences, he said, "over-simplifies the moral life".

This matters for teachers facing dilemmas, such as a learner confiding a family problem while asking for secrecy, when the teacher also has a duty to protect the child. A Ross-style analysis lists the duties in tension (keeping confidence, preventing harm, following child-protection rules), considers outcomes, and judges which duty takes priority here.

On the exam, "both duty and consequences must be weighed" points to Ross.
Memory aid: "Ross: many duties, weigh them with results."
