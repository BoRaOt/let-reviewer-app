Three Western movements are often tested.

**Renaissance art** flourished mainly in the **14th to 16th centuries** in Europe, across painting, sculpture, architecture, music and literature. It grew from a new awareness of nature, a revival of classical learning and a more individual view of people. Artists developed **linear perspective** to show depth realistically. Michelangelo is among its masters.

**Impressionism** arose in the **19th century**. Its painters used **visible brushstrokes**, open compositions and ordinary subjects, and above all tried to capture **changing light** at a moment in time. The name comes from **Claude Monet's** painting *Impression, Sunrise* (1872).

**Cubism**, in the early 20th century, was pioneered by **Pablo Picasso** and **Georges Braque**. Instead of one viewpoint, it broke subjects into **geometric facets**, showing several angles at once.

A quick test: deep realistic space → Renaissance; flickering light and loose strokes → Impressionism; fractured geometric planes → Cubism.
Memory aid: "Renaissance = Realistic perspective; Impressionism = Instant light; Cubism = Cubes."
