**Multiple choice** is the most used objective format because it can test higher thinking, such as application, not just facts. With four or five options, guessing is lower than in true-false, and it does not need the homogeneous lists matching requires.

Writing one well takes three steps.
1. **State the problem clearly in the stem.** If the options are unrelated statements, the item is really several true-false items.
2. **Write plausible alternatives.** Wrong options should be believable to learners who have not mastered the content, and all options should fit the stem grammatically.
3. **Remove irrelevant clues.** Do not make the correct option the longest one. Do not cluster answers in position b or c; use every position about equally, but not in an exact pattern. Avoid negatives and double negatives, and avoid trivial questions.

This very question bank checks answer length and position for these reasons.
Memory aid: "Clear stem, plausible distractors, no clues."
