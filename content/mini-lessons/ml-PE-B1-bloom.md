**Bloom's taxonomy** classifies educational objectives. It was developed by a committee chaired by **Benjamin Bloom** in **1956** and covers three domains: **cognitive** (thinking), **affective** (feelings and values) and **psychomotor** (physical skills).

The original cognitive domain had six levels: Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis and Evaluation. The **2001 revision** renamed them as verbs and swapped the top two: **Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create**. So in the revision, Create is the highest level.

The affective handbook came out in 1964. Bloom's group did not finish the psychomotor domain; **Elizabeth Simpson** later proposed a seven-level psychomotor taxonomy.

The LET table of specifications itself uses the revised levels, so you should be able to tell, for example, that "design a weekly menu" is Create while "judge which menu is healthier" is Evaluate.
