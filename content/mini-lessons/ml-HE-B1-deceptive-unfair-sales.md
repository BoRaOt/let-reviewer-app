The Consumer Act bans two kinds of bad sales behavior, before, during or after a sale.
- **Deceptive:** the seller uses concealment, false representation or fraudulent manipulation to get the consumer into a deal. For example, the seller claims a product has an approval or sponsorship it does not have.
- **Unfair or unconscionable:** the seller takes advantage of the consumer's physical or mental infirmity, ignorance, illiteracy, lack of time, or surroundings to push a deal that is grossly one-sided.

The Act also forbids chain distribution plans or pyramid sales schemes for consumer products. It makes false, deceptive or misleading advertisements unlawful in print, radio, television, outdoor signs or any other medium. On the exam, ask yourself: did the seller lie or hide something, or did the seller exploit a weakness? Lying points to deceptive. Exploiting points to unfair or unconscionable.
Memory aid: "Deceive = lie; unconscionable = exploit."
