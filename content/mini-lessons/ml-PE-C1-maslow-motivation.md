Motivation is what directs behavior toward a goal. Intrinsic motivation comes from inside, from the satisfaction of the task itself. Extrinsic motivation is about getting something from others, such as grades, prizes or praise. Abraham Maslow, in 1943, arranged needs in a hierarchy, usually drawn as a pyramid.
1. Physiological needs for survival, such as food, water and sleep (the base).
2. Safety and security.
3. Love and belonging.
4. Esteem, meaning self-worth and confidence.
5. Self-actualization, reaching one's full potential, at the top.

Maslow held that the top can be reached only when the lower needs are met. That is the logic behind school feeding programs and a safe, welcoming classroom: a hungry, frightened or excluded learner struggles to focus on lessons.
