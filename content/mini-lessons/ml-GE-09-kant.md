**Deontology** comes from the Greek *deon*, duty, and means "the study of duty". Deontologists do not judge an act only by its consequences.

**Immanuel Kant** argued that the only thing good without condition is a **good will**, the choice to do one's moral duty. Outcomes have only conditional value.

Kant distinguished **hypothetical imperatives** ("If you want good grades, study") from **categorical imperatives**, universal laws we must obey regardless of our desires. He gave two key formulations.
1. **Universal law:** act only on a maxim you could will to become a universal law. A false promise fails: if everyone made them, promises would mean nothing.
2. **Humanity:** treat humanity, in yourself and others, always as an **end** and never merely as a **means**.

So for Kant, cheating on an exam is wrong even if no one is hurt, because the maxim cannot be universalized and it uses others merely as means.
Memory aid: "Universalize it? Respect persons as ends? Then it's your duty."
