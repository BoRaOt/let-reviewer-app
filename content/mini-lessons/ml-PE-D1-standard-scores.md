A **standard score** reports a result in standard-deviation units from the mean.

The **z-score** has mean 0 and SD 1: z = (score − mean) ÷ SD. If the mean is 100 and the SD is 15, a score of 130 gives z = (130 − 100) ÷ 15 = 2, two SDs above the mean. A negative z means below the mean.

The **T-score** has mean 50 and SD 10, so T = 50 + 10z. A T-score of 70 equals z = 2.

**Stanines** use a nine-point scale with mean 5 and SD 2, reported only as whole numbers.

A **grade-equivalent** score such as 3.7 means performance typical of a Grade 3 learner in the seventh month. It is easily misread: a Grade 4 learner scoring 6.0 performed on the Grade 4 test as a Grade 6 learner would, not that the child can do Grade 6 work.
Memory aid: "z 0/1, T 50/10, Stanine 5/2."
