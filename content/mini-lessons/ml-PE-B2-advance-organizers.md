Lectures and readings are the classic teacher-directed methods. They work only if learners actively listen or read, so teachers add supports.

An **advance organizer**, an idea from **David Ausubel**, is a brief overview given **before** new material, either spoken or drawn. It partly organizes the content for learners so they know where new ideas will fit. For example, before a lesson on food preservation, the teacher shows a chart with three branches: heat, cold and drying, and says the lesson will fill in each branch.

Learners can also organize material themselves. **Outlining** and **note-taking** pull out key points. A **concept map** is a diagram of how concepts relate, usually drawn by the learner; two people's maps of the same idea can reveal how differently they think about it.

The order matters: organizer first, details second.
Memory aid: "Organize in Advance — Ausubel's map before the trip."
