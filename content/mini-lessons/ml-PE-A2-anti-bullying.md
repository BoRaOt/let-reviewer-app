The Anti-Bullying Act of 2013 (RA 10627) defines bullying as severe or repeated use, by one or more students, of written, verbal or electronic expression, or a physical act or gesture, against another student. To count, it must cause fear of harm, create a hostile school environment, infringe the victim's rights, or disrupt schooling. Every elementary and secondary school, public or private, must adopt an anti-bullying policy. The policy covers bullying on school grounds, at school activities, on school vehicles and through school technology, and also forbids retaliation against people who report. The principal oversees the policy. Staff, students, parents and volunteers must report bullying they witness. Schools report their policies, and later yearly bullying statistics, to the schools division superintendent. Note that the law's definition is about students bullying students.
Memory aid: "Student to student, school must have a policy."
