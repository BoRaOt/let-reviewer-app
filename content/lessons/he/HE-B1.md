# HE-B1 — Home management: household resources and consumer education

Annex F gives this line **9 items**, mostly *Apply* (5), with *Analyze* (2), *Understand* (1) and *Evaluate* (1). It covers two courses [SRC-0002, Annex F, B.1]:
- **Household resource management:** recognize housekeeping tools, utensils and materials; understand resource-management principles; apply financial management, budgeting and resource allocation; balance household, work and personal life.
- **Consumer education:** interpret consumer **rights and responsibilities**; propose solutions to consumer problems; and evaluate situational problems involving **consumer laws**. That last outcome makes the Consumer Act a must.

## 1. Plain explanation

**The Consumer Act of the Philippines** (RA 7394, 1992) is the main consumer-protection law.
- It protects consumers from unsafe products and from deceptive, unfair or unconscionable sales practices.
- It requires clear labels and price tags and governs warranties.
- It makes manufacturers liable for defective products.
- It orders **consumer education in all schools from primary to secondary level**, which is the legal reason HE teaches it.
- Three departments enforce it, by product type: **DOH** (food, drugs, cosmetics, devices and substances), **DA** (agricultural products) and **DTI** (other consumer products).

**Newer laws** cover new kinds of buying. RA 11967 (2023) protects online shoppers in business-to-consumer and business-to-business transactions. RA 11765 (2022) protects consumers of financial products such as savings, loans and insurance.

**Managing household money** starts with a budget.
- List income and expenses.
- Separate **fixed** expenses (such as rent) from **variable** ones (such as groceries).
- Sort expenses into categories, compare the totals, and plan for the unexpected.
- One simple guideline is **50-30-20**: half to needs, 30% to wants, 20% to savings and debt payments.

**Housekeeping** has a competency standard. TESDA's Housekeeping NC II lists the units a worker must master, from workplace hygiene and safety to cleaning rooms and public areas and doing laundry.

## 2. Testable facts

**Consumer Act (RA 7394): policy and agencies**
- State policy: protect the consumer's interests, promote their general welfare, and set standards of conduct for business and industry. [SRC-0068, Art. 2]
- Objectives: (a) protection against hazards to **health and safety**; (b) protection against **deceptive, unfair and unconscionable** sales acts and practices; (c) **information and education** for sound choices; (d) adequate **rights and means of redress**; (e) involvement of consumer representatives in social and economic policy. [SRC-0068, Art. 2]
- Implementing agencies for product standards: **DOH** for food, drugs, cosmetics, devices and substances; **DA** for agricultural products; **DTI** for other consumer products. [SRC-0068, Art. 6]
- The Act creates a **National Consumer Affairs Council** to improve coordination of consumer programs. [SRC-0068, Art. 148]

**Consumer education and responsibilities**
- The education department, with the Council, must integrate a **consumer education program** into the curricula of **all public and private schools from primary to secondary level**, plus a program for out-of-school youth and adults. [SRC-0068, Art. 154]
- The program teaches four consumer **responsibilities**: **critical awareness** (be alert and questioning about the use, price and quality of goods); **assertiveness** (act to get a fair deal; a passive consumer keeps being exploited); **social concern** (awareness of one's consumption's impact on others, especially the disadvantaged); **environmental awareness** (understand the environmental consequences of consumption and conserve resources for future generations). It also covers **consumer rights** and practical daily problems. [SRC-0068, Art. 154]
- A consumer may file a **petition or letter-complaint** and the concerned department may investigate; departments must set up simple, accessible complaint procedures. [SRC-0068, Art. 159]

**Sales practices, labels, prices**
- A sales act is **deceptive** when the seller, through concealment, false representation or fraudulent manipulation, induces a consumer into a transaction, whether before, during or after it. [SRC-0068, Art. 50]
- A sales act is **unfair or unconscionable** when the seller takes advantage of the consumer's physical or mental infirmity, ignorance, illiteracy, lack of time or surroundings to get a grossly one-sided deal. [SRC-0068, Art. 52]
- **Chain distribution plans or pyramid sales schemes** shall not be used to sell consumer products. [SRC-0068, Art. 53]
- The **metric system** is the official system of weights and measures for products and commercial transactions. [SRC-0068, Art. 63]
- **Minimum label contents** for consumer products: registered trade or brand name; registered trademark; registered business name; Philippine address of the manufacturer, importer or repacker; general make or active ingredients; net quantity in metric units; country of manufacture if imported; among others. [SRC-0068, Art. 77]
- **Price tags:** no consumer product may be offered for retail without a publicly displayed price tag, label or marking. It may not be sold above the tagged price, and the same price applies to all buyers. [SRC-0068, Art. 81]
- **False, deceptive or misleading advertisement** to induce purchases, in any medium, is unlawful. [SRC-0068, Art. 110]

**Warranties and liability**
- A seller or manufacturer giving an **express warranty** must state its terms in clear language: who the warrantor is, who is covered, which products or parts, what the warrantor will do and at whose expense, what the consumer must do, and the period for action. [SRC-0068, Art. 68(a)]
- Written warranties are **operative from the moment of sale**. [SRC-0068, Art. 68(b)]
- Manufacturers, producers and importers are liable **independently of fault** for damages caused by defects in design, manufacture, assembly, formulas, handling, packing, or by inadequate information on use and hazards. A product is **defective** when it does not offer the safety rightfully expected of it. [SRC-0068, Art. 97]

**Online and financial consumers**
- The **Internet Transactions Act** (RA 11967) covers **business-to-business and business-to-consumer** internet transactions under DTI, where a party is in the Philippines or the platform or merchant targets the Philippine market. **Online media content and consumer-to-consumer (C2C)** transactions are **not** covered. [SRC-0069, Sec. 3]
- A **financial consumer** (RA 11765) is a person or entity who buys, leases or receives, or may buy, lease or receive, financial products or services, such as savings and deposits, from a financial service provider. [SRC-0070, Sec. 3(a),(c)]

**Household budgeting**
- **Fixed expenses** do not change over time (rent, car payments, insurance, internet). **Variable expenses** change month to month (gasoline, groceries). [SRC-0090, 6.5]
- Budget steps include **categorizing** expenses, **totaling and comparing** monthly income and expenses (adjust if expenses are higher), and **planning for unplanned expenses**. [SRC-0090, 6.5]
- Yearly, quarterly or semiannual bills are budgeted monthly by **dividing the bill by the number of months it covers**. [SRC-0090, 6.5]
- **50-30-20 budget philosophy:** 50% of income for **needs**, 30% for **wants**, 20% for **savings and debt service**. Wants and needs are often confused. [SRC-0090, 6.5]
- **Simple interest** is computed on the **principal**, the amount borrowed or deposited, using the annual rate and the term in years. [SRC-0090, 6.3]

**Housekeeping competencies (TESDA Housekeeping NC II)**
- Units of competency include: participate in workplace communication; work in team environment; practice career professionalism; practice occupational health and safety; observe workplace hygiene procedures; perform workplace and safety practices; provide effective customer service; provide housekeeping services to guests; clean and prepare rooms for incoming guests; laundry linen and guest clothes; clean public areas, facilities and equipment. [SRC-0078, units of competency]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Rights vs responsibilities.** Article 154's four items (critical awareness, assertiveness, social concern, environmental awareness) are *responsibilities*. "Being alert and questioning about price and quality" = critical awareness. "Speaking up to get a fair deal" = assertiveness.
- **Deceptive vs unfair or unconscionable.** Deceptive = lying or concealing (false representation). Unfair or unconscionable = exploiting a weakness (illiteracy, illness, being rushed) for a grossly one-sided deal.
- **Which agency?** Food, drugs and cosmetics → DOH. Farm products → DA. Most other consumer goods → DTI.
- **Online law scope.** A buy-and-sell between two private individuals (C2C) is **not** covered by RA 11967.
- **Fixed vs variable.** Rent and insurance are fixed even if large; groceries and fuel are variable even if small.
- **Warranty starts at sale**, not at registration or at the first defect.

## 4. Memory aid

**"50-30-20 = Needs, Wants, Savings."**
