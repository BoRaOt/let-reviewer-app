**Consequentialism** judges an action by its results: an action is right when it produces the greatest good for everyone. Its most famous form is **utilitarianism**.

**Jeremy Bentham** (1748–1832) first set out the **principle of utility**. **John Stuart Mill** formulated the **greatest happiness principle**: right actions are those that produce the **greatest happiness for the greatest number** of people. Everyone affected counts, not just the person acting.

Utilitarians split on how to apply it. **Act utilitarians** weigh each act case by case: "Will this particular lie produce more happiness?" Critics say this can justify shocking acts. **Rule utilitarians** use the principle to choose **rules**: "Does a general rule of honesty produce more happiness overall?" Then they follow the rule.

Mohism, founded by Mozi in ancient China, is an early consequentialist ethic.
Memory aid: "Count everyone's happiness; Act = each case, Rule = best rules."
