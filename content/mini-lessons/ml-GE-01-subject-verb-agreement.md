The subject and verb in a sentence must **agree in number**: singular with singular, plural with plural. Three rules cause most errors.

**Words between subject and verb do not change agreement.** Find the real subject. "The **price** of these shoes at all stores **is** unbelievable." *Price* is the subject, not *shoes*. The same holds for phrases like "along with" and "as well as".

**Compound subjects joined by *or* or *nor*:** the verb agrees with the **subject closest to it**. "Either your uncles or your **mother remembers** the recipe." "Neither the teacher nor the **students are** ready."

**Indefinite pronouns** such as **anybody, anyone, each, either, everybody, everyone, neither, nobody, no one, nothing, one, somebody, someone** and **something** take a **singular** verb. "Each of the learners **has** a module."

Readers of formal documents expect writers to follow these conventions.
Memory aid: "Skip the middle, look at the nearer one with or/nor, and every 'one' or 'body' is singular."
