# GE-06 — Art Appreciation

GenEd unit, **15 items** (10%). Outcomes: **interpret artistic creations**; develop an **informed appreciation** of art forms. [SRC-0001, Annex A, GE] HE-B5 covers Philippine crafts and GAMABA; this lesson covers art in general and Philippine fine arts.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Why art?** Art has **non-motivated** functions (part of being human: harmony and balance, the mysterious, imagination, ritual) and **motivated** functions (communication, entertainment, political change, social inquiry, social causes, healing, propaganda and commerce).

**How to look.** Use the **elements** (line, shape, form, color, value, texture, space) and **composition** (rule of thirds, rule of odds, symmetry, movement).

**Movements.** **Renaissance** (14th–16th c.; perspective; Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael). **Impressionism** (19th c.; Monet's *Impression, Sunrise*, 1872; visible brushstrokes and light). **Cubism** (Picasso and Braque; geometric faceting).

**Philippine art.** **Juan Luna's *Spoliarium*** won the first gold medal in Madrid, **1884** (Hidalgo won silver). **Fernando Amorsolo** — first National Artist, known for **backlighting** rural scenes. The **Order of National Artists** began with **Proclamation 1001 (1972)**; the **GAMABA** honors folk artists.

**Institutions and law.** **NCCA** (RA 7356), **National Cultural Heritage Act** (RA 10066), artists' **moral rights** (RA 8293).

## 2. Testable facts

**Functions of art**
- **Non-motivated** functions are integral to being human: a basic instinct for **harmony, balance and rhythm**; **experience of the mysterious**; **expression of the imagination**; **ritualistic and symbolic** functions. [SRC-0156, "Non-motivated functions"]
- **Motivated** functions are intentional: **communication**; **entertainment**; the **avant-garde / political change** (Dadaism, Surrealism, Russian constructivism); a "free zone"; **social inquiry or subversion**; **social causes**; **psychological and healing** purposes (art therapy); **propaganda or commercialism**. The functions overlap. [SRC-0156, "Motivated functions"]

**Elements and composition**
- **Elements of art/design:** **line** (path the eye follows), **shape** (2-D, geometric or organic), **form** (3-D: length, width, depth), **color** (hue, value, intensity), **value** (lightness and darkness), **texture** (real or implied surface quality), **space** (positive and negative). [SRC-0154, sections; SRC-0155, "Elements of design"]
- **Composition** ("putting together") is the organization of an artwork, distinct from its **subject**; techniques include the **rule of thirds**, **rule of odds**, **rule of space**, simplification, geometry and symmetry, and creating movement. [SRC-0155, lead and "Compositional techniques"]
- Color schemes: **monochromatic, complementary, split-complementary, analogous, triadic**. [SRC-0153, scheme sections]

**Movements**
- **Renaissance art** (painting, sculpture, architecture, music, literature) was produced mainly in the **14th–16th centuries** in Europe; its masters include **Michelangelo**; studies of **perspective** shaped painters such as Masaccio. [SRC-0230, lead and body]
- **Impressionism** (19th century): **visible brushstrokes**, open composition, emphasis on the **changing qualities of light**, ordinary subject matter; the name comes from **Claude Monet's *Impression, Sunrise*** (1872). [SRC-0228, lead]
- **Cubism** was pioneered by **Pablo Picasso and Georges Braque**; associated movements share the **faceting or simplification of geometric forms**. [SRC-0229, lead]

**Philippine art and artists**
- **Juan Luna's *Spoliarium*** was submitted to the **Exposición Nacional de Bellas Artes, Madrid, 1884**, winning the **first gold medal** (of three); **Félix Resurrección Hidalgo** won a **silver** medal; **Rizal** toasted their triumph as "fresh proof of racial equality". [SRC-0226, lead and "José Rizal and the Spoliarium"]
- The *Spoliarium* hangs in the **National Museum of Fine Arts**, Manila, and the Museum considers it the **largest painting in the Philippines**. [SRC-0226, lead]
- **Fernando Amorsolo** (1892–1972), portraitist and painter of **rural Philippine landscapes**, the "**Grand Old Man of Philippine Art**", was the **first National Artist**, recognized for his impressionistic technique and use of **lighting and backlight**. [SRC-0227, lead]
- The **Order of National Artists** is administered by the **Cultural Center of the Philippines** (under **Proclamation No. 1001, April 27, 1972**) and the **NCCA**; because Proclamation 1001 is non-retroactive, artists who died before 1972 cannot be named. [SRC-0225, lead]
- The **Gawad sa Manlilikha ng Bayan** (RA 7355, 1992) honors **traditional folk artists**, separate from the National Artists. [SRC-0170, "History"; SRC-0048, Sec. 3]

**Institutions and law**
- **RA 7356** created the **National Commission for Culture and the Arts**; it declares that every citizen has a duty to **preserve and conserve** Filipino historical and cultural heritage. [SRC-0047, Secs. 1, 7]
- **Cultural property** under RA 10066 is all products of human creativity by which a people reveal their identity, tangible or intangible. [SRC-0049, Sec. 3(o)]
- Authors of works have **moral rights** (e.g. to be credited and to object to distortion), independent of economic rights. [SRC-0044, Sec. 193.1–193.3]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Spoliarium won gold (Luna); Hidalgo won silver** — same 1884 Madrid exposition.
- **Amorsolo = first National Artist (1972)**; not a GAMABA awardee.
- **Impressionism named after Monet**, not Manet.
- **Cubism = Picasso + Braque.**
- **Form is 3-D, shape is 2-D.**
- **Composition ≠ subject**: the same subject can be composed many ways.
- **Art therapy is a motivated (healing) function**; ritual decoration can be non-motivated.
