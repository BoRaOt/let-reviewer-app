Refrigerators and air conditioners do not "make cold". They **remove heat** from an enclosed space and release it somewhere warmer. Most use the **vapor-compression** cycle, in which a refrigerant changes between liquid and gas.

The system has four main parts in a loop.
1. The **compressor** squeezes the refrigerant gas, raising its pressure and temperature.
2. The **condenser**, the coils outside or at the back, releases that heat to the surroundings, and the refrigerant becomes a liquid.
3. The **expansion (throttle) valve** drops the pressure suddenly, so the refrigerant becomes very cold.
4. The **evaporator**, inside the cooled space, lets the cold refrigerant absorb heat and boil back into gas, which returns to the compressor.

So the evaporator cools the room and the condenser warms the outside. Dirty condenser coils make the whole system work harder.
Memory aid: "Compress, Condense, Expand, Evaporate — heat in inside, heat out outside."
