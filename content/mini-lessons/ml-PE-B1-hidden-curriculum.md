Not everything students learn in school is in the curriculum guide. The **hidden curriculum** is the set of lessons "which are learned but not openly intended" to be taught: the norms, values and beliefs that come through classroom routines and the school's social life.

Examples include learning to wait your turn, learning who gets called on most, learning that some groups are seated in front, or learning ideas about gender from which students are asked to carry chairs and which to clean. During breaks, students learn unspoken lessons about cliques, bullying, self-expression and conflict resolution.

The hidden curriculum can reinforce existing social inequalities even when the school aims for equal development. Learners with limited social awareness, such as some learners with autism, may miss these unwritten norms unless they are taught directly.

Teachers should notice what their routines teach without words.
Memory aid: "Hidden = learned but never planned."
