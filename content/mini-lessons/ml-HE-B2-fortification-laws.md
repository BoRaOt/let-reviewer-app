Three laws shape food in the Philippines.
- **Philippine Food Fortification Act (Republic Act 8976):** fortification means adding nutrients to processed foods at levels above their natural state. It targets micronutrient deficiencies by fortifying widely eaten foods. A micronutrient is an essential nutrient needed in very small amounts, measured in milligrams or micrograms.
- **ASIN law (Republic Act 8172):** salt iodization is a priority health program, and food outlets, restaurants and stores must offer customers only iodized salt.
- **Food Safety Act of 2013 (Republic Act 10611):** strengthens the food safety regulatory system. It protects the public from food-borne and water-borne illnesses and from unsanitary, unwholesome, misbranded or adulterated food, while building trust in the system and supporting fair trade.

On the exam, a scenario about adding iron or vitamin A to a staple food is fortification.
Memory aid: "Fortify the food, iodize the salt, regulate the risk."
