Jacob **Kounin's** classic classroom studies (1970) gave teachers three useful terms.

**Withitness** is being aware of everything happening in the room at once, like having "eyes in the back of your head". It does not mean giving every activity equal attention, only noticing all of them. Experienced teachers show more withitness than beginners, but it grows with practice.

**Overlapping** is acting on two or more events at nearly the same time, such as helping one group while signaling a wandering learner to sit down. Withitness makes overlapping possible.

The **ripple effect** is the spread of misbehavior: when a small disruption is left alone, others may copy it.

Simply showing learners that you are withit, by scanning the room, moving around and naming what you see, can prevent off-task behavior before it starts.
Memory aid: "With-it sees all; Overlap handles two; Ripple spreads if ignored."
