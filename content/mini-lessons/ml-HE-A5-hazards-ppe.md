TESDA's unit on occupational health and safety has five elements: identify hazards and risks, evaluate them, control them, maintain safety awareness, and perform basic first aid. Hazards are grouped into four types.
- **Physical:** impact, poor lighting, pressure, noise, vibration, temperature and radiation.
- **Biological:** bacteria, viruses, plants, parasites, mites, molds, fungi and insects.
- **Chemical:** dusts, fibers, mists, fumes, smoke, gases and vapors.
- **Ergonomic:** over-exertion, awkward or static positions, fatigue and monotony.

Evaluating a hazard means comparing exposure with maximum tolerable limits, called threshold limit values. Controlling it means following procedures and wearing protective equipment, such as masks, gloves, goggles, hairnets, face shields, ear muffs and aprons. Emergency measures include evacuation, isolation, decontamination and calling emergency personnel.
Memory aid: "Physical, Biological, Chemical, Ergonomic — PBCE, Please Be Careful Everyone."
