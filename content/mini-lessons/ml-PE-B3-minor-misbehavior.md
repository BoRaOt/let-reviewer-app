Responses to misbehavior should match its size. Seifert and Sutton start with the mildest.

**Ignoring** can be wise when a behavior is minor, infrequent and unlikely to disrupt others, such as a brief sigh or doodle. Attention can sometimes reward the very behavior you want to stop. The hard part is judging whether the behavior is truly minor; if it spreads, the ripple effect makes ignoring a mistake.

**Gesturing nonverbally** is the next step: eye contact, a raised eyebrow, a finger to the lips, or moving closer to the learner. These redirect behavior with little or no talk and do not interrupt the lesson for everyone else.

Be careful with cultural differences. Young children and learners raised in other cultural communities may have learned different nonverbal signals and may misread yours. Explain your signals explicitly early in the year.
Memory aid: "Ignore the tiny, signal the small, speak only when needed."
