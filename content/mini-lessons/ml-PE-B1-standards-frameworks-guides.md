Curriculum documents range from broad to detailed. **Standards** state, in general terms, what students should know and be able to do in a subject and grade. They are a first step from big educational purposes toward classroom teaching, but they say little about daily lessons.

A **curriculum framework** organizes the standards for a subject across grade levels. It breaks each standard into a dozen or more specific skills, describes the **scope and sequence**, and names **observable behaviors** that show learning. Sometimes it suggests how to assess them.

A **curriculum guide** is more detailed still, closer to what teachers actually plan from. Even so, teachers must turn guides into unit plans and **objectives** for their own learners.

A quick check on the exam: if a document lists skills by grade without daily activities, it is a framework, not a lesson plan.
Memory aid: "Standards say what; frameworks say in what order; guides say how; teachers say today."
