Woven fabric has three directions. The **straight grain** runs along the warp threads, parallel to the **selvedge**, the finished edge. It stretches the least, so most garments are cut with the straight grain running from top to bottom. The **cross grain** runs across, along the weft, and stretches a little more. The **bias** is any diagonal; at 45° it is called true bias. Fabric is stretchiest and most fluid on the bias, which is why bias-cut skirts drape softly and bias tape bends around curves.

**Seam allowance** is the strip between the cut edge and the stitching line. Home-sewing patterns use about 1/4 to 5/8 inch, with 5/8 inch (1.5 cm) common because it keeps fraying fabric safely inside the seam. Curves such as necklines get narrower allowances.

TESDA requires patterns to be pinned along the grain line and seam allowances marked before cutting.
Memory aid: "Selvedge = straight = steady; Bias = bend."
