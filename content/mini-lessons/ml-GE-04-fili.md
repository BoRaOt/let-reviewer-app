**El Filibusterismo**, "The Reign of Greed" in one translation, is the sequel to the Noli. It was first published in **1891** in **Ghent**, Belgium.

Its central figure is the same **Crisóstomo Ibarra**, who returns years later in disguise as the rich jeweler **Simoun**. This time he seeks **vengeance**, secretly plotting to provoke a revolution by deepening corruption in the colonial government. The novel is darker than the Noli and asks whether violent revolution can bring true change.

Rizal's sense of injustice was shaped by events such as the **Cavite Mutiny of 1872**, after which three Filipino priests, **Gómez, Burgos and Zamora (Gomburza)**, were executed by the colonial authorities. Their martyrdom deeply affected Rizal's generation.

To keep the novels straight: Noli, Berlin, 1887, Ibarra the reformer; Fili, Ghent, 1891, Simoun the avenger.
Memory aid: "Berlin begins with Ibarra; Ghent ends with Simoun."
