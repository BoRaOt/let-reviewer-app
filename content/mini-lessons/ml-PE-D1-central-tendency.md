Measures of central tendency describe a typical score.
- **Mean:** add all scores and divide by how many there are.
- **Median:** the middle score when scores are in order; half are above and half below.
- **Mode:** the most frequent score. Two modes make a distribution **bimodal**.

Each tells you something different. The mean is used in further statistics, but it is **pulled by extreme scores**, called outliers. The median is **not**.

Example: ten quiz scores are 4, 18, 18, 19, 19, 19, 19, 19, 20 and 20. The mean is 175 divided by 10, or 17.5, dragged down by the single 4. The median and the mode are both 19, which better describes most of the class. So when a few scores are extreme, the median is often the better summary of typical performance.

In a perfectly normal, bell-shaped distribution, the mean, median and mode are equal.
Memory aid: "Mean = average, Median = middle, Mode = most; outliers move only the mean."
