Two Philippine fibers are famous worldwide. **Piña** is drawn from the leaves of the pineapple plant. Each strand is scraped by hand and knotted into a long thread, then hand-woven into sheer, lace-like *nipis* cloth used for the barong tagalog and baro't saya. Aklan piña handloom weaving was added to UNESCO's intangible heritage list in 2023.

**Abacá**, or Manila hemp, comes from *Musa textilis*, a banana relative found naturally only in the Philippines. The Bicol region has the most varieties, and the country supplies most of the world's abacá.

The law supports these fabrics. **RA 9242 (2004)**, the Philippine Tropical Fabrics Law, defines tropical fabrics as those with natural fibers produced, spun, woven or knitted and finished in the Philippines, and requires them for government officials' and employees' uniforms.
Memory aid: "Piña = pineapple leaf; Abacá = banana cousin; RA 9242 dresses the government in them."
