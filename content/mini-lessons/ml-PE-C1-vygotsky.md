Lev Vygotsky argued that thinking develops through social interaction, not only through a child's solo discovery. His best-known idea is the zone of proximal development, or ZPD. It holds concepts and skills just beyond what a learner can do alone, which the learner is ready to master with a little help, often from a teacher, a parent or a more skilled peer. That help is called scaffolding. Like training wheels on a bicycle, it is given while the learner needs it and then gradually withdrawn as the learner becomes competent. In a Home Economics class, a teacher who demonstrates a stitch, guides the student through the first few, and then steps back is scaffolding inside the ZPD. Remember the difference: the ZPD is the zone of possible learning; scaffolding is the support used within it.
Memory aid: "Zone = where; scaffold = how (and then remove it)."
