Culture shapes how people see themselves. Psychologists compare **individualist** and **collectivist** cultures.

In **individualist cultures**, people tend to believe that **independence, competition and personal achievement** are important. The self is defined by personal traits and goals: "I am hardworking and ambitious."

In **collectivist cultures**, people value **social harmony, respectfulness and group needs over individual needs**. The self is defined more by relationships and roles: "I am a daughter, a teammate, a member of this barangay." Asian cultures tend to be more collectivist, and people in them tend to be less extroverted.

Because the self is built through social interaction, as Mead argued, the culture around a child teaches which kind of self to build.

For teachers in the Philippines, this explains why some learners prefer group work and avoid standing out, and why public praise or criticism can feel very different across learners.
Memory aid: "Individualist = I-centered achievement; Collectivist = We-centered harmony."
