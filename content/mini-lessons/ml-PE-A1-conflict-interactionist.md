Not every sociologist sees schools as neutral.
- **Conflict theorists** argue that education can widen social inequality. One mechanism is the hidden curriculum: nonacademic lessons learned through informal learning and cultural transmission, which reward students who already have more cultural capital. Another is tracking, which sorts students into advanced and low groups. Conflict theorists say tracking keeps inequality in place and creates self-fulfilling prophecies.
- **Symbolic interactionists** look at everyday interaction between teachers and students. When a student is labeled a low achiever, the label is hard to shake off and can come true because people act on it.
- **Feminist theorists** study how gender inequality is rooted in, and repeated by, education.

For teachers, the practical lesson is to watch what expectations, groupings and labels communicate, not only what the official lesson plan says.
Memory aid: "Conflict counts winners; interaction watches labels."
