**Integrated pest management (IPM)** combines every useful method so that pesticides are used as little as possible. FAO describes it as integrating **biological, chemical, physical and cultural** measures to grow healthy crops while reducing pesticide risks.

Start with **prevention**: resistant varieties, healthy crops, rotation and intercropping. If pests rise, use **mechanical** controls such as hand-picking, water sprays, barriers and traps. Encourage **biological** control by protecting or adding natural enemies like spiders, wasps and frogs. Use **chemical** control, including biopesticides like Bt, only when needed.

The key IPM idea is the **action threshold**: act only when the pest level would cause damage costing more than the treatment. That requires regular monitoring. The cycle is: sample, identify, learn the pest's biology, set the threshold, choose tactics, evaluate. In Asian rice fields, early spraying killed the planthopper's predators and made outbreaks worse.
