**Inquiry learning**, also called discovery learning (Bruner) or progressive education (Dewey), turns lecture upside down. The teacher, or sometimes a student, poses a thoughtful question, such as "Why does bread go moldy faster in some kitchens?" The teacher **avoids answering directly**, building on students' ideas with more questions and guiding their investigation. Learners gain new knowledge and practice a way of learning they can reuse.

**Cooperative learning**, or collaborative learning, has students work in groups that are rewarded partly or wholly for **group success**. It needs time and space for talk, and a grading plan. If one grade goes to the whole group, some members may **freeload**. If grades go only to individual parts, members may **overspecialize** and stop cooperating. Good designs combine group goals with individual accountability, and teachers monitor groups and point out quieter members' contributions.
Memory aid: "Inquiry: ask, don't tell. Cooperative: sink or swim together, but each one swims."
