subtopic: Functionalism — manifest and latent functions of education
source_ids: [SRC-0088]
---
Functionalists see education as one of society's most important institutions, doing two kinds of work. Manifest, or primary, functions are the intended and visible ones. The first is socialization. The French sociologist Émile Durkheim called schools socialization agencies that teach children to get along with others and prepare them for adult economic roles. Another manifest function is social control: schools teach students to follow rules, obey laws and respect authority, which prepares them for the workplace. Latent, or secondary, functions are hidden and unintended by-products of going to school. Examples include meeting a future partner, building friendships and networks that later help in finding a job, and learning to work in small groups. On the exam, decide whether the outcome was the school's purpose. If it was, it is a manifest function; if it was a side effect, it is latent.
Memory aid: "Manifest = meant; latent = by-product."
