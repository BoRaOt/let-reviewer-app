# PE-B1 — The teacher and the school curriculum

Section B of ProfEd is **45 items** (30%). Our working split gives this line about **15 items**. Outcomes: curriculum **planning, design, development and evaluation**. [SRC-0001, Annex A, ProfEd B]

## 1. Plain explanation

**What is a curriculum?** The planned set of goals, content, learning experiences and assessments a school offers. Students also learn a **hidden curriculum** — norms and values no one openly set out to teach.

**Classic planning model: Tyler (1949).** Four questions: purposes → learning experiences → organization → evaluation.

**Backward design: Understanding by Design (Wiggins & McTighe, 1998).** Start from desired results, then decide on evidence (assessment), then plan activities.

**Spiral curriculum (Bruner).** Revisit topics at increasing depth. K to 12 is required by law to use the **spiral progression approach**.

**Philippine curriculum law (RA 10533).** K to 12 = 1 year kindergarten + 6 elementary + 4 junior high + 2 senior high. The curriculum must be learner-centered, inclusive, developmentally appropriate, relevant, research-based, culture-sensitive, contextualized and global, constructivist and inquiry-based, spiral, and flexible enough to **localize and indigenize**. RA 12027 (2024) discontinued the mother tongue as medium of instruction in K–3.

**From standards to lessons.** Standards (broad) → curriculum framework (scope and sequence, specific skills) → curriculum guide → unit and lesson objectives.

**Writing objectives.** Mager's behavioral objectives: observable behavior, condition, and criterion. Bloom's taxonomy sorts objectives by thinking level.

## 2. Testable facts

**Curriculum models**
- **Ralph Tyler**, *Basic Principles of Curriculum and Instruction* (**1949**): (1) What **educational purposes** should the school seek to attain? (2) How can **learning experiences** be selected? (3) How can they be **organized**? (4) How can their effectiveness be **evaluated**? [SRC-0177, "Thoughts on curriculum"]
- Tyler: "It is what he does that he learns, not what the teacher does." He headed evaluation for the **Eight-Year Study** (1933–1941) and is called the father of educational evaluation. [SRC-0177, lead, "Eight-Year Study", "Thoughts on curriculum"]
- **Understanding by Design** (Wiggins & McTighe, 1998) is **backward design**: Stage 1 identify **desired results** (understandings, essential questions); Stage 2 determine **assessment evidence**; Stage 3 plan **learning activities**. [SRC-0178, lead and "Backward design"]
- Backward design responds to the habit of starting planning with **activities and textbooks** instead of goals. [SRC-0178, "Backward design"]
- **Spiral curriculum (Jerome Bruner):** each subject is revisited at intervals at a **more sophisticated level** each time, linking later learning to earlier learning. [SRC-0180, lead]
- **Hidden curriculum:** lessons "which are learned but not openly intended", such as norms, values and beliefs conveyed in the classroom and social environment; it can reinforce social inequalities. [SRC-0181, lead]

**K to 12 law (RA 10533, Enhanced Basic Education Act of 2013)**
- Structure: at least **1 year kindergarten, 6 years elementary, 6 years secondary** (**4 junior high + 2 senior high**). Kindergarten entrants are at least **5 years old**; typical entrant ages are **6** (Grade 1), **12** (JHS) and **16** (SHS). [SRC-0021, Sec. 4]
- **DepEd** formulates the curriculum, working with **CHED** (harmonized basic and tertiary curricula) and **TESDA**, and consulting DOLE, PRC, schools, teachers, parents and industry. [SRC-0021, Sec. 5]
- Curriculum standards and principles: (a) **learner-centered, inclusive, developmentally appropriate**; (b) **relevant, responsive, research-based**; (c) **culture-sensitive**; (d) **contextualized and global**; (e) pedagogy that is **constructivist, inquiry-based, reflective, collaborative and integrative**; (f) MTB-MLE principles ("from the known to the unknown"); (g) **spiral progression**; (h) **flexible** so schools can **localize, indigenize and enhance** it. [SRC-0021, Sec. 5(a)–(h)]
- Approval of locally produced teaching materials **devolves to regional and division** offices. [SRC-0021, Sec. 5(h)]
- A **Curriculum Consultative Committee** chaired by the DepEd Secretary (with CHED, TESDA, DOLE, PRC, DOST and business representatives) oversees review and evaluation of the curriculum. [SRC-0021, Sec. 6]
- **RA 12027 (2024)** **discontinued** the mother tongue as **medium of instruction** in kindergarten to Grade 3; instruction reverts to **Filipino and English**, with regional languages as **auxiliary** media. [SRC-0022, Sec. 1]
- MTB-MLE may still be used in **monolingual classes** that meet requirements (KWF orthography, documented vocabulary, materials, trained teachers); DepEd and KWF develop a **language mapping** policy. [SRC-0022, Sec. 2]

**From standards to classroom objectives (Seifert & Sutton Ch. 10)**
- **Standards** statements are general; a **curriculum framework** organizes standards into specific skills (the **scope and sequence**) and names observable behaviors; a **curriculum guide** is more detailed still. Neither standards nor frameworks lay out daily activity plans. [SRC-0109, Ch. 10 "Curriculum frameworks and curriculum guides"]
- Two approaches to objectives: the **cognitive** approach moves from **general to specific** (goals with representative **indicators**, Gronlund); the **behavioral** approach moves from **specific to general** (Mager). [SRC-0109, Ch. 10 "Formulating learning objectives"]
- **Mager's behavioral objective** has three features: an **observable behavior**, the **conditions** of performance, and a **minimum acceptable level** (criterion). Example criterion: "100 per cent accuracy within 15 minutes". [SRC-0109, Ch. 10 "From specific to general: behavioral objectives"]
- Objectives describe what **students** will do, not what the teacher will do; planning needs both long-term goals and short-term objectives. [SRC-0109, Ch. 10 "Formulating learning objectives"]

**Bloom's taxonomy**
- Developed by a committee chaired by **Benjamin Bloom (1956)**; domains: **cognitive, affective, psychomotor**. [SRC-0179, lead]
- Original cognitive levels: Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, **Synthesis, Evaluation**. **2001 revision:** **Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create** (renamed, and the top two reordered). [SRC-0179, lead and "Cognitive"]
- The affective handbook appeared in **1964**; **Elizabeth Simpson** (1972) proposed a seven-level psychomotor taxonomy. [SRC-0179, "History", "Psychomotor"]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Tyler starts with objectives; UbD starts with results and evidence.** Both are "ends first", but UbD puts **assessment before activities**.
- **Spiral ≠ repetition.** Topics return at a **higher level** each time.
- **K–12 has 6 years of secondary** (4 JHS + 2 SHS), not 4.
- **MTB-MLE is no longer the K–3 medium of instruction** (RA 12027, 2024), though it may continue in qualifying monolingual classes.
- **"The teacher will explain…" is not an objective** — objectives state learner behavior.
- **Revised Bloom: Create is highest**, Evaluate second; in 1956 Evaluation was highest and Synthesis below it.
- **Hidden curriculum is unintended**, not "secret content".

## 4. Memory aid

**Tyler "POOE"**: Purposes, Opportunities (experiences), Organization, Evaluation.
**UbD "Results → Evidence → Activities"** ("REA" — Really End At the start).
**Mager "ABC-D" shortcut: Behavior, Condition, Degree.**
**Revised Bloom "Remember Understand Apply Analyze Evaluate Create" — "Real Understanding Always Achieves Excellent Creations."**
