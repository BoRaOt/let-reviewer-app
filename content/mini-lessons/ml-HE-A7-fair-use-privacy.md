Digital teaching still follows the law. Under the Intellectual Property Code, **fair use** of a copyrighted work for criticism, comment, news reporting, **teaching (including multiple copies for classroom use)**, scholarship and research is not infringement. Fair use is weighed by factors, including whether the use is commercial or non-profit educational and the nature of the work. It is not a blanket permission.

The Data Privacy Act treats learners' **education and health** records as sensitive personal information, so grades and medical notes should not be posted in public group chats.

When choosing media, teachers often cite **Edgar Dale's Cone of Experience** (1946), which ranks media from concrete experiences to abstract symbols. Dale's original cone contained **no percentages**. The familiar "people remember 10% of what they read" figures came later from someone else.
Memory aid: "Teach fairly, protect privately, and Dale had no percentages."
Sources: SRC-0044 Sec. 185.1; SRC-0045 Sec. 3(l); SRC-0148 Cone of Experience
