**Edgar Dale**, an American educator, introduced the **Cone of Experience** in a 1946 textbook on audiovisual teaching and revised it in 1954 and 1969. The cone arranges kinds of learning experiences from the most **concrete** at the base, such as direct, hands-on experience, to the most **abstract** at the tip, such as words and symbols. Its message: young or new learners often need concrete experiences before abstract ones, and media should be chosen with that in mind.

A popular version adds percentages, such as "we remember 10 percent of what we read and 90 percent of what we do". **Dale never included those numbers** and warned readers not to take the cone too literally; the figures came from a later, non-scholarly article. If an item attributes the percentages to Dale, it is wrong.

Choosing media also follows **TPACK**: the medium should fit both the content and the teaching method.
Memory aid: "Concrete at the base, abstract at the tip — and no percentages from Dale."
