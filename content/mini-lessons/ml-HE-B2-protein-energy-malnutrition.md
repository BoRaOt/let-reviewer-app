Two syndromes come from too little protein.
- **Kwashiorkor:** caused by a severe lack of dietary protein. The name comes from a language in Ghana and means "rejected one". It was often seen in children who had just been weaned because a new baby arrived, and who were then fed watery, low-protein porridge. Its hallmark is swelling, or edema, of the feet and abdomen, giving a pot-belly. This happens because without enough protein the body cannot make enough albumin to hold water in the blood vessels. Other signs include poor skin, light thinning hair, low muscle mass and slowed growth.
- **Marasmus:** the person lacks both protein and calories. The word is Greek for starvation. Marasmus causes extreme wasting, a skin-and-bones look, and mostly affects infants under one year in poor countries.
Memory aid: "Kwashiorkor swells; marasmus wastes."
