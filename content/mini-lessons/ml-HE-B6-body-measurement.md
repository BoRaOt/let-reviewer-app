A pattern can only fit if the measurements are right, so TESDA's dressmaking standard treats measuring as its own element. Prepare the measuring tools first: **tape measure, hip curve rule, L-square and meter stick**. Then take the client's measurements **in a fixed sequence and position**, following standard body measurement, and **record** them at once. Assessors watch the order and the client's position, not only the numbers.

The standard measurements include the shoulder, neck circumference, chest width and bust circumference, bust height and width, front and back figure, **waistline**, **hips**, **armhole circumference**, and the lengths of the sleeve, blouse, skirt or dress.

Before measuring, the dressmaker plans the design with the client, agreeing on fabric and special needs such as the kind of collar, sleeve, pocket or skirt. Measurements without a clear design waste time.
Memory aid: "Tools ready, client still, same order, write it down."
