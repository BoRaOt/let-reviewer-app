**Ralph Tyler**, often called the father of educational evaluation, set out a simple model in *Basic Principles of Curriculum and Instruction* (1949). It asks four questions in order.
1. **What educational purposes** should the school seek to attain? This means defining objectives.
2. **What learning experiences** are likely to help attain them? This means selecting experiences.
3. **How should those experiences be organized** for effective instruction?
4. **How can their effectiveness be evaluated?** Evaluate, then revise what did not work.

Tyler stressed the learner's activity: "It is what he does that he learns, not what the teacher does." His ideas grew from his work as head of evaluation for the Eight-Year Study of American high schools in the 1930s.

On the LET, a question that starts from objectives and ends with evaluation is describing Tyler's rationale.
Memory aid: "POOE — Purposes, Opportunities for learning, Organization, Evaluation."
