TESDA treats work ethics as a competency called **practice career professionalism**. It appears in every TLE trade, from cookery to dressmaking, and has three elements.

1. **Integrate personal objectives with organizational goals.** Pursue personal growth plans that improve your qualifications, keep good working relationships, and show commitment to the organization in your daily duties.
2. **Set and meet work priorities.** Rank competing demands so personal, team and organizational goals are met. Use human, financial and technological resources efficiently, and follow procedures for the **economical use and maintenance** of equipment and facilities.
3. **Maintain professional growth.** Take trainings, accept recognition, and obtain and **renew licenses and certifications**, such as TESDA National Certificates.

So an ethical HE worker is not only honest but also careful with the employer's resources and keeps their skills current.
