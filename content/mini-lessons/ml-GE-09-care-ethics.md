**Lawrence Kohlberg** studied how people reason about dilemmas such as the **Heinz dilemma**, whether a man should steal a drug to save his dying wife. He cared about the reasons given, not the yes or no answer, and his stages center on **justice**.

Psychologist **Carol Gilligan** (1982) noticed that people often approach moral problems from two perspectives: **justice** and **care**. In her research, men more often used justice and women more often used care. Her work led to **care ethics**, which puts relationships, responsibility and responsiveness to others' needs at the center. **Nel Noddings**, in *Caring* (1984), developed the idea further.

Non-Western traditions add more. **Daoism**, founded by **Laozi** around the sixth century BCE, centers on **wu wei**, effortless action in harmony with the Dao.

In class, care ethics shows up as attending to the particular learner, not only the rule.
