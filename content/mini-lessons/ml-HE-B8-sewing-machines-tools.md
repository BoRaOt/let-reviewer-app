A dressmaking shop uses four kinds of machines: the **single-needle lockstitch** machine for ordinary seams, the double-needle lockstitch, the **edging** machine for finishing raw edges, and the **buttonhole** machine.

Basic maintenance has three steps. Start, stop and watch the machine as the manufacturer directs. **Clean and lubricate** it according to the manufacturer's instructions. Check that it runs correctly, and **write down and report** any problem to the supervisor.

TESDA separates faults by seriousness. **Minor faults** such as loose threads, a broken needle or a control problem can usually be fixed by the operator. **Major faults** such as a defective electric motor, timing that is not synchronized, or broken body parts need a technician.

For drafting patterns, the standard measuring tools are the tape measure, hip curve rule, L-square and meter stick.
Memory aid: "Needle broke? Fix it. Motor or timing? Report it."
