At Earth's surface, the air is about **78 percent nitrogen**, **21 percent oxygen** and **1 percent argon**, with small amounts of water vapor, carbon dioxide and other gases, plus dust and droplets.

Most of the atmosphere lies in the bottom 10 kilometers, the **troposphere**. Warm air rises and cools there, so clouds, rain and storms form in this layer, and it is where airplanes fly.

Above it is the stratosphere. Near its top is the **ozone layer**. Ozone, O₃, is a form of oxygen with three atoms. It absorbs much of the Sun's harmful **ultraviolet** light, protecting living things from skin and eye damage. Regulating ozone-destroying chemicals called **CFCs** prevented far worse damage to this layer.

So, weather happens in the troposphere, and UV protection happens in the stratosphere.
