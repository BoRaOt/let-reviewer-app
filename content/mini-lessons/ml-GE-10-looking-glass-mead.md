Sociologists see the self as built through other people.

**Charles Cooley** (1864–1929) proposed the **looking-glass self** in 1902. We form our self-understanding partly from how we **imagine others see us**, as if others were a mirror. A learner praised as "good with her hands" may come to see herself as skilled.

**George Herbert Mead** (1863–1931) said the **self** develops through **social interaction** and requires the ability to see ourselves through others' eyes, which we are not born with. He described a path:
1. **Preparatory stage:** children only **imitate** caregivers.
2. **Play stage:** children take on the role of **one other person**, like playing teacher.
3. **Game stage:** children consider **several roles at once** and how they fit, as in a team sport.
4. Finally they grasp the **generalized other**: society's common expectations. Then they have a "self".
Memory aid: "Cooley = mirror; Mead = Imitate, Play, Game, Generalized other."
