Foodborne illness comes in two kinds, depending on the cause.
- **Food infection:** caused by live microbes such as bacteria.
- **Food intoxication:** caused by toxins, such as those some molds produce.

Pathogens include bacteria and viruses. Salmonella is one of the most common bacterial causes. It lives in the intestines of birds, reptiles and mammals and reaches people through meat, poultry, eggs, dairy and seafood. It brings fever, diarrhea and abdominal cramps within about twelve to seventy-two hours. Parasites matter too. Anisakis worms can come from raw fish, and Cryptosporidium can spread through contaminated drinking water. Molds are microscopic fungi that grow in warm, damp, humid conditions on fruit, grains, meat and dairy, often as gray or green fur. On the exam, a scenario about a toxin, rather than live germs, points to intoxication.
Memory aid: "Germs infect; toxins intoxicate."
