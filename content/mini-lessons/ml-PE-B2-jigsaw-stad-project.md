Three cooperative formats appear often on the LET.

**Jigsaw** (Aronson; Slavin): each member of a home group takes one part of a topic, meets with members of other groups who took the same part to become an **expert**, then returns to **teach** the home group. In a nutrition lesson, one learner becomes the expert on carbohydrates, another on proteins, another on fats.

**STAD**, Student Teams–Achievement Divisions (Slavin): the teacher presents a lesson, teams of four or five study together, and each learner takes a test alone. Scores count partly by **improvement**, so every member helps teammates improve.

**Project-based learning** (Katz): a question or problem of interest is investigated by students working together over an **extended period**, ending in a **product**, presentation or written report, such as a family meal plan with costing.
Memory aid: "Jigsaw = experts teach back; STAD = team improvement points; Project = long work, final product."
