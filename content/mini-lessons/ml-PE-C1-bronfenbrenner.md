Urie Bronfenbrenner pictured the child at the center of nested systems.
- **Microsystem:** the settings the child directly experiences, such as family, classmates, teachers and health workers.
- **Mesosystem:** the connections between those settings. A parent–teacher conference is the classic example.
- **Exosystem:** settings the child never enters that still affect them. A parent's new work-from-home policy changes life at home, for instance.
- **Macrosystem:** the widest layer: culture, values, religion, the economy and national laws.
- **Chronosystem:** time. All the layers change together through historical events and life transitions.

A common exam trap mixes up the mesosystem and the exosystem. If two of the child's own settings are talking to each other, it is the mesosystem. If a setting the child is not part of affects the child, it is the exosystem.
Memory aid: "My Mom Expects Many Chores" — Micro, Meso, Exo, Macro, Chrono.
