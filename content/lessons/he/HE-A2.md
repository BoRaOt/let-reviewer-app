# HE-A2 — Home Economics literacy, family and consumer life skills

Annex F gives this exploratory course **11 items** (Remember 1, Understand 2, Apply 5, Analyze 2, Evaluate 1). Its outcomes list: why HE matters; hygiene and grooming; nutritious meals; household resource management; fabrics and textiles; sewing a simple garment and repairs; marriage and family; family roles, functions and life cycle; informed buying; saving and managing income; conflict resolution and parenting basics. [SRC-0002, Annex F, A.2]

This is the **introductory** version of the HE major. The deeper versions are HE-B1 (home management, consumer laws), HE-B2 (food and nutrition), HE-B4 (family and child) and HE-B6 (clothing). This lesson covers what those do not: hygiene, fibers and fabrics, simple sewing and care, saving, and family conflict.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Why HE matters.** Home Economics teaches the everyday skills of a household: keeping clean, feeding a family well, managing money and things, dressing, and getting along. At the exploratory level, learners try each area before choosing a specialization.

**Hygiene and food safety.** Clean hands stop germs moving from person to person and from raw food to cooked food. The four steps of safe food handling are **clean, separate, cook, chill**.

**Fibers and fabrics.** Fabric is made from fibers. Fibers are **natural** (from plants, animals or minerals), **synthetic** (made from petrochemicals, like nylon and polyester), or **semi-synthetic** (natural cellulose that is chemically processed, like rayon). Plant fibers are classed by the part of the plant they come from.

**Philippine fabrics.** The Philippines is known for **piña** (pineapple leaf) and **abacá** (Manila hemp) fabrics, used in the barong tagalog. A 2004 law requires Philippine tropical fabrics for government uniforms.

**Simple sewing and care.** Check the fabric, follow its grain when laying patterns, sew, then finish (hems, closures) and press at the right heat. Know the common machine faults.

**Money.** Budget first, then save. A savings account is flexible but earns little interest. Keep an emergency fund.

**Family.** Marriage under the Family Code is a permanent union and the foundation of the family. Parent–teen conflict is normal, usually mild, and part of how roles are renegotiated.

## 2. Testable facts

**Hygiene and food handling**
- The four key steps in handling, preparing and serving food: **clean, separate, cook, chill**. [SRC-0108, Ch. 17 "Efforts on the consumer level"]
- Wash hands with **warm, soapy water for at least 20 seconds** before preparing food and after handling raw foods; this prevents cross-contamination and person-to-person spread. [SRC-0108, Ch. 17 "Efforts on the consumer level"]
- Never thaw meat, poultry or seafood at **room temperature**; thaw in the refrigerator, microwave, or a sealed bag in cold water. [SRC-0108, Ch. 17 "Efforts on the consumer level"]
- A cutting board can be sanitized with about **1 teaspoon (5 mL) of chlorine bleach per 1 litre of water**; use separate boards for produce and raw meat. [SRC-0108, Ch. 17 "Efforts on the consumer level"]

**Fibers**
- Fibers fall into three groups: **natural, synthetic, semi-synthetic**. Natural fibers come from plants, animals and minerals (e.g. cotton, wool, silk, linen). [SRC-0141, paragraph on fiber categories]
- Plant fibers by source: **seed** fiber (cotton); **leaf** fiber (pineapple, abacá/banana); **bast** fiber from the stem's outer layer (flax, jute, ramie, kenaf, hemp). [SRC-0140, "Plant fibers"]
- **Animal fibers are proteins** (e.g. keratin in wool, fibroin in silk). **Silk** is secreted by insects making cocoons. [SRC-0140, "Animal fibers"]
- **Rayon (viscose)** is regenerated cellulose; the first successful viscose process dates from **1894** (Cross, Bevan and Beadle). [SRC-0141, "The first synthetic fibers"]
- **Nylon** was the first fully synthetic fiber, developed by **Wallace Carothers** at DuPont (nylon 66 first made in 1935). [SRC-0141, "The first synthetic fibers"]
- Of all fibers, **cotton** dominates textile production and use among natural fibers. [SRC-0140, "Industrial use"]

**Philippine fabrics**
- **Piña** is a traditional Philippine fiber from **pineapple leaves**, woven into sheer *nipis* fabric for the barong tagalog and baro't saya. [SRC-0142, lead and "History"]
- **Aklan piña handloom weaving** was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in **2023**. [SRC-0142, infobox]
- **Abacá** (*Musa textilis*, "Manila hemp") is a banana species **endemic to the Philippines**; the **Bicol region** has the most cultivars. [SRC-0143, lead and "Genetic diversity"]
- Per the Fiber Industry Development Authority (as reported), the Philippines supplied about **87%** of the world's abacá in 2014. [SRC-0143, "Cultivation"]
- **RA 9242 (2004), Philippine Tropical Fabrics Law:** tropical fabrics are those containing **natural fibers produced, spun, woven or knitted and finished in the Philippines**; they are prescribed for **official uniforms of government officials and employees**. [SRC-0050, Secs. 2–3]
- RA 9242 funds the **Fiber Industry Development Authority (FIDA)** of the Department of Agriculture to promote plant-fiber production; the **Civil Service Commission** issues the implementing rules. [SRC-0050, Secs. 5–6]

**Fabric care and simple sewing (TESDA Dressmaking NC II)**
- Before cutting, fabric is checked for **quality, faults, width and selvedges**, and soaked or drip-dried and pressed according to standard fabric care. [SRC-0080, "Prepare and cut materials", element 1 and range of variables]
- Patterns are laid out and pinned **according to the fabric grain line**, and seam allowances are marked. [SRC-0080, "Prepare and cut materials", element 2]
- Standard fabric care methods listed: **dry cleaning, soaking and drying, machine washing, tumble-drying**. [SRC-0080, "Prepare and cut materials", range of variables 3]
- Common sewing-machine faults: **puckering, uneven stitch, loose threads, skipping stitch**. [SRC-0080, "Sew casual apparel", range of variables 2]
- Pressing tools: **flat iron, ironing board, sleeve board, press mitt, tailor's ham**. Heat levels given: **Level 1 silk, Level 2 cotton, Level 3 maong (denim)**. [SRC-0080, "Apply finishing touches", range of variables 2 and 4]
- Finishing touches include **closures, hemming, accessories and accents, stitches**. [SRC-0080, "Apply finishing touches", range of variables 1]

**Saving and managing income**
- Three main savings vehicles: **savings account, certificate of deposit (time deposit), money market account**. [SRC-0090, 6.6]
- A savings account is **flexible** (deposits and withdrawals anytime) but earns **low interest**, so it is poor for growing money. [SRC-0090, 6.6]
- Experts recommend keeping savings to cover **3 to 6 months of living expenses** as an emergency fund. [SRC-0090, 6.6]
- A budget starts by listing income, then **categorizing expenses** (housing, food, transport…) and comparing the two. [SRC-0090, 6.5]

**Marriage, family and conflict**
- The Family Code defines marriage as a **special contract of permanent union** between a man and a woman, the **foundation of the family** and an inviolable social institution. [SRC-0071, Art. 1]
- An **extended family** household includes at least one parent and child plus other relatives such as grandparents, aunts, uncles and cousins. [SRC-0088, 14.1]
- Contrary to popular belief, many families get along well in adolescence; warmth and closeness are especially common in **authoritative** households. [SRC-0087, 10.5]
- Parent–adolescent conflict is linked to teens' growing **autonomy**, new reasoning skills and changing roles; it can both drive and result from the redefinition of the relationship. [SRC-0087, 10.5]

**Basic weaves (Stage 7 addition; Wikipedia, tertiary)**
- The **three fundamental weaves** are **plain, twill and satin**. [SRC-0256, lead; SRC-0257, lead]
- **Plain weave** is the most basic (simple over-under); fabrics are generally **strong and durable** with a smooth surface. [SRC-0256, lead]
- **Twill** has a pattern of **parallel diagonal ribs**; it is used for sturdy work clothing, and **denim is a twill**. [SRC-0257, lead]
- **Satin weave** has **long floats** (yarns floating over four or more crossing yarns), giving a **glossy, smooth** face and a dull back; it is **not very durable** because floats snag. [SRC-0255, lead]

**Fabric construction and finishes (Stage 7 addition; Wikipedia, tertiary)**
- **Weaving** interlaces two sets of yarns **at right angles**: lengthwise **warp** and crosswise **weft**. [SRC-0267, lead]
- **Knitting** interloops a **continuous length of yarn** into rows of **connected loops**. [SRC-0264, lead]
- **Felt** is made by **matting, condensing and pressing fibers** together (no weaving or knitting); in wet felting, hot water with **agitation and compression** makes wool fibers mat. [SRC-0265, lead and "Wet felting"]
- **Mercerisation** treats cotton (and flax) to improve **dye uptake and tear strength**, reduce shrinkage and give a **silk-like luster**; devised by **John Mercer in 1844**. [SRC-0266, lead and "Development"]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Rayon is not "fully synthetic".** It is semi-synthetic (regenerated cellulose). Nylon was the first fully synthetic fiber.
- **Piña vs abacá.** Piña comes from the *pineapple* leaf; abacá from a *banana* relative. Both are leaf fibers. Cotton is a *seed* fiber; flax/linen and jute are *bast* (stem) fibers.
- **Silk and wool are protein fibers**, not cellulose. (Relevant to care: protein fibers need low heat; silk is pressing Level 1.)
- **Thawing on the counter** is wrong, even if it is "faster".
- **Savings account = safe and flexible, not high-growth.**
- **Teen–parent conflict** is not a sign that a family is failing; constant severe conflict is the exception.
- **Denim is twill, not plain weave.** Satin's shine comes from floats, not from the fiber.
- **Felt is non-woven.** Hot water plus agitation is exactly what mats (felts) wool, which is why wool is washed gently in cool water.

## 4. Memory aid

**Fibers "SLB-P":** **S**eed = cotton, **L**eaf = piña/abacá, **B**ast = flax/jute; **P**rotein = silk/wool.
**Food safety "Clean, Separate, Cook, Chill."**
**Weaves "Plain = Plus sign, Twill = Tilt, Satin = Slide"** (over-under, diagonal, long floats).
**"Weave = Cross, Knit = Loop, Felt = Press."**
