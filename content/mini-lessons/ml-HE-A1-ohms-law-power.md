Three formulas solve most basic electricity problems. **Ohm's law: V = IR.** Voltage (volts) equals current (amperes) times resistance (ohms). Rearrange it as I = V/R or R = V/I.

**Power: P = IV.** A device drawing 2 amperes from a 220-volt outlet uses 440 watts. Combining with Ohm's law also gives P = V²/R and P = I²R.

**Energy: E = Pt.** Electric companies bill energy in **kilowatt-hours**. A 1,000-watt iron used for 2 hours uses 2 kWh. If electricity costs 12 pesos per kWh, that costs 24 pesos. Always convert watts to kilowatts by dividing by 1,000, and minutes to hours by dividing by 60, before multiplying.

These skills appear in home economics too, when learners compare the running cost of appliances such as rice cookers, refrigerators and electric fans.
Memory aid: "VIR and PIV — then kilowatts times hours times price."
