The **Organic Agriculture Act (RA 10068, 2010)**, amended by **RA 11511 (2020)**, promotes farming that is ecologically sound, socially acceptable, economically viable and technically feasible. Organic farming sharply reduces outside inputs by **not using chemical fertilizers, pesticides or pharmaceuticals**. Instead it builds soil life and long-term fertility, increases biodiversity, and **recycles plant and animal wastes** as compost.

A farm does not become organic overnight. The **conversion period** is the time from starting organic management to being certified.

Certification can be **first party** (the producer checks itself), **second party** (buyers set the rules) or **third party** (an independent body checks). RA 11511 added the **Participatory Guarantee System (PGS)**, a local, trust-based system run by organic practitioners themselves. Produce certified by a third party or by PGS may be labelled organic. The National Organic Agricultural Board sits under the Department of Agriculture.
Memory aid: "No synthetic sprays, feed the soil, convert, then certify (third party or PGS)."
