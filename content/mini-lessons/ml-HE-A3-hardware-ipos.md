Every computer, from a phone to a server, follows the same **information processing cycle**: **input** (typing, scanning), **processing** (the computer works on the data), **output** (screen or printout), and **storage** (saving for later).

Inside, the **motherboard** connects everything and sits in the system unit. The **CPU** holds the microprocessor, the computer's brain, which has a control unit and an arithmetic-logic unit.

Memory comes in kinds. **RAM** is primary, short-term memory. It holds what you are working on and clears when the power goes off; more RAM means faster work. **ROM** holds the hardwired start-up instructions used before the operating system loads. **Secondary storage**, such as hard disks and flash drives, keeps files permanently.

All of it is stored as 1s and 0s. One binary digit is a bit, and eight bits make one byte.
