The Family Code (Executive Order 209) calls marriage a special contract of permanent union and the foundation of the family. A valid marriage needs two kinds of requisites.
- **Essential requisites** concern the persons: legal capacity of the contracting parties, a male and a female, and consent freely given in the presence of the solemnizing officer.
- **Formal requisites** concern the procedure: the solemnizing officer's authority, a valid marriage license (except in cases the Code exempts), and a ceremony where the couple appear before the officer and declare they take each other as husband and wife, before at least two witnesses of legal age.

The minimum age to marry is eighteen. If any essential or formal requisite is missing, the marriage is void from the beginning, with one narrow exception the Code names.
Memory aid: "Essence is the couple; form is the ceremony."
