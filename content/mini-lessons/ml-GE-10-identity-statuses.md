**Erik Erikson's** fifth stage, **identity versus role confusion**, usually runs from about ages **12 to 18**. Adolescents explore different roles and identities to form a stable, whole sense of who they are.

**James Marcia** built on Erikson by asking two questions: Has the person **explored** options? Has the person **committed** to an identity? The answers give four statuses.
- **Identity achievement:** explored, then committed.
- **Identity foreclosure:** committed **without** exploring, often by adopting parents' choices.
- **Identity moratorium:** actively exploring but not yet committed, an "identity crisis". Many adolescents spend much time here.
- **Identity diffusion:** little exploration and low commitment; the person feels lost and is not seeking an identity, close to Erikson's role confusion.

A student teacher who chose education only because a parent insisted, without considering other paths, would be in foreclosure.
Memory aid: "Explore + Commit = Achieve; Commit only = Foreclose; Explore only = Moratorium; Neither = Diffuse."
