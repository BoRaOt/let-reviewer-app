When a learner misbehaves, the most effective consequences are closely tied to the behavior itself.

A **natural consequence** happens on its own, without anyone deciding it. A learner who comes late misses the instructions and struggles with the task. A learner who rushes through cooking burns the food.

A **logical consequence** is decided by someone else, such as the teacher, but has an obvious link to the act. A learner who steals a classmate's lunch pays for it. A learner who leaves the kitchen station dirty cleans it before the next activity. A learner who misses class work because of chatting makes it up later as homework.

In real life the two often mix: in a fight, injury may be natural, losing friends logical. Research finds both can reduce misbehavior because learners can see why the consequence follows. Unrelated punishments, such as extra math for talking, teach less.
Memory aid: "Nature happens; Logic is given, but it fits."
