Countries have been grouped in several ways. **Cold War terminology** (1945–1980) used **first, second and third world**, based on economic development and living standards. The **World Bank** now groups countries by **gross national income per capita** into high-, middle- and low-income nations. High-income countries face **capital flight**, when capital moves abroad, and **deindustrialization**.

Immanuel **Wallerstein's world-systems approach (1979)** sees the global economy as a hierarchy.
- **Core nations** are dominant capitalist countries, highly industrialized, technological and urbanized.
- **Semi-peripheral nations** are in between: not powerful enough to dictate policy, but major sources of raw materials and growing middle-class markets for the core, while also exploiting the periphery. Mexico is an example.
- **Peripheral nations** have very little industrialization, often only outdated factories or ones owned by core nations.

Conflict theorists focus on how core nations exploit peripheral resources.
Memory aid: "Core commands, Semi-periphery supplies, Periphery provides."
