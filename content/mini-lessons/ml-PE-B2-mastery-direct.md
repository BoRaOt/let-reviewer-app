**Mastery learning** aims for **all learners to reach the same high level**, accepting that some need more time. A typical cycle: a short lesson, then an **ungraded** check. Learners who have mastered it get **enrichment**; others get **tutoring** or extra materials until they master it, then take another test. Two challenges are fairness, since slower learners may get only dull drills, and the need for a detailed curriculum broken into small units.

**Direct instruction** usually means a **highly scripted** form of mastery learning. It mixes behaviorism, such as immediate and explicit praise for correct answers, with cognitive ideas, such as stating objectives in advance and frequent review. Content comes in small, logical steps with lots of practice. Research finds it very effective for **basic skills** such as early reading and arithmetic, especially from kindergarten to Grade 3.
Memory aid: "Mastery = same goal, different time; Direct = small steps, scripted, praised."
