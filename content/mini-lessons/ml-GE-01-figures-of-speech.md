Figures of speech create effects beyond literal meaning.
- **Simile:** compares unlike things using **like** or **as**: "Her smile was like sunrise."
- **Metaphor:** compares without *like* or *as*: "The classroom was a zoo."
- **Personification:** gives human traits to non-human things: "The moon smiled down."
- **Hyperbole:** deliberate exaggeration: "I've told you a million times."
- **Metonymy and synecdoche:** refer to something by a related object or by a part: "Malacañang announced…" for the President's office; "all hands on deck".
- **Alliteration:** repeats an initial consonant sound: "Peter Piper picked…"
- **Onomatopoeia:** words that imitate sounds: *buzz*, *tik-tak*.
- **Oxymoron:** a two-word paradox of opposites: "deafening silence".
- **Irony:** language presenting something opposite to what is expected or meant.

Quick tests: *like/as* → simile; impossible exaggeration → hyperbole; object acting human → personification.
Memory aid: "Like = simile, Is = metaphor, Lives = personification, Lots = hyperbole."
