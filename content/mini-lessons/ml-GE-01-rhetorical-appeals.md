Persuasive messages use four rhetorical appeals, as the OpenStax Writing Guide explains.

**Ethos** is a believable, authoritative voice that earns the audience's **credibility** and trust. A health message delivered by a doctor, or a mascot made to seem like a trustworthy parent figure, uses ethos.

**Pathos** appeals to **feelings and emotions** the audience shares, such as fear of being left out or love for family.

**Logos** presents **credible information, facts, reasons or examples** as evidence leading to a sensible conclusion, such as statistics on handwashing and illness.

**Kairos** is a sense of **appropriate timing**: the right message at the right moment, like a snack ad shown when people are likely to be hungry.

In a rhetorical analysis, identify which appeals a text uses and how well they suit its audience and situation.
Memory aid: "Ethos = trust me, Pathos = feel this, Logos = here's proof, Kairos = right now."
