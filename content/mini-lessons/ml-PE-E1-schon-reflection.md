**Donald Schön's** book *The Reflective Practitioner* (1983) explained how professionals improve through a kind of practiced improvisation. He described two types of reflection.

**Reflection-in-action** happens **while** you are acting. A student teacher sees puzzled faces during a demonstration, realizes the steps are too fast, and slows down and adds a visual on the spot.

**Reflection-on-action** happens **after** the event. That evening, the same student teacher writes in a journal about what went wrong, what went right, and what to do differently next time.

For Schön, professional growth begins when a practitioner starts to **doubt** his or her own actions and to frame situations as problems to solve, rather than accepting routines without question. Field study journals, post-observation conferences and video review all build both kinds of reflection.
Memory aid: "In = during the lesson; On = after the lesson."
