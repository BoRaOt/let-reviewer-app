# GE-10 — Understanding the Self

GenEd unit, **15 items** (10%). Outcomes: explain the **factors that shape self-identity**; apply **self-understanding** to a career in teaching. [SRC-0001, Annex A, GE]

## 1. Plain explanation

**Philosophy asks what the self is.** **Descartes**: the mind is a *thinking thing*, separate from the body (dualism). **Locke**: personal identity rests on a thinking being that is conscious of itself as the same over time. **Hume**: the self is a **bundle of perceptions** in constant flux. **Buddhism**: *anatman*, no permanent self. Hinduism: *atman*.

**Sociology says the self is built socially.** **Cooley's looking-glass self**: we see ourselves as we imagine others see us. **Mead**: the self develops through social interaction, from imitation to play to game to the **generalized other**. Family is the **first agent of socialization**; peers, school, media and workplaces follow.

**Psychology describes the self and personality.** **Rogers**: self-concept; **congruence** between real and ideal self; **unconditional positive regard**. **Maslow**: self-actualization at the top. **Big Five** traits: openness, conscientiousness, extroversion, agreeableness, neuroticism. Neo-Freudians: Adler (inferiority), Jung (collective unconscious), Horney, Erikson.

**Culture** shapes the self: individualist cultures prize independence; collectivist cultures prize harmony and group needs.

**Identity in adolescence:** Erikson's identity vs role confusion; Marcia's four statuses.

**Well-being:** the **Mental Health Act** (RA 11036) defines mental health and protects rights.

## 2. Testable facts

**Philosophical self (OpenStax Introduction to Philosophy 6.2)**
- **René Descartes (dualism):** the mind is a **thinking thing (*res cogitans*)**; the body is an **extended, non-thinking thing (*res extensa*)**. [SRC-0089, 6.2 "Rene Descartes and Dualism"]
- **John Locke** (1632–1704) described a person as "a thinking intelligent Being, that has reason and reflection, and can consider it self as itself, the same thinking thing in different times and places." [SRC-0089, 6.2]
- **David Hume** (1711–1776), **bundle theory**: the self is "a bundle or collection of different perceptions… in a perpetual flux and movement". [SRC-0089, 6.2 "Bundle Theory"]
- In Hindu traditions the self is **atman**; Buddhism's **anatman** denies a permanent self. [SRC-0089, 6.2 "Hindu and Buddhist Views of Self"]

**Sociological self (OpenStax Sociology 3e 5.1, 5.3)**
- **Charles Cooley** (1864–1929): self-understanding is constructed partly from our perception of **how others view us** — "the **looking glass self**" (1902). [SRC-0088, 5.1]
- **George Herbert Mead** (1863–1931): the **self** is a distinct identity developed through **social interaction**; it requires seeing oneself through others' eyes, which is learned, not inborn. [SRC-0088, 5.1]
- Mead's path: **preparatory stage** (imitation only) → **play stage** (taking the role of one other person) → **game stage** (considering several roles at once) → understanding the **generalized other**, society's common expectations. [SRC-0088, 5.1]
- **Family is the first agent of socialization**; others include **peer groups**, **media**, and formal institutions such as **schools, workplaces and government**. [SRC-0088, 5.3]

**Psychological self (OpenStax Psychology 2e 11.3–11.8)**
- **Carl Rogers:** **self-concept** = our thoughts and feelings about ourselves; **congruence** occurs when the real self and ideal self are very similar; parents help by giving **unconditional positive regard** (unconditional love). [SRC-0086, 11.5]
- **Abraham Maslow** studied healthy, creative, productive people; the highest need is **self-actualization**, achieving one's fullest potential. [SRC-0086, 11.5]
- **Big Five (Five Factor Model):** **openness to experience, conscientiousness, extroversion, agreeableness, neuroticism**. [SRC-0086, 11.7]
- **Trait theorists:** **Allport** found about **4,500** English words describing people; **Cattell** identified **16** factors; the **Eysencks** proposed two dimensions, **extroversion/introversion** and **neuroticism/stability**, rooted in temperament. [SRC-0086, 11.7]
- **Neo-Freudians:** **Alfred Adler** (individual psychology; drive to compensate for **feelings of inferiority**), **Carl Jung** (**collective unconscious**), **Karen Horney** (rejected "penis envy"; growth blocked by **basic anxiety** from unmet needs), **Erik Erikson**. [SRC-0086, 11.3]
- **Culture:** individualist cultures value **independence, competition and personal achievement**; collectivist cultures value **social harmony, respectfulness and group needs** over individual needs; Asian cultures tend to be more collectivist. [SRC-0086, 11.8]

**Identity (Lifespan Development 10.1)**
- Erikson's fifth stage, **identity vs role confusion** (about ages **12–18**), involves exploring roles to form a stable identity. [SRC-0087, 10.1]
- **James Marcia's identity statuses** (exploration × commitment): **achievement** (explored, then committed), **foreclosure** (committed without exploring), **moratorium** (exploring, not yet committed — an "identity crisis"), **diffusion** (low commitment, not seeking). [SRC-0087, 10.1]

**Mental health (RA 11036, Mental Health Act, 2018)**
- **Mental health** is a state of well-being in which a person realizes their own abilities, copes with normal stresses, shows resilience, works productively and contributes to the community. [SRC-0040, Sec. 4(j)]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Looking-glass self is Cooley**, not Mead. Mead = role-taking stages and the generalized other.
- **Hume denies a fixed self** (bundle); **Descartes affirms a thinking self** (cogito, dualism).
- **Congruence (Rogers)** = real self close to ideal self — not "conformity to others".
- **Neuroticism** is a Big Five trait (emotional instability), not a disorder.
- **Foreclosure vs achievement:** both are committed, but foreclosure skipped exploration.
- **Family is the first agent of socialization**, not school.
