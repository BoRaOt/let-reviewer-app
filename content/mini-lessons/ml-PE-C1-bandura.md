Albert Bandura showed that people can learn by watching others, not only from their own rewards and punishments. This is observational learning, also called modeling. His work stressed that humans are social. We live, learn and develop among other people, and we both influence them and are influenced by them. In a classroom, the teacher is always a model. A Home Economics teacher who washes hands, sets out tools neatly and handles knives safely teaches those habits by example, often more powerfully than by lecture. Students also learn from peers they watch, which is why showing a good example from a classmate can work. On the exam, a scenario where a learner copies a behavior after seeing someone else do it, and receives no direct reward, points to Bandura.
