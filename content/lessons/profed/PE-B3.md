# PE-B3 — Educational technology, resources and classroom management

Section B is 45 items; our working split gives this line about **15 items**. Outcomes: strategies, materials, technology and **classroom management**, including for **learners from indigenous groups**; use of **conventional and non-conventional resources including ICT**. [SRC-0001, Annex A, ProfEd B] HE-A7 covers TPACK, CRAAP, gamification and adaptive learning; this lesson adds media selection, management and IP education.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Choosing resources.** Edgar Dale's **Cone of Experience** orders media from **concrete** (direct experience) to **abstract** (verbal symbols). It is a guide to concreteness, not a retention chart. Technology helps most when it fits content and pedagogy (TPACK).

**Managing a class is mostly prevention.** Arrange space for learning, set **procedures** (routines for repeated tasks) and a few **rules** (standards of behavior), plan **transitions**, and stay **"withit"** — aware of everything going on — so you can deal with several things at once (**overlapping**).

**Responding to misbehavior** goes from mild to strong: ignore minor behavior that will not spread; use gestures, eye contact and proximity; use **natural and logical consequences**; for conflicts, use Thomas Gordon's steps: decide **who owns the problem**, **active listening**, **assertive I-messages**, then **negotiation**.

**Indigenous learners.** Under IPRA (RA 8371), the State provides an integrated, culturally relevant education to IP children and respects their right to education in their own language and cultural ways of teaching.

## 2. Testable facts

**Media and technology**
- **Edgar Dale** introduced the **Cone of Experience** in **1946** (revised **1954, 1969**) as an intuitive model of the **concreteness** of audiovisual media. Dale included **no percentages** and warned against taking it too literally; the "remember 10% of what you read" numbers came later (Treichler, 1967). [SRC-0148, "Cone of Experience"]
- TPACK: effective integration needs the overlap of **technological, pedagogical and content knowledge**. [SRC-0144, lead]
- Even a **single classroom computer** can present assignments, enrich students with special interests, and give extra review; more technology helps the teacher move from "full-frontal teaching" to "guide on the side". [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 "New trend #2"]
- Gamification uses game elements (points, badges, leaderboards, feedback, levels); adaptive learning uses algorithms to tailor content to each learner. [SRC-0146, lead; SRC-0147, lead]

**Preventing problems (Seifert & Sutton Ch. 7)**
- **Arranging space:** whatever the layout, it should help students focus and minimize distraction. Displays should reinforce curriculum and recognize student work, but **too many make a room busy**; decorate some space and leave some for later. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Arranging classroom space", "Displays and wall space"]
- **Procedures/routines** are specific ways of doing common, repeated tasks (e.g. handing in homework); they are like **social conventions**. **Rules** express **standards of behavior** for which students take personal responsibility. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Establishing daily procedures and routines", "Establishing classroom rules"]
- Keep rules **few** (the sample lists five) and general. There is a **stronger case for involving students in making rules** than in making procedures, because rules concern personal responsibility. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Establishing classroom rules"]
- **Transitions** are times of lost time and likely misbehavior; plan them. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Managing transitions"]
- **Withitness (Kounin, 1970):** awareness of everything happening in the classroom at once; experienced teachers show it more. It makes **overlapping** possible — responding to several nearly simultaneous events. Merely showing you are "withit" can deter off-task behavior. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "withitness" passage]
- **Ripple effect (Kounin):** misbehavior left alone can be contagious. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Responding to student misbehavior"]

**Responding to misbehavior**
- **Ignoring** may be wise when the behavior is minor and unlikely to disrupt others. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Ignoring misbehaviors"]
- **Gestures, eye contact and body language** can redirect students with little or no talk; young children and students from other cultures may misread nonverbal cues. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Gesturing nonverbally"]
- **Natural consequences** happen without anyone's intention (a late student misses material). **Logical consequences** are imposed by others but clearly related to the act (a student who steals a lunch repays it). Both can be effective. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Natural and logical consequences"]
- **Thomas Gordon, Teacher Effectiveness Training:** first decide **problem ownership** — the owner is the person primarily troubled by the behavior (student, teacher, or another student), who takes primary responsibility for solving it. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Step 1: clarifying and focusing: problem ownership"]
- **Active listening:** attend to all of what a student says and keep checking your understanding with questions. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Step 2: active, empathetic listening"]
- **Assertive discipline and I-messages:** comments should be **assertive** (neither passive nor hostile; Canter) and use **I-messages** (Gordon), which state how the behavior affects the teacher, e.g. "Your talking is making it hard for me to remember what I'm saying." [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Step 3: assertive discipline and I-messages"]
- **Negotiation** for persistent conflicts: define the problem (with active listening), **brainstorm solutions with the student**, choose and try one, and evaluate. [SRC-0109, Ch. 7 "Step 4: negotiation"]

**Indigenous peoples' education (IPRA, RA 8371)**
- The State, through the **NCIP**, provides a complete, adequate and **integrated system of education relevant to the needs** of IP children and youth. [SRC-0036, Sec. 28]
- The State respects IPs' right to **preserve and protect their culture, traditions and institutions**. [SRC-0036, Sec. 29]
- The State provides **equal access** to educational opportunities **without prejudice** to IPs' right to establish and control their own educational systems, providing education **in their own language** and in a manner **appropriate to their cultural methods of teaching and learning**; IP children have the right to all levels and forms of State education. [SRC-0036, Sec. 30]

**Safe classrooms**
- The Anti-Bullying Act requires schools to adopt anti-bullying policies (see PE-A2). [SRC-0025, Sec. 3]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Dale's Cone is not a retention pyramid.** No percentages are Dale's.
- **Procedures ≠ rules.** Lining up for lunch is a procedure; "respect others' property" is a rule.
- **Withitness vs overlapping.** Withitness is awareness; overlapping is handling two things at once.
- **Natural vs logical.** Natural = no one imposes it; logical = someone imposes it, but it fits the act.
- **I-message vs You-message.** "I can't hear the group when there's talking" (I-message) vs "You're so noisy" (You-message).
- **Problem ownership first.** If only the teacher is bothered, the teacher owns the problem.
- **IP education under IPRA** includes the right to the IP's own language and cultural methods, not only "access to regular schools".
