subtopic: Norm-referenced vs criterion-referenced interpretation
source_ids: [SRC-0109]
---
The same test score can be read in two ways.

A **criterion-referenced** interpretation compares a learner with a **standard**. It describes what the learner can or cannot do: "Alisha mastered 65 percent of the Grade 2 problems." Mastery checks, competency assessments and most classroom quizzes work this way.

A **norm-referenced** interpretation compares a learner with **other learners**, the norm group. It reports rank, such as a percentile: "Alisha scored higher than 75 percent of the norm group." Entrance tests and ranking for honors use this logic.

For planning instruction, teachers usually need to know what learners can and cannot do, so **criterion-referenced** information is generally more useful. Standards-based accountability also relies mostly on criterion-referenced tests. Norm-referenced tests are now used more for diagnosis and placement, for example of learners with special needs.
Memory aid: "Criterion = against the Content; Norm = against the Neighbors."
