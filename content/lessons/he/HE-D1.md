# HE-D1 — Basic research in Home Economics

Annex F gives this line **8 items** (Remember 1, Understand 1, Apply 4, Analyze 1, Evaluate 1). Outcomes: **parts of a research paper**; **action research methods** in HE; **using research findings** to improve HE teaching. [SRC-0002, Annex F, D.1] ProfEd E2 (action research) overlaps.

## 1. Plain explanation

**A research article** usually follows **IMRaD**: Introduction (why, research question), Methods (who, when, where, how), Results (what was found), Discussion (what it means and what next).

**Research vocabulary.** The **population** is everyone you want to know about; the **sample** is the group you actually study. A **parameter** describes the population; a **statistic** describes the sample. Data are **qualitative** (categories, words) or **quantitative** (counts and measurements).

**Sampling.** Random methods are simple random, **stratified** (random within each subgroup), **cluster** (randomly pick whole groups), and **systematic** (every nth). **Convenience** sampling is non-random and can be biased.

**Experiments.** The **explanatory (independent)** variable is what you change; the **response (dependent)** variable is what you measure. A **control group** and **blinding** guard against bias; **lurking variables** can confuse results.

**Action research** is teacher research on one's own class: plan (identify the problem, review literature, plan data collection) → act (collect and analyze data) → develop an action plan → communicate and reflect, then repeat.

**Ethics.** Get **informed consent** when data will be shared outside the school; participation in research is voluntary; protect privacy.

## 2. Testable facts

**Parts of a research paper**
- **IMRaD = Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion**, the most common structure for original research articles. [SRC-0168, lead]
- Introduction: why the study was done, the research question or hypothesis. Methods: who, when, where and how, materials and participants. Results: what answer was found; was the hypothesis supported. Discussion: implications, fit with other research, future research. [SRC-0168, "Overview"]
- The IMRaD flow is often pictured as a **"wine glass" (hourglass) model**: from general (introduction) to specific (methods, results) and back to general (discussion). [SRC-0168, "Overview"]
- Other common elements outside the acronym include **disclosure statements** (funding, conflicts of interest) and **ethical compliance statements**. [SRC-0168, "Other elements"]

**Population, sample and data (OpenStax Introductory Statistics 2e)**
- A **statistic** is a number describing a property of the **sample**; a **parameter** describes the **population**. [SRC-0091, 1.1]
- **Qualitative (categorical) data** come from categorizing or describing attributes, usually words or letters; **quantitative data** come from counting or measuring and are always numbers (discrete or continuous). Pie charts and bar graphs display qualitative data. [SRC-0091, 1.2]
- **Stratified sample:** divide the population into strata and take a proportionate random sample from **each** stratum. **Cluster sample:** divide into clusters and randomly select **some clusters**, taking all their members. **Systematic sample:** random start, then **every nth** item from a list. [SRC-0091, 1.2]
- **Convenience sampling** uses readily available results; it is **non-random** and may be highly biased. [SRC-0091, 1.2]

**Experimental design**
- The **explanatory variable** causes change in the **response variable**; **lurking variables** are other variables that can cloud the study, so the explanatory variable must be isolated. [SRC-0091, 1.4]
- A **control group** balances the effects of being in an experiment; **blinding** counters the power of suggestion; in a **double-blind** experiment both subjects and the researchers dealing with them are blinded. [SRC-0091, 1.4]
- Participants must give **informed consent**. [SRC-0091, 1.4 "Ethics"]

**Action research**
- **Kurt Lewin** coined "action research" in **1944**; he described "a spiral of steps, each of which is composed of a circle of **planning, action and fact-finding** about the result of the action". [SRC-0169, lead]
- **Action research (teacher research):** investigation by teachers of their own students or their own teaching, leading to **concrete decisions** that improve teaching and learning in a particular context. [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 "New trend #4: increased professionalism of teachers"]
- Compared with ordinary good teaching, action research is **planned more thoughtfully, recorded more systematically, and shared more openly** with colleagues. [SRC-0109, Ch. 1, after Table 1]
- Mertler's cycle: **Planning** (identify and define the problem; review related literature; develop a research plan of what data, how and when) → **Acting** (collect and analyze data; keep it manageable) → **Developing an action plan** (make and document changes) → **Communicating and reflecting** ("What did I learn? What should I do next?"), which starts a new cycle. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Cycles of planning, acting and reflecting"]
- Data in action research can include student work, observation, **videotaping one's own lessons**, a **journal diary**, and **interviews**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 Table 1]

**Ethics and privacy**
- If action-research data will be shared **outside the school community**, permission from **parents or guardians and students** is needed, usually via an **informed consent form** that describes the study and data, states participation is **voluntary**, and guarantees **confidentiality or anonymity**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Ethical issues"]
- Students may **choose not to participate** in research, unlike ordinary class tasks. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Ethical issues"]
- Learners' **education and health** information is sensitive personal information under RA 10173. [SRC-0045, Sec. 3(l)]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Methods vs Results.** "We surveyed 40 Grade 8 learners using a 20-item checklist" is Methods; "68% preferred…" is Results; "This suggests that…" is Discussion.
- **Stratified vs cluster.** Stratified samples from *every* group; cluster takes *whole* groups chosen at random.
- **Convenience sampling is not random**, even if it is easy and large.
- **Statistic ↔ sample; parameter ↔ population.** (S–S, P–P.)
- **Independent = explanatory; dependent = response.**
- **Action research starts with a classroom problem**, not with a hypothesis from a textbook; it ends with action and reflection, not only a report.
- **Consent:** research participation is voluntary; class activities are not.

## 4. Memory aid

**"I Must Report Discoveries"** = Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion.
**Action research "Plan–Act–Change–Share/Reflect" (PACS).**
**Sampling "Strata = Some of every group; Cluster = Choose whole groups."**
