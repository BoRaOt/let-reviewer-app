---
Action research data come from ordinary classroom life, gathered more systematically. Seifert and Sutton's examples include: **assessing students' assignments**; **observing** students as they work; **interviewing** students about their experiences; **videotaping** your own lessons; and keeping a **journal diary** of your observations.

Analysis looks for **patterns**: common strengths and problems across several students, or differences in how you interact with different groups. The teacher then tries a change and watches what happens. Results are shared in a brief written or oral report to fellow staff, a summary in the journal, or a talk with students.

A few research-design ideas help interpret results honestly. A comparison or **control group** shows whether change came from the new method. **Lurking variables**, such as a holiday week, can cloud results. A **convenience sample**, like only the learners who stay after class, may be biased.
Memory aid: "Work, Watch, Ask, Record, Write — then look for patterns."
