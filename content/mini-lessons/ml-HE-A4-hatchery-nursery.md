A **hatchery** breeds fish or shellfish and raises them through their earliest life stages; a **nursery** grows the larvae into juveniles big enough to stock farms. Hatcheries matter because they give a supply outside the natural spawning season, allow genetic improvement through selective breeding, and reduce dependence on wild-caught fry.

The steps run in order. First, **broodstock conditioning**: keep adult breeders in tanks with the right light, temperature, salinity, water flow and food so they produce good eggs. Second, **spawning**, which may be natural or induced by stripping eggs and milt, thermal shock, or hormone injection. Third, **fertilization**. Fourth, **larval rearing** in the nursery with proper feeding.

The law protects wild breeders: catching or possessing mature milkfish, called **sabalo**, and other spawners is unlawful except for allowed breeding or research.
