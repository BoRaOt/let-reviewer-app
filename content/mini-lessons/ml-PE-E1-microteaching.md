**Microteaching** is a teacher-training technique invented in **1963 at Stanford University** by **Dwight W. Allen**. It simplifies the complex job of teaching so one skill can be practiced and improved.

In the original form, a trainee prepared a **short lesson**, usually about **20 minutes**, for a **small group** of learners. The lesson was **video-recorded**. Afterwards the trainee, fellow trainees, a master teacher and the learners watched the recording together and commented on what worked and what could improve, with reference to the lesson's objectives. The trainee could then reteach.

Everything is **scaled down**: class size, time, task and content. That makes feedback specific, for example on questioning or giving directions. John Hattie's review of research found microteaching among the most effective methods for improving student outcomes.

In teacher education, microteaching usually comes before full teaching internship.
Memory aid: "Small class, short lesson, recorded, reviewed, retaught."
