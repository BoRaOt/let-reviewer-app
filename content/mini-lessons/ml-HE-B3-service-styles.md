TESDA's Food and Beverage Services standard names three serving styles. In **American service**, also called plate service, food is plated in the kitchen and carried to each guest. In **Russian service**, or platter service, food comes to the table on platters and is served onto plates. In **buffet service**, guests serve themselves from a display table. A school cafeteria line works like a buffet with servers behind the counter.

The **sequence of service** runs: greet, seat, take beverage orders, present the menu, take food orders, serve food, crumb the table, serve coffee or tea, then present the bill. Soon after serving, staff do a **"3-minute check"** to confirm guests are satisfied and refill condiments.

Closing down means clearing soiled dishes, handling food scraps hygienically, cleaning and storing equipment, resetting tables, and switching off electrical equipment.
Memory aid: "American = Already plated; Russian = platteR; Buffet = By yourself."
