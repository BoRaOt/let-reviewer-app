# HE-A4 — Agriculture and Fishery Arts I and II (exploratory)

Annex F gives this exploratory course **11 items** (Remember 1, Understand 2, Apply 5, Analyze 2, Evaluate 1). Outcomes: **crop production** (organic agriculture, practices, site selection); **aquaculture** (systems, hatchery and nursery, water quality, responsible fish capture); **animal production** (breeds, livestock and poultry management); **pest management** (preventive and corrective measures, organic and conventional protection). [SRC-0002, Annex F, A.4]

## 1. Plain explanation

**Organic agriculture** (RA 10068, amended by RA 11511) grows food without chemical fertilizers, pesticides and drugs, and builds soil fertility, biodiversity and recycling of farm wastes. Products are certified organic by **third-party certification** or a **Participatory Guarantee System (PGS)**.

**Good crop practice** includes **crop rotation** (not monocropping), which cuts pests and weeds and balances soil nutrients; legumes add nitrogen.

**Aquaculture** is raising fish and other aquatic species in fresh, brackish or marine water. In the Philippines the top products are **milkfish (bangus), tilapia, tiger shrimp, seaweed and mud crab**. Systems include **ponds, pens and cages**, bivalve farming on bamboo stakes, and seaweed lines.

**Hatcheries** breed fish and rear larvae; **nurseries** grow the larvae to juveniles (fingerlings) for stocking.

**Responsible fishing** means protecting municipal waters, mangroves, reefs and spawners. The Fisheries Code bans fishing with **explosives, poison or electricity**, **fine-mesh nets**, **muro-ami**, and converting **mangroves** into fishponds.

**Integrated pest management (IPM)** combines cultural, mechanical, biological and chemical controls, and sprays only when pests pass an **action threshold**.

**Animal production** in the Philippines includes the **carabao** (swamp buffalo, draft animal) and poultry raised as **layers** (eggs) or **broilers** (meat), in cages, barns or free-range.

## 2. Testable facts

**Organic agriculture (RA 10068 as amended by RA 11511)**
- **Organic agriculture** includes all systems that promote the ecologically sound, socially acceptable, economically viable and technically feasible production of food and fibers; it **dramatically reduces external inputs by refraining from chemical fertilizers, pesticides and pharmaceuticals**. [SRC-0054, Sec. 3(b)]
- An organic production system aims to **enhance biodiversity**, **increase soil biological activity**, **maintain long-term soil fertility**, **recycle plant and animal wastes** to return nutrients to the land, rely on renewable resources, and minimize pollution of soil, water and air. [SRC-0054, Sec. 3(c)]
- **Conversion period:** the time between the start of organic management and the certification of crops, livestock or aquaculture products as organic. [SRC-0054, Sec. 3(d)]
- Certification types: **first party** (producer sets and checks its own rules), **second party** (buyers or industry set rules), **third party** (an independent body checks compliance). [SRC-0054, Sec. 3(j)–(l)]
- RA 11511 (2020) adds the **Participatory Guarantee System (PGS)**: a locally focused quality-assurance system run by people engaged in organic agriculture, built on trust, social networks and knowledge exchange. [SRC-0055, Sec. 2 amending Sec. 3(s)]
- Products certified by **third-party certification or PGS** may be labelled and sold as **organic**. [SRC-0055, amended Sec. 18]
- The **National Organic Agricultural Board (NOAB)**, attached to the **Department of Agriculture**, is chaired by the **Secretary of Agriculture**. [SRC-0054, Secs. 6–7]

**Crop practices**
- **Crop rotation** grows different crops in sequence on the same land; it reduces dependence on one set of nutrients, pest and weed pressure, and resistant pests. **Monocropping** depletes nutrients and builds specialized pests. [SRC-0162, lead]
- Rotations may aim at weed control, **increasing soil nitrogen** (e.g. with legumes), erosion control or soil structure. [SRC-0162, "Crop choice"]

**Aquaculture**
- **Aquaculture:** fishery operations raising and culturing fish and other species in **fresh, brackish and marine** waters. [SRC-0056, Sec. 4(3)]
- Top five Philippine aquaculture commodities: **milkfish, tilapia, jumbo tiger shrimp, seaweed, mud crab**. Tilapia, carp and catfish are usually freshwater; shrimp and crab brackish; seaweed and green mussel marine; **milkfish grows in fresh, brackish and salt water**. [SRC-0158, "Products farmed"]
- **Fish pens** use the seabed or lakebed as their floor; **fish cages** have an artificial bottom, cost less to build but need more feeding. **Brackish fishponds** are the traditional method, often multi-pond for milkfish. [SRC-0158, "Fish ponds", "Fish pens and cages"]
- Mussels and oysters are grown on **bamboo stakes**; sale is threatened by **red tide**. Seaweed (Eucheuma, Kappaphycus, Caulerpa) is profitable even on small farms; first harvest in about 105–135 days. [SRC-0158, "Bivalves", "Seaweed"]
- Public lands such as tidal swamps, mangroves and foreshore suitable for fishery **may not be disposed of**; a **Fishpond Lease Agreement (FLA)** runs **25 years, renewable for another 25**, primarily for qualified fisherfolk cooperatives. [SRC-0056, Sec. 45]
- Many **mangrove forests** were converted into brackish ponds, often in violation of the law. [SRC-0158, "Land use"]
- In onshore aquaculture, humans control living conditions such as **water quality (oxygen)**, feed and temperature. [SRC-0163, lead]

**Hatchery and nursery**
- A **hatchery** breeds, hatches and rears fish and shellfish through early life stages; juveniles go to on-growing farms. Benefits: **out-of-season supply, genetic improvement, less dependence on wild-caught fry**. [SRC-0159, lead and "Purpose"]
- Steps: **broodstock conditioning** (optimal light, temperature, salinity, flow, food) → **spawning** (natural or induced by stripping, thermal shock or hormone injection) → **fertilization** → **larval rearing in nurseries** to juvenile size. [SRC-0159, "Production steps"]

**Responsible fishing (Fisheries Code RA 8550 as amended by RA 10654; FAO Code)**
- **Municipal waters** extend **15 km** from the coastline; **municipal fishing** uses vessels of **3 gross tons or less** (or no vessel). [SRC-0056, Sec. 4(57)–(58)]
- A **closed season** is a period when taking specified species with specified gear is prohibited in an area. [SRC-0056, Sec. 4(8)]
- Unlawful: fishing with **explosives, noxious or poisonous substances, or electricity**; possession of explosives for illegal fishing. [SRC-0057, Sec. 92]
- Unlawful: nets with **mesh smaller** than DA-set limits (exceptions for gathering fry and similar). [SRC-0057, Sec. 93]
- **Muro-ami** and methods that pound coral reefs are banned; owners and operators face fines of five times the catch value or **₱2 million**, whichever is higher. [SRC-0057, Sec. 97]
- Unlawful to **convert mangroves into fishponds** or for any other purpose. [SRC-0057, Sec. 99]
- Unlawful to catch or possess mature milkfish (**sabalo**) and other breeders/spawners, except for local breeding or research as allowed. [SRC-0057, Sec. 103]
- FAO: **selective and environmentally safe gear** and practices should be developed and given priority, to maintain biodiversity and protect fish quality. [SRC-0111, Art. 6.6]

**Pest management**
- **IPM** (FAO definition): careful consideration of all pest-control techniques, integrating **biological, chemical, physical and cultural** measures to grow healthy crops and **minimize pesticide use** and risks. [SRC-0157, lead]
- First line of defense: **cultural practices** — resistant varieties, healthy crops, crop rotation, intercropping. Then **mechanical** controls: hand-picking, water spraying, barriers, **traps**. [SRC-0157, "Prevention"]
- **Action (economic) threshold:** controls are applied only when pests exceed a level where damage would cost more than treatment; **monitoring** (scouting, traps) is essential. [SRC-0157, "Monitoring"]
- **Biological control** promotes beneficial predators and parasites; **augmentative** biocontrol increases natural enemies. Biopesticides such as **Bt** count as chemical controls. [SRC-0157, "Biological controls", "Chemical controls"]
- IPM's six repeating steps: **sample/monitor → identify → learn pest biology → set action threshold → choose tactics → evaluate**. [SRC-0157, "Process"]
- In Southeast Asian rice, early spraying killed spiders, frogs and wasps that prey on the **brown planthopper** and bred resistance; IRRI encourages planting flowers and vegetables on paddy banks to attract natural enemies. [SRC-0157, "Southeast Asia"]

**Animal production**
- The **carabao** is a genetically distinct **swamp-type water buffalo** of the Philippines, the traditional **draft animal** for paddy rice farming, also used for milk, meat and hide. [SRC-0160, lead and infobox]
- Poultry farming raises chickens, ducks, turkeys and geese for **meat or eggs**; egg-laying hens may be kept **free-range**, in barns, or in **battery cages**; meat chickens (**broilers**) are mostly raised indoors. [SRC-0161, lead and sections]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Organic ≠ "no inputs at all".** Organic farming refrains from *synthetic chemical* fertilizers, pesticides and pharmaceuticals; it uses compost and recycled wastes.
- **PGS vs third-party.** PGS is community-run assurance by practitioners; third-party is an independent certifier. Both allow the "organic" label after RA 11511.
- **Pen vs cage.** A pen uses the natural bottom; a cage has its own net bottom.
- **Hatchery vs nursery.** Hatchery = breeding and hatching; nursery = growing larvae into fingerlings.
- **Municipal waters = 15 km**, not 15 nautical miles; small boats are 3 gross tons or less.
- **IPM does not ban pesticides.** It uses them last, after thresholds are passed.
- **Mangrove conversion is illegal**, even for aquaculture.
