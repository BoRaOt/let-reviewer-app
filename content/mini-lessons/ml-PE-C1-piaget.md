Jean Piaget described four stages of thinking.
- **Sensorimotor (about 0–2):** babies learn through their senses and actions and develop object permanence, the knowledge that things still exist when hidden.
- **Preoperational (about 2–6 or 7):** children use words and images and enjoy pretend play, but their reasoning is not yet logical and they are egocentric, finding it hard to take another person's view.
- **Concrete operational (about 7–11):** children reason logically about real, concrete things and master conservation, for example that pouring water into a taller glass does not change the amount.
- **Formal operational (about 12 and up):** abstract and hypothetical reasoning becomes possible.

Most high school learners are in the last stage, so "what if" problems, debates and moral reasoning suit them. Exam items test the order and the hallmark of each stage more than the exact edge ages.
