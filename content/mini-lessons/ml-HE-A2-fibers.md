Every fabric starts as fiber. Fibers come in three groups. **Natural fibers** come from plants, animals or minerals. **Synthetic fibers** are made entirely from chemicals; nylon, developed by Wallace Carothers at DuPont in the 1930s, was the first fully synthetic one, and polyester followed. **Semi-synthetic fibers** start from natural cellulose that is chemically processed; rayon, also called viscose, is the classic example.

Plant fibers are named after the part of the plant they come from. **Seed** fibers come from seeds, like cotton. **Leaf** fibers come from leaves, like pineapple (piña) and abacá. **Bast** fibers come from the outer layer of the stem, like flax (linen), jute and ramie.

Animal fibers are **proteins**: wool and other animal hair contain keratin, and silk is spun by insects making cocoons. Protein fibers need gentle heat.
