Seifert and Sutton note that the most common special need is a **learning disability**: an impairment in a specific aspect of learning, especially **reading**. **ADHD**, attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, is a problem with **sustaining attention and controlling impulses**. Less common are speech and language disorders and cognitive disabilities.

Inclusive teaching asks three things of teachers: provide **alternative ways of assessment**, arrange a learning environment that is as normal, or **least restrictive**, as possible, and help create **individual educational plans (IEPs)**. (See PE-A1 for RA 11650, the Philippine inclusion law.)

Learners also differ in **cognitive style**. **Field-dependent** learners perceive things globally and often like working with others; **field-independent** learners prefer analytic tasks and working alone. Be careful with the label "learning styles": people are less consistent across situations than the term suggests.
