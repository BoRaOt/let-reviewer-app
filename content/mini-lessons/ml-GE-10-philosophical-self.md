Philosophers disagree about what a "self" is.

**René Descartes** was a **dualist**. He called the mind a **thinking thing**, *res cogitans*, and the body an **extended, non-thinking thing**, *res extensa*. The self, for him, is above all the thinking mind.

**John Locke** tied personal identity to consciousness: a person is a thinking, reasoning being that can consider itself "the same thinking thing in different times and places". Remembering yourself as the same person over time is what makes you you.

**David Hume** disagreed. He proposed **bundle theory**: when he looked inward, he found only "a bundle or collection of different perceptions" in constant flux, never a fixed self behind them.

Eastern traditions differ too. Hindu thought speaks of **atman**, the true self, while Buddhism teaches **anatman**, the denial of a permanent self.
