A **register** is a variety of language used for a particular purpose or situation. Registers form a spectrum, but linguist **Martin Joos** described five styles of spoken English that are often tested.
1. **Frozen:** fixed wording that never changes, such as the Panatang Makabayan, oaths and scripture.
2. **Formal:** one-way, no interruptions, precise or technical vocabulary, such as a graduation speech or a conference talk.
3. **Consultative:** two-way; background is explained; listeners give "uh huh, I see"; interruptions allowed. Teacher–student and doctor–patient talk are typical.
4. **Casual:** among friends; little background given; slang and shortened sentences common.
5. **Intimate:** private talk with family and close friends, where tone matters more than grammar and non-verbal cues carry meaning.

Purposive communication means matching register to audience and purpose: a parent conference is consultative, not casual.
