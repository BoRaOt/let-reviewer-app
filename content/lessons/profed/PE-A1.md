# PE-A1 — Foundations: social foundations and inclusive education

The outline asks you to "apply philosophical and sociological principles in teaching-learning situations" and "apply foundation theories of special and inclusive education" [SRC-0001, ProfEd Section A, outcomes 1.1–1.2]. This lesson covers the **sociological** foundations, the **legal and historical** foundations, and **inclusive education** under Philippine law. *Philosophies of education* are pending (see the gaps note at the end).

## 1. Plain explanation

**Three sociological lenses** explain what schools do.
- **Functionalism** sees education as a key social institution with *manifest* functions (intended and visible, such as socialization and social control) and *latent* functions (hidden and unintended, such as friendships, networks and teamwork).
- **Conflict theory** sees schooling as widening inequality, through the *hidden curriculum* and *tracking*.
- **Symbolic interactionism** studies classroom interaction and labeling, which can create self-fulfilling prophecies. **Feminist theory** examines gender inequality in education.

**Inclusive education** is now law. RA 11650 (2022) requires every public and private early and basic education school to admit and serve learners with disabilities. It uses tools such as the **Child Find System**, the **Individualized Education Plan (IEP)** and **Inclusive Learning Resource Centers (ILRCs)**, which replace the old SPED Centers.

**The Constitution** guarantees the right to quality education. It requires free public elementary and high school education, compulsory elementary education, and encouragement of non-formal, informal and indigenous learning systems.

**Philippine public schooling** began under the American regime. Act No. 74 (1901) created a Department of Public Instruction, made primary instruction free, and made English the basis of instruction.

## 2. Testable facts

**Sociology of education**
- **Functionalists** view education as one of the most important social institutions. It has **manifest (primary)** functions, which are intended and visible, and **latent (secondary)** functions, which are hidden and unintended. [SRC-0088, 16.2]
- The first manifest function is **socialization**. **Émile Durkheim** called schools "socialization agencies" that teach children to get along with others and prepare them for adult economic roles. [SRC-0088, 16.2]
- **Social control** is a manifest function: schools teach conformity to law and respect for authority. [SRC-0088, 16.2]
- **Latent functions** include courtship, social networks that can later help with jobs, and the ability to work with others in small groups. [SRC-0088, 16.2]
- **Conflict theorists** view education as widening social inequality. The **hidden curriculum** is the nonacademic knowledge learned through informal learning and cultural transmission; it favors those with more cultural capital. [SRC-0088, 16.2]
- **Tracking** sorts students into advanced and low tracks. Conflict theorists argue it perpetuates inequality and leads to self-fulfilling prophecies. [SRC-0088, 16.2]
- **Symbolic interactionists** study classroom dynamics and interactions between students and teachers. Labeling, such as calling a student a "low achiever", can become a **self-fulfilling prophecy**. [SRC-0088, 16.2]
- **Feminist theory** studies the roots and effects of gender inequality in education. [SRC-0088, 16.2]

**Inclusive education (RA 11650 and related laws)**
- **Inclusive education** is the process of responding to the diversity of needs of all learners, toward full participation, presence and achievement. It involves **accommodation, modification, adaptation and individualization** in content, approaches, structures and strategies. [SRC-0032, Sec. 4]
- The **policy of inclusion** covers all early and basic education schools, **public and private**. No learner may be **denied admission on the basis of disability**. [SRC-0032, Sec. 5]
- **IEP:** systematic, purposive, developmental programming designed to meet the educational requirements of a learner with disability. It includes the services, their duration and frequency, and the learner's present level. [SRC-0032, Sec. 4]
- DepEd and LGUs shall establish at least one **ILRC** in every city and municipality. **All existing SPED Centers are converted and renamed "Inclusive Learning Resource Center".** [SRC-0032, Sec. 6]
- The ILRC implements the **Child Find System**, identifying, locating and evaluating learners with disabilities who are not receiving education services, and helps include them in the general education system. [SRC-0032, Sec. 7]
- RA 11650's objectives include free and appropriate public early and basic education for learners with disabilities and access to the general education system. [SRC-0032, Sec. 3]
- **Filipino Sign Language** is declared the national sign language of the Filipino deaf (RA 11106). [SRC-0035, title]
- The **Alternative Learning System Act** (RA 11510) institutionalizes ALS for out-of-school children in special cases, youth and adults. [SRC-0037, title]

**Constitutional and historical foundations**
- The State shall protect and promote the right of all citizens to **quality education at all levels** and make it accessible to all. [SRC-0007, Art. XIV Sec. 1]
- The State shall maintain **free public education** in the elementary and high school levels; **elementary education is compulsory** for all children of school age, without limiting parents' natural right to rear their children. [SRC-0007, Art. XIV Sec. 2(2)]
- The State shall encourage **non-formal, informal and indigenous learning systems**. [SRC-0007, Art. XIV Sec. 2(4)]
- **Act No. 74 (1901)** established a Department of Public Instruction. **All primary instruction** in its schools was **free**, and **English** was to be made the basis of all public school instruction "as soon as practicable". [SRC-0011, Secs. 1, 14]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Manifest vs latent.** Intended and visible (socialization, social control, transmitting culture) = manifest. Unintended by-products (dating, friendships, networking) = latent.
- **Hidden curriculum vs latent function.** Both are "unofficial". The *hidden curriculum* is a **conflict-theory** idea about values and status that reproduce inequality.
- **Labeling or self-fulfilling prophecy** belongs to **symbolic interactionism**. Tracking-as-inequality belongs to **conflict theory**.
- **SPED Center vs ILRC.** Under RA 11650 all SPED Centers became ILRCs. "Inclusion" means learners with disabilities in general education with support, not separate schooling by default.
- **Free vs compulsory.** The Constitution makes public elementary *and* high school *free*, but makes *elementary* education *compulsory*.

## 4. Memory aid

**"Function, Fight, Feel-the-label"**: Functionalism (what schools do for society), conflict (who wins and loses), interactionism (labels in the classroom).
**"SPED → ILRC"**: one per city or municipality, running the Child Find System.
