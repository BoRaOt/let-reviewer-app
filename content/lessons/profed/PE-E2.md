# PE-E2 — Action research

Part of ProfEd Section E (30 items); our working split gives this line about **12 items**. Outcome: identify **teaching-learning problems** and **recommend solutions based on research**. [SRC-0001, Annex A, ProfEd E] HE-D1 covers IMRaD, sampling and variables; this lesson goes deeper on what action research is, its cycle, data and ethics.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Action research (teacher research)** is systematic, intentional inquiry by teachers into their **own** practice to improve it. It starts from a real classroom problem, produces knowledge about **this** class, and is shared mainly with **other teachers**.

**The cycle (Mertler):** **plan** (identify the problem, review literature, plan data collection) → **act** (collect and analyze data) → **develop an action plan** (make and document changes) → **communicate and reflect** — then begin again. Lewin, who coined the term in 1944, called it a spiral of planning, action and fact-finding.

**Data** can be simple: student work, observation, video of your own lessons, a teaching journal, interviews. Keep it manageable.

**Ethics.** When results are shared outside the class: protect **privacy** (pseudonyms, change identifying details), get **informed consent**, and respect **freedom to participate**. A teacher's first duty is the students' welfare.

## 2. Testable facts

**Nature of action research (Seifert & Sutton Appendix C; Ch. 1)**
- **Action research** (teacher research) is "systematic, intentional inquiry by teachers for the purpose of **improving their own practice**"; it is **not** the same as research **about** teaching by professional researchers. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "The nature of action research"]
- Defining characteristics: (1) it **originates in problems and dilemmas of classroom practice**; (2) its outcomes are about **particular teachers and classrooms**, not teachers in general; (3) its audience tends to be **other teachers**, giving "insider" perspectives. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "The nature of action research"]
- Supporters argue that focusing on specific contexts makes action research **more credible as practical information**, being attuned to real classrooms. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "The nature of action research"]
- Common assumptions: teaching is itself a **form of research**; action research requires **substantial reflection**; **collaboration** among teachers is crucial; teachers' knowledge should be **shared publicly**. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "Action research in practice"]
- Compared with ordinary good teaching, action research is **planned more thoughtfully, recorded more systematically, and shared more openly**; it reflects and raises teacher professionalism. [SRC-0109, Ch. 1, after Table 1]
- **Kurt Lewin** coined "action research" in **1944**; in 1946 he described "a spiral of steps, each of which is composed of a circle of **planning, action and fact-finding** about the result of the action". [SRC-0169, lead]

**The cycle (Mertler, in Seifert & Sutton Ch. 11)**
- **Planning stage:** (1) **identify and define the problem** — often starting as vague unease and turned into a researchable question; (2) **review related literature**; (3) **develop a research plan** (what data, how and when). [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Cycles of planning, acting and reflecting"]
- **Acting stage:** **collect and analyze data**; data and analyses need not be complex — action research must be **manageable**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Cycles of planning, acting and reflecting"]
- **Developing an action plan:** make and implement changes; **document** actions carefully. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Cycles of planning, acting and reflecting"]
- **Communicating and reflecting:** share results with colleagues, in classes, at conferences or in teachers' journals; reflect ("What did I learn? What should I have done differently? What next?"), which starts a **new cycle**. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Cycles of planning, acting and reflecting"]

**Data sources (Seifert & Sutton Ch. 1, Table 1)**
- Examples of data: **assessing students' assignments**, **observing** students, **interviewing** students, **videotaping one's own teaching**, a **journal diary** by the teacher. Analysis looks for **patterns**, common strengths and problems, then tries changes and observes the result. Results may be reported in a **brief written or oral report to staff**, a journal summary, or shared with students. [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 Table 1]
- Example questions: "How successful are my students at finding high-quality information online?" and "Am I responding to my ESL students as helpfully as to my English-speaking students?" [SRC-0109, Ch. 1 Table 1]
- Report structure for publication commonly follows **IMRaD** (Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion). [SRC-0168, lead]
- Design vocabulary: explanatory vs response variables, **control group**, **lurking variables**, **convenience vs random samples**. [SRC-0091, 1.2, 1.4]

**Ethics (Seifert & Sutton Appendix C; Ch. 11)**
- Possible **conflict of interest**: a teacher's first priority is **students' welfare**; a researcher's is the field of study. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "Ethical cautions about action research"]
- Ethical issues concern **privacy, informed consent, and freedom to participate**, and arise when results are made **public** or shared outside the classroom. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "Ethical cautions about action research"]
- **Privacy:** use a **pseudonym** and change identifying details in any report or even hallway conversation, without changing essential information. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "Insuring privacy of the student"]
- **Informed consent** form: summarizes the research and data, states participation is **voluntary**, guarantees **confidentiality or anonymity**; students may **decline** research participation even though class work is required. [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Ethical issues—privacy, voluntary consent"]
- Learners' education and health data are **sensitive personal information** (RA 10173). [SRC-0045, Sec. 3(l)]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Action research vs educational research.** Action research is by the teacher, on their own class, to improve their practice; it does not aim to generalize to all schools.
- **Starts with a problem, not a hypothesis from the literature.** The literature review comes after defining the problem.
- **Ends in action and reflection**, not only a report.
- **Ethical rules apply when results go public**; class assessment for teaching does not need consent, research participation does.
- **Pseudonyms are required even in informal talk** about the study.

## 4. Memory aid

**Mertler cycle "Plan → Act → Action plan → Share & Reflect" ("PAAS").**
**Ethics "PIF": Privacy, Informed consent, Freedom to participate.**
