Feedback turns assessment into learning. Seifert and Sutton give three guidelines.

**Make it prompt.** The longer the gap between the work and the feedback, the longer learners keep their misconceptions, and the less they remember what they were thinking when they did the task.

**Make it specific.** Tell learners clearly what they did well and what needs changing. Comments like "good work" or "needs improvement" do not show how to improve. A well-designed rubric helps by naming strengths and weaknesses criterion by criterion.

**Keep the grade from drowning the message.** Grades are often required, but placing the grade after the comments, or on the last page, helps learners read the comments first. Letting learners keep grades private also reduces comparison.

When reporting to parents, the same rule applies: describe what the child can do and what to work on next, not only the number.
