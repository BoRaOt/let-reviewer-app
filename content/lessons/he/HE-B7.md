# HE-B7 — Workplace safety, occupational health standards and ethics in HE

Annex F gives this line **5 items** (Understand 2, Apply 2, Analyze 1). [SRC-0002, Annex F, B.7] Items are likely to be situations in an HE workplace: a kitchen, a canteen, a salon, a sewing room.

HE-A5 already covers the four OSH worker rights (know, refuse, report, PPE) and TESDA's hazard types. This lesson adds **how a workplace organizes safety** (program, committee, safety officer, training, welfare facilities, penalties), the **Sanitation Code rules for food workers**, **salon safety**, and **professional ethics**.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Organizing safety (RA 11058).** Every covered workplace needs a written **OSH program**, an **OSH committee**, a **safety officer**, health personnel, training, and basic welfare facilities. The employer pays for all of this as part of normal operating costs. Employers who ignore OSH standards face daily fines.

**Food workers (PD 856, Sanitation Code).** A food establishment needs a **sanitary permit** from the local health office, posted where people can see it. Every worker needs a **health certificate**. Food handlers wear clean clothes and caps or hairnets, keep good hygiene, and wash and dry hands before work and after using the toilet.

**Salons (TESDA Beauty Care).** Keep walkways clear, remove spills and hair promptly, disinfect linen, store and check tools, rotate stock, and lift safely.

**Ethics and professionalism (TESDA "Practice career professionalism").** Align personal goals with the organization's, prioritize work, use resources economically, keep learning, and keep licenses and certificates current.

## 2. Testable facts

**OSH program, committee and officer (RA 11058)**
- Covered workplaces must have a **safety and health program** that includes, among others: a commitment to comply; a **drug-free workplace**; HIV/AIDS, tuberculosis and hepatitis prevention; PPE; safety signage; **toolbox meetings**; accident investigation and reporting; an **emergency preparedness and response plan**; and waste management. [SRC-0063, Sec. 12]
- The program is prepared by the employer **in consultation with workers**, submitted to **DOLE**, and made available to everyone in the workplace. [SRC-0063, Sec. 12]
- The **OSH committee**: the **employer or representative is chairperson**; the **safety officer is secretary**; members include contractor safety officers, health personnel (ex officio) and **workers' representatives** (from the union, or elected by simple majority if there is no union). [SRC-0063, Sec. 13]
- A **safety officer** is trained by DOLE and tasked to implement the program. Duties: oversee the program, monitor and inspect, assist government inspectors, and **issue work stoppage orders when necessary**. [SRC-0063, Secs. 3(n), 14]
- All workers must take the mandatory **eight-hour safety and health seminar** required by DOLE. [SRC-0063, Sec. 16(b)]
- Welfare facilities required: safe **drinking water**, sanitary and washing facilities, living quarters where applicable, and **separate** sanitary, washing and sleeping facilities for men and women where applicable. [SRC-0063, Sec. 19]
- The employer, project owner, contractor and anyone who manages or supervises the work are **jointly and solidarily liable** for compliance. [SRC-0063, Sec. 21]
- If work stops because of an imminent danger caused by the **employer's** violation, the employer **pays the workers' wages** during the stoppage. [SRC-0063, Sec. 23]
- Willful non-compliance: administrative fine **not exceeding ₱100,000 per day** until corrected, counted from notice of the violation. Retaliating against workers who gave information to inspectors carries an added ₱100,000 fine. [SRC-0063, Sec. 28(a)–(b)]

**Food establishments (PD 856, Code on Sanitation)**
- No food establishment may operate for public patronage without a **sanitary permit** from the local health office; the permit is **posted in a conspicuous place**. A "food establishment" is where food or drinks are manufactured, processed, stored, sold or served. [SRC-0059, Sec. 14(a)–(b)]
- After a change of owner or occupant, the new occupant must have the change **noted within 14 days**. [SRC-0059, Sec. 14(d)]
- No person may be employed in a food establishment without a **health certificate** from the local health authority, issued after physical and medical exams and required immunizations. [SRC-0059, Secs. 15, 19(a)]
- Food handlers must wear **clean working garments**; the **cook wears a prescribed cap**, and female employees wear caps or **hairnets**; they observe good personal hygiene. [SRC-0059, Sec. 19(b)]
- Food handlers wash hands with **soap and water** and dry them with a clean or disposable towel or hand dryer **immediately before working and after visiting the toilet**. [SRC-0059, Sec. 19(b)(iii)]

**Kitchen OHS (TESDA Cookery NC II)**
- The Cookery TR's OHS unit lists required emergency-related drills: fire drill, earthquake drill, basic life support/CPR, first aid, spillage control, disaster preparedness. [SRC-0075, OHS unit, range of variables 5]
- Safety regulations listed in the OHS unit include the Clean Air Act, the Building Code, the **National Electrical and Fire Safety Codes**, waste-management rules and the Philippine OSH Standards. [SRC-0075, OHS unit, range of variables 1 (safety regulations)]

**Salon safety (TESDA Beauty Care NC II, "Maintain a safe, clean and efficient work environment")**
- Comply with health regulations: apply salon policies on **personal hygiene** and follow state and local **health regulations**. [SRC-0079, safe work environment unit, element 1]
- Keep reception, work areas and **walkways uncluttered**; store and dispose of waste per OHS requirements; **promptly remove spills, hair and other hazards** from floors; store, clean and **disinfect linen**. [SRC-0079, safe work environment unit, element 2]
- Store tools safely, prepare them for each service, check maintenance needs and **refer for repair**; carry out **stock rotation** and record stock levels; use **safe lifting and carrying** techniques. [SRC-0079, safe work environment unit, elements 3–4]

**Professional ethics (TESDA "Practice career professionalism")**
- Elements: (1) integrate personal objectives with organizational goals; (2) set and meet work priorities; (3) maintain professional growth and development. [SRC-0075, career professionalism unit]
- Setting priorities includes using resources **efficiently** and following practices on the **economic use and maintenance of equipment and facilities**. [SRC-0075, career professionalism unit, element 2]
- Professional growth includes obtaining and **renewing licenses and certifications** (e.g. National Certificates, Certificates of Competency, professional licenses). [SRC-0075, career professionalism unit, element 3 and range of variables 5]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Chair vs secretary.** The *employer* chairs the OSH committee; the *safety officer* is secretary.
- **Who pays?** OSH program costs and PPE are the employer's; wages during an imminent-danger stoppage caused by the employer are paid.
- **Sanitary permit vs health certificate.** The *establishment* gets a sanitary permit (posted); each *worker* gets a health certificate.
- **Hairnets and caps** are a Sanitation Code requirement, not just a "best practice".
- **Safety officers can stop work.** That is an explicit duty.
- **Ethics in TESDA terms** is about goals, priorities, economical use of resources and keeping certifications, not only "honesty".

## 4. Memory aid

**"Permit on the wall, certificate in the wallet."** (Sanitary permit is posted; health certificate is personal.)
**OSH committee "Boss chairs, Officer writes, Workers sit."**
