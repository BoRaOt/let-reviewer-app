Why are some countries poor? Two theories dominate.

**Modernization theory** says low-income countries are held back by a **lack of industrialization**. They can improve by **adjusting cultural values and attitudes toward work** and by **industrializing** and pursuing economic growth. Critics call it **ethnocentric**: it assumes every country has the same resources, can follow the same Western path, and wants to be "developed" in the same way.

**Dependency theory** arose partly as a reply. It says global inequality is caused mainly by **core nations exploiting** semi-peripheral and peripheral nations, keeping them dependent as suppliers of cheap labor and raw materials. Critics note that some former low-income nations, such as China, have risen to major economic power.

On the exam, "the problem lies within the poor country" points to modernization; "the problem lies in exploitation by rich countries" points to dependency.
Memory aid: "Modernize yourself vs Depend on others' exploitation."
