# PE-E1 — Field study and teaching internship

ProfEd Section E (Field Study, Teaching Internship and Action Research) is **30 items** (20%); our working split gives this line about **18 items** and PE-E2 about 12. Outcomes: **experiential learning**, **teaching assistantship**, **portfolio**, and **reflective teaching**.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Field study and internship are experiential learning.** You learn by doing, then reflecting, then making sense of it, then trying again — **Kolb's cycle**: concrete experience → reflective observation → abstract conceptualization → active experimentation.

**Reflection is the engine.** Schön distinguishes **reflection-in-action** (while teaching) from **reflection-on-action** (afterwards). Gibbs gives a six-step reflective cycle; Brookfield offers four lenses (your own story, students' eyes, colleagues, theory).

**Student teaching** is the supervised, culminating practice: the intern teaches under a **cooperating teacher** in the school, with a **college supervisor** linking school and college. **Microteaching** scales teaching down (short lesson, small group, recorded) for focused feedback.

**Portfolios** collect evidence of growth and accomplishment; a teaching portfolio usually mixes a working collection with a showcase for an audience.

**Professional growth** continues through colleagues, associations, workshops and reading — and through action research (PE-E2).

## 2. Testable facts

**Experiential learning**
- Kolb's experiential learning model has four stages: **Concrete Experience → Reflective Observation → Abstract Conceptualization → Active Experimentation**. [SRC-0188, "Kolb's experiential learning model" figure]
- Genuine experiential learning requires that the learner be **willing to be actively involved**, able to **reflect**, able to **conceptualize** (analytical skills), and able to **decide and solve problems** using the new ideas. [SRC-0188, "Elements"]
- Experiential learning needs self-initiative, an "**intention to learn**" and an active phase of learning. [SRC-0188, "Implementation"]
- Kolb was influenced by **Dewey, Piaget and Lewin**. [SRC-0189, "Kolb"]

**Reflective practice**
- **Donald Schön**, *The Reflective Practitioner* (**1983**): **reflection-on-action** looks back on an experience and asks what could have been done differently; **reflection-in-action** is reflecting **while** acting. [SRC-0189, lead and "Schön"]
- For Schön, growth begins when practitioners **doubt** their actions and frame situations as problems. [SRC-0189, "Schön"]
- **Gibbs' reflective cycle (1988):** **Description → Feelings → Evaluation → Analysis → Conclusions → Action plan**. [SRC-0189, "Gibbs 1988"]
- **Brookfield (1998)**, four lenses for critical reflection: our **autobiography as learners**, our **learners' eyes**, our **colleagues' experiences**, and **theoretical literature**. [SRC-0189, "Brookfield 1998"]

**Student teaching and microteaching**
- **Student teaching** (teaching practice) is a supervised instructional experience, usually the **culminating course** of a pre-service teacher education program, lasting about a school term. [SRC-0191, lead]
- The student teacher is monitored by the **cooperating teacher** from the host school and by a **college supervisor**, who acts as liaison between the cooperating teacher and the college. [SRC-0191, lead]
- **Microteaching** was invented in **1963** at **Stanford** by **Dwight W. Allen**: a short lesson (originally about **20 minutes**) to a small group is **recorded** and reviewed with peers, a master teacher and students for feedback; class size, time, task and content are **scaled down**. [SRC-0190, lead and "Techniques"]

**Portfolios (Seifert & Sutton Ch. 11)**
- A portfolio is a **purposeful** collection that tells the story of achievement or growth. Dimensions: assessment **for vs of** learning; **progress vs current accomplishments**; **showcase (best work) vs documentation (all work)**; **working vs finished** (a finished portfolio is designed for an audience, such as a job application). [SRC-0109, Ch. 11 "Portfolios"]

**Professional learning (Seifert & Sutton Appendix C)**
- Resources for reflection fall into four categories: **talking and collaborating with colleagues**, **professional associations**, **professional development workshops and conferences**, and **reading professional literature**. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "Types of resources for professional development"]
- Conversations that stimulate reflection should be about **educational matters**; **group study** among teachers is especially useful for beginning teachers learning from veterans. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "Colleagues as a resource"]

**Licensure context**
- To take the LET an applicant must be a Filipino citizen (or from a country with reciprocity), **at least 18**, in good health and of good moral reputation, not convicted of an offense involving moral turpitude, and a **graduate** of a government-recognized school with the required teacher-education degree. [SRC-0016, Sec. 15]

**Second pass (Stage 5)**
- **Dewey**, on reflection in experiential learning: "successive portions of reflective thought grow out of one another and support one another". Reflection can be **facilitated or independent**. [SRC-0188, "Implementation"]
- **Christopher Johns (1995)** designed structured reflection carried out by **sharing with a colleague or mentor**, "looking in" on one's thoughts and emotions and "looking out" at the situation. [SRC-0189, "Johns 1995"]
- In John **Hattie's** review of evidence (Visible Learning), **microteaching** ranked as the **6th most effective** method for improving student outcomes. [SRC-0190, lead]
- **Student teaching** (for those not yet certified) differs from a **practicum**, taken by already-certified teachers seeking an extension to another specialization; both are college-supervised, field-based experiences. [SRC-0191, lead]
- Professional association meetings and conferences draw on **wider expertise** than one school, but take **effort and money**; balanced professional development also needs frequent, low-cost activities such as **reading professional literature**. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "Professional associations"]
- When reading research, teachers should understand each study's **purpose** and consider **contributing** to professional knowledge themselves through **action research**. [SRC-0109, Appendix C "Reading professional literature"]
- Brookfield's **learners'-eyes** lens often reveals that learners read **diverse meanings** into a teacher's words and actions; **colleagues** act as "critical mirrors". [SRC-0189, "Brookfield 1998"]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Kolb's order**: Experience → Reflect → Conceptualize → Experiment (not Reflect first).
- **In-action vs on-action.** Adjusting mid-lesson when learners look lost = reflection-**in**-action. Writing a journal after class = reflection-**on**-action.
- **Cooperating teacher ≠ college supervisor.** The cooperating teacher is in the host school; the supervisor is from the college.
- **Microteaching is scaled-down and recorded**, not "teaching a micro-topic only".
- **Gibbs starts with Description and ends with Action plan.**

## 4. Memory aid

**Kolb "Every Rider Can Experiment"** = Experience, Reflect, Conceptualize, Experiment.
**Gibbs "Don't Forget Every Amazing Class Activity"** = Description, Feelings, Evaluation, Analysis, Conclusions, Action plan.
**Schön: "in = during, on = after."**
