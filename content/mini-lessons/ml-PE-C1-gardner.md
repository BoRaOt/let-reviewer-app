Howard Gardner, a Harvard psychologist and former student of Erik Erikson, proposed that each person has at least eight intelligences. They are linguistic, logical-mathematical, musical, bodily-kinesthetic, spatial, interpersonal, intrapersonal and naturalistic. Interpersonal intelligence means understanding other people. Intrapersonal means understanding oneself. Bodily-kinesthetic covers skilled movement, which matters in cooking and sewing. The theory is popular with educators because it suggests everyone can be smart in some way. But cognitive psychologists heavily criticize it for lacking empirical evidence. For teaching, the useful lesson is to offer varied tasks, such as hands-on work, discussion, visuals and reflection, rather than to label children as one type. On the exam, expect to match a description to the right intelligence. The pair interpersonal and intrapersonal is the classic trap.
Memory aid: "Inter = between people; intra = inside me."
