The family life cycle describes the predictable stages families pass through. Paul Glick sketched it in 1955: people grow up, start families, raise and launch their children, live through an "empty nest", and the cycle begins again in the next generation. His colleague Evelyn Duvall described seven classic stages.
1. Marriage family, a couple without children.
2. Procreation family, with children up to about two and a half.
3. Preschooler family, with children about two and a half to six.
4. School-age family, with children six to thirteen.
5. Teenage family, with children thirteen to twenty.
6. Launching family, as children begin to leave home.
7. Empty nest family, after adult children have left.

Each stage brings its own needs. A family with school-age children faces school costs, and a launching family faces college and first jobs. A family succeeds by adapting to each transition.
