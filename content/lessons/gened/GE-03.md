# GE-03 — Readings in Philippine History

GenEd unit, **15 items** (10%). Outcomes: identify **significant periods** of Philippine history and **relate them to the transformation of society**. [SRC-0001, Annex A, GE] The CHED course of the same name is built on **reading primary sources** and weighing **conflicting accounts**; expect items on which account says what, and why dates are disputed.

## 1. Plain explanation

**Primary vs secondary sources.** A primary source comes from the time or the participants (Pigafetta's journal, the Act of Declaration of Independence, a constitution). A secondary source interprets primary sources. Primary is not automatically more reliable.

**Key periods and turning points.**
- **1521** Battle of Mactan — Lapulapu defeats Magellan; known mainly from **Pigafetta's** account.
- **1872** Cavite Mutiny — used by colonial authorities to execute **Gomburza**.
- **1892** Katipunan founded; **1896** "Cry" — tearing of cédulas; disputed date (Aug 23 vs 26) and place (Pugad Lawin vs Balintawak).
- **June 12, 1898** Declaration of Independence at Kawit; **1899** Malolos Constitution (First Republic).
- **Dec 10, 1898** Treaty of Paris — Spain cedes the Philippines to the US for $20 million.
- **1901** Act No. 74 — English as basis of public instruction. **1934** Tydings–McDuffie Act — ten-year Commonwealth transition; **1935** Constitution; **1937** EO 134 — national language based on Tagalog.
- **1972** Martial law (Proclamation 1081); **1973** Constitution (parliamentary); **1981** martial law formally lifted; **Feb 22–25, 1986** EDSA People Power; **1987** Constitution.

**Inclusive history.** RA 10908 (2016) integrates **Filipino-Muslim and Indigenous Peoples' history** into Philippine history teaching.

## 2. Testable facts

**Sources**
- **Primary sources** are distinguished from **secondary sources**, which interpret, analyze or comment on primary sources; a primary source is **not necessarily more authoritative**. [SRC-0198, lead]

**Spanish period**
- The **Battle of Mactan** was fought on **April 27, 1521** between **Lapulapu** and **Ferdinand Magellan**, who was killed; troop figures come from **Antonio Pigafetta's** account. [SRC-0203, lead and infobox]
- The **Cavite Mutiny** was an uprising of Filipino military personnel at **Fort San Felipe**, Cavite, on **January 20, 1872**, sparked when taxes and the *falla* (fee to be exempt from forced labor) were deducted from laborers' pay. [SRC-0200, lead]
- Colonial authorities and friars used the mutiny to implicate three secular priests, **Mariano Gómez, José Burgos and Jacinto Zamora (Gomburza)**, who were executed. [SRC-0200, lead and "Execution of Gomburza"]
- Modern consensus places the founding of the **Katipunan** on **July 7, 1892**, after **Rizal's arrest**. [SRC-0208, "Formation"]
- The first edition of the **Kartilya ng Katipunan** was written by **Emilio Jacinto**. [SRC-0202, lead]
- The **Cry** (late August **1896**, led by **Andrés Bonifacio**) began the Philippine Revolution; it may refer to the first clash with the Guardia Civil or the **tearing of cédulas personales** (community tax certificates). [SRC-0201, lead]
- Because accounts differ, the exact **date and place are disputed**: from 1908 to 1963 it was held to be **August 26 in Balintawak**; in **1963** the government declared **August 23** (Pugad Lawin, Quezon City). [SRC-0201, lead]

**First Republic**
- **Emilio Aguinaldo** proclaimed the **Declaration of Independence on June 12, 1898** at **Cavite el Viejo (Kawit), Cavite**; it was written by **Ambrosio Rianzares Bautista** and ratified by the **Malolos Congress** on September 29, 1898. [SRC-0199, lead and infobox]
- The **Malolos Constitution** declared that all Filipinos constitute a nation whose state is the **Philippine Republic**, and that government is popular, representative, alternative and responsible with **three distinct powers: legislative, executive, judicial**. [SRC-0008, Arts. 1, 4]

**American period and Commonwealth**
- The **Treaty of Paris**, signed **December 10, 1898**, ended the Spanish–American War; the cession of the Philippines involved **US$20 million** paid to Spain. [SRC-0204, lead and body]
- **Act No. 74 (1901)** made **English** "the basis of all public school instruction". [SRC-0011, Sec. 14]
- The **Tydings–McDuffie Act** (Philippine Independence Act, 1934) set a **ten-year transition** to independence and a framework for a **Commonwealth** constitution to be drafted within two years. [SRC-0205, lead]
- **EO 134 (1937)** proclaimed the national language **based on Tagalog**. [SRC-0012, title and text]

**Martial law to EDSA**
- **Proclamation No. 1081**, placing the country under **martial law**, was announced by **Ferdinand Marcos** on **September 23, 1972**; martial law was formally lifted on **January 17, 1981** (Proclamation No. 2045), but authoritarian rule continued — a **14-year** period in all. [SRC-0206, lead]
- The **1973 Constitution** created a parliamentary-style **National Assembly** with a **Prime Minister**. [SRC-0010, Art. VIII–IX]
- The **People Power Revolution** at EDSA took place **February 22–25, 1986**; the nonviolent revolution led to the **departure of Marcos** (to exile in Hawaii), the end of his 20-year rule, and **Corazon Aquino**'s assumption of the presidency. [SRC-0207, infobox and lead]
- The **1987 Constitution** declares that the Philippines is a democratic and republican State and pursues an independent foreign policy. [SRC-0007, Art. II Secs. 1, 7]

**Inclusive history**
- **RA 10908 (Integrated History Act of 2016)** integrates the **history, culture and identity of Filipino-Muslims and Indigenous Peoples** into the narrative of Philippine history, in consultation with recognized experts. [SRC-0014, Secs. 2–3]

**Government under the 1987 Constitution (second pass, Stage 7)**
- The **Bill of Rights** is **Article III**. [SRC-0007, Art. III]
- **Legislative power** is vested in the **Congress**, consisting of a **Senate** and a **House of Representatives**; **executive power** in the **President**; **judicial power** in **one Supreme Court** and lower courts. [SRC-0007, Art. VI Sec. 1; Art. VII Sec. 1; Art. VIII Sec. 1]
- **Senators:** six-year term, **no more than two consecutive terms**. **House members:** three-year term, **no more than three consecutive terms**. [SRC-0007, Art. VI Secs. 4, 7]
- **President:** elected for **six years** and **not eligible for any re-election**. [SRC-0007, Art. VII Sec. 4]
- The territorial and political subdivisions are **provinces, cities, municipalities and barangays**, with autonomous regions in **Muslim Mindanao and the Cordilleras**; elective local officials (except barangay officials) serve **three-year terms, maximum three consecutive**. [SRC-0007, Art. X Secs. 1, 8]

## 3. Common mistakes

- **Cry of Pugad Lawin date is contested**: official since 1963 = August 23; older accounts (e.g. Guardia Civil-era writers) say August 26 at Balintawak.
- **Independence was declared in Kawit, not Malolos**; Malolos was the Congress and Constitution.
- **Treaty of Paris is between Spain and the US**; no Filipino signed it.
- **Cavite Mutiny = 1872**, not 1896.
- **Martial law "lifted" in 1981** did not end Marcos's rule; EDSA did in 1986.
- **A region is not a local government unit** under Art. X Sec. 1 (except the autonomous regions); the LGUs are provinces, cities, municipalities and barangays.
- **Primary ≠ always true**: Pigafetta was an eyewitness on Magellan's side.
