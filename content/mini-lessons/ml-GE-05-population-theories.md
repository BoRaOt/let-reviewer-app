Four theories describe population and resources.

**Malthusian theory:** **Thomas Malthus** (1766–1834), an English clergyman, warned in 1798 that population would outgrow the earth's **carrying capacity**, the number of people an area's resources can support. Three factors would then check population: **war, famine and disease**. Population kept growing past his predictions, mainly because of technology and food production.

**Zero population growth** argues for keeping births and deaths balanced to protect the environment.

**Cornucopian theory** is optimistic: human ingenuity will solve resource problems as they arise.

**Demographic transition theory** describes societies moving through stages. In **Stage 1**, birth, death and infant mortality rates are all high and life expectancy is short. Later stages bring falling death rates, then falling birth rates, and finally low, stable growth.

The Philippines' population and urban growth link these ideas to local issues like housing, food and jobs.
