subtopic: Kohlberg's levels of moral reasoning

Lawrence Kohlberg extended Piaget's work on moral reasoning. He gave people dilemmas such as the Heinz dilemma, where a husband steals an overpriced drug to save his dying wife. He scored the reasoning, not the yes or no answer. He found three levels.
- **Pre-conventional:** common before about age nine or ten. Choices rest on consequences to oneself: avoiding punishment, obeying, or getting a reward.
- **Conventional:** from early adolescence. People judge by what others will think, or by rules and laws that keep order.
- **Post-conventional:** reached only after formal operational thinking, and by only a few people. Reasoning rests on internalized principles such as justice and compassion, even when these conflict with the law.

If a student says "stealing is wrong because you will get caught," that is pre-conventional. "Because people will think you are a thief" is conventional.
