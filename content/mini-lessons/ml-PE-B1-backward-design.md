**Understanding by Design (UbD)**, by Grant Wiggins and Jay McTighe (1998), is a **backward design** approach. Its authors noticed that teachers often begin planning with a favorite activity or the next textbook chapter. UbD reverses that.

**Stage 1: Identify desired results.** What should students understand and be able to do? Write "Students will understand that…" statements and essential questions, drawing on content standards.

**Stage 2: Determine acceptable evidence.** Decide how students will show that understanding, usually through performance tasks and other assessments.

**Stage 3: Plan learning experiences.** Only now choose the activities and materials that will lead students to the results and prepare them for the assessment.

The key difference from many models is that **assessment is planned before activities**. In an HE unit on meal planning, the teacher first defines the understanding, then designs a family-menu task, then plans lessons.
Memory aid: "Results, Evidence, Activities — begin with the end."
