Diana Baumrind described three parenting styles that differ in warmth and in structure.
- **Authoritarian:** high structure but low warmth. Rules come first, children are expected to obey, and parents direct rather than negotiate.
- **Authoritative:** moderate to high structure together with high warmth and emotional support. Parents explain, listen and talk problems through.
- **Permissive:** high warmth but low structure. Parents focus on the child's happiness, avoid discipline, and rarely refuse requests.

Maccoby and Martin (1983) later added a fourth style, **uninvolved**, which is low in both warmth and structure. These parents give little attention, discipline or support.

On the exam, first ask whether the parent is warm and whether there are clear rules, then match. "Because I said so" with little warmth points to authoritarian. Firm rules explained kindly point to authoritative.
Memory aid: "Authoritative = rules with a hug; authoritarian = rules without one."
