No single strategy is best. Seifert and Sutton group strategies along two lines: how much the **teacher directs** the learning, and whether the focus is on **groups or individuals**. Teacher-directed methods include lectures, advance organizers, mastery learning, direct instruction and Hunter's model. Student-centered methods include independent study, inquiry, cooperative learning and projects.

Choose by asking three questions. What kind of **thinking** do I want? How much must the **content be organized** for learners before they can understand it? How much **responsibility** can these learners take for directing their own learning? New, tightly sequenced skills often suit direct instruction; open questions suit inquiry or projects.

Sequence lessons the way the K to 12 law expects: **spiral progression**, from the **known to the unknown**, using constructivist, inquiry-based, reflective, collaborative and integrative approaches.
Memory aid: "Thinking, Organizing, Responsibility — then spiral from known to new."
