HACCP stands for Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points. It is a systematic way to prevent food from becoming unsafe during production, instead of only testing the finished dish. A food business first analyses where biological, chemical or physical hazards could enter, such as raw chicken juices, cleaning chemicals or broken glass. It then sets control points where those hazards can be stopped, for example by cooking to a safe temperature or by chilling quickly. By 1997, seven HACCP principles had become the standard. Temperature control matters because food-borne bacteria grow quickly in the danger zone, roughly 4 to 63 degrees Celsius, although agencies set slightly different limits. Keep hot food hot, keep cold food cold, and do not leave cooked food standing at room temperature.
