Cooking methods are often tested by their defining detail. To sauté, cook food quickly in a small amount of fat in a shallow pan over fairly high heat, tossing it. To blanch, scald a vegetable briefly in boiling water, then plunge it into iced water to stop the cooking. To poach, keep food submerged in liquid at a gentle heat, about 70 to 80 degrees Celsius, cooler than a simmer or a boil. To braise, first brown the food at high heat, then simmer it covered in a little liquid, so braising combines dry and wet heat. To broil, grill with the heat source above the food. To deep-fry, submerge the food completely in hot oil, unlike pan-frying. To steam, cook the food in steam without letting it touch the water.
Memory aid: "Brown then Bathe" is braising; "Bath then Ice" is blanching.
