Two models give reflection a structure.

**Gibbs' reflective cycle (1988)** has six stages. **Description:** what happened? **Feelings:** what were you thinking and feeling? **Evaluation:** what was good and bad? **Analysis:** what sense can you make of it? **Conclusion:** what else could you have done? **Action plan:** what will you do next time? It turns a vague "the lesson went badly" into a plan.

**Stephen Brookfield (1998)** says critically reflective teachers check their assumptions through **four lenses**. First, their own **autobiography as learners**. Second, their **learners' eyes**, which often reveal surprising interpretations of what the teacher meant. Third, **colleagues' experiences**, which act as critical mirrors. Fourth, **theoretical literature**, which helps name what seemed like personal problems.

An internship reflection paper that uses student feedback and a theory reading is using two of Brookfield's lenses.
