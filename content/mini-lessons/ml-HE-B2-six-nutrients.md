subtopic: Six classes of nutrients and energy values

The body needs six classes of nutrients: carbohydrates, lipids or fats, proteins, water, vitamins and minerals. Carbohydrates, lipids and proteins are macronutrients. They are needed in large amounts, and the body can turn them into energy. Vitamins and minerals are micronutrients, needed in smaller amounts but still essential. There are thirteen vitamins and sixteen essential minerals. The energy values are worth memorizing: one gram of carbohydrate gives four kilocalories, one gram of protein also gives four, and one gram of fat gives nine, more than double the other two. Fat also builds cell membranes, cushions organs and insulates the body. Protein's main job is not energy. It builds bones, muscles and skin, and carries out most of the body's chemical reactions. Water, though it gives no energy, is a nutrient too.
Memory aid: "4-4-9" — carbohydrate, protein, fat in kilocalories per gram.
