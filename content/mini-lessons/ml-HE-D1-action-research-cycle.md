**Action research**, a term coined by **Kurt Lewin** in 1944, is research by teachers on their own students or their own teaching, aimed at concrete improvements. Lewin described it as a spiral of steps, each a circle of planning, action and fact-finding.

Seifert and Sutton, following Mertler, describe four stages.
1. **Planning:** identify and define the problem as a researchable question, review related literature, and plan what data to collect, how and when.
2. **Acting:** collect and analyze data. Keep it simple and manageable.
3. **Developing an action plan:** make changes in teaching and document them.
4. **Communicating and reflecting:** share results with colleagues, then ask "What did I learn? What should I do next?" This starts a new cycle.

Example: an HE teacher notices poor handwashing in the kitchen lab, tries a visual checklist, observes compliance for three weeks, and shares results at a faculty meeting.
Memory aid: "Plan, Act, Change, Share and Reflect — then spiral again."
